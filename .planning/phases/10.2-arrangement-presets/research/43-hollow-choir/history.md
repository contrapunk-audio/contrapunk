# HISTORY / PRIMARY-SOURCE RESEARCH — Preset 43 “Hollow Choir”

## Summary

“Hollow Choir” can honestly reference a **bounded 2014–2018 *Hollow Knight* sound-world**, not Christopher Larkin’s whole career: ruined-sacred ambience, melancholy melody, sparse piano/strings or organ, distant wordless voice, and a game-scale progression from intimate exploration to orchestral or operatic crisis. The strongest vocal primary evidence is **Amelia Jones’s solo soprano on “City of Tears,” not a live choir**; Larkin says the first score was otherwise largely sample-based. The preset should therefore promise an original, choir-like dark-fantasy arrangement approximation—not a recreation of Larkin, *Hollow Knight*, Hallownest, any cue, or any copyrighted melody.

## 1. Scope and corpus

### Referenced period

This report covers the original game’s development and release cycle, approximately **2014–2017**, plus the four content-pack expansion cycle collected on *Gods & Nightmares* in **2018**. Team Cherry’s December 2014 Kickstarter update already publicised Larkin’s involvement, while the original soundtrack was released on Bandcamp on 10 February 2017; Team Cherry later described the 2018 album as music added through the content packs. [Kickstarter update](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/11662585/hollow-knight/posts/1083628) [Official soundtrack](https://christopherlarkin.bandcamp.com/album/hollow-knight-original-soundtrack) [Team Cherry, 2018](https://www.teamcherry.com.au/blog/godmaster-release-date-announced)

Larkin reports receiving very early **Stencyl** builds, continuing after the project moved to **Unity**, playing builds, capturing footage, and composing against that material in Cubase. This makes the score a product of iterative game development rather than a concert work later attached to finished pictures. [Larkin Q&A](https://indiegamefans.com/soundtrack-spotlight-hollow-knight/)

### Bounded cue corpus

The preset name suggests a narrower “ruined, sacred, vocal” subset. The defensible corpus is:

- **“City of Tears”** — central exploration/area cue; documented pitter-patter piano ostinato and live solo soprano by Amelia Jones. [Larkin Q&A](https://indiegamefans.com/soundtrack-spotlight-hollow-knight/) [Official Steam credits](https://store.steampowered.com/app/598190/Hollow_Knight__Official_Soundtrack/)
- **“Soul Sanctum” / Soul Master material** — area-to-boss function; Larkin identifies a Gothic, cold organ and says the organ evokes a sacred or old-scholarly space. [Game Developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/crafting-an-evocative-score-for-i-hollow-knight-i-) [Polygon interview](https://www.polygon.com/2024/8/8/24215602/christopher-larkin-hollow-knight-interview/)
- **“White Palace” and “The White Lady”** — regal, elegiac late-game/place-and-character functions. Larkin’s own interview describes “The White Lady” as a regal harp elegy; the soundtrack’s official sequence places “White Palace” near the endgame escalation. [Bandcamp Daily interview](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/christopher-larkin-review) [Official track list](https://store.steampowered.com/app/598190/Hollow_Knight__Official_Soundtrack/)
- **“Sealed Vessel” and “Radiance”** — climactic narrative/boss functions in which the restrained world expands toward orchestral/operatic scale. The official album description explicitly frames the game-long arc as soft piano growing to orchestral scale as the kingdom’s fate unfolds. [Official soundtrack](https://christopherlarkin.bandcamp.com/album/hollow-knight-original-soundtrack)
- **“Dirtmouth,” “Crossroads,” and the main “Hollow Knight” theme** — control corpus for the persistent intimate/melancholy side: soft piano, viola, distant strings/harp and recurring thematic material. [Game Developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/crafting-an-evocative-score-for-i-hollow-knight-i-) [Native Instruments interview](https://blog.native-instruments.com/christopher-larkin-on-scoring-for-game-film-and-tv/)

“Greenpath,” “Crystal Peak,” “Mantis Lords,” comic character cues, *Silksong*, film/TV work, and Larkin’s career at large are comparisons only. They prevent overgeneralisation; they are not the preset’s target.

## 2. Historical and aesthetic context

### The brief and dramatic problem

Team Cherry’s brief was “dark elegance,” melancholy, classical character, and minimal instrumentation. The dramatic problem was to make a ruined subterranean kingdom feel sad, mysterious, and worth exploring without covering every moment in blockbuster orchestration. Larkin describes the intended world as a once-prosperous kingdom fallen to ruin, requiring melancholy, intrigue, and mystery. [Game Developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/crafting-an-evocative-score-for-i-hollow-knight-i-) [Native Instruments interview](https://blog.native-instruments.com/christopher-larkin-on-scoring-for-game-film-and-tv/)

Instrumentation was chosen to bind location to sound: harp and earthy percussion for Greenpath; cold Gothic organ for Soul Sanctum; kalimba, wine glasses, and guitar harmonics for Crystal Peak; the rain-like piano ostinato for City of Tears. The historical takeaway is **not** “Larkin always uses choir” but “this project assigns sparse, legible timbres to spaces and narrative functions.” [Game Developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/crafting-an-evocative-score-for-i-hollow-knight-i-) [Larkin Q&A](https://indiegamefans.com/soundtrack-spotlight-hollow-knight/)

### Influences and tradition

Larkin names Hisaishi, Uematsu, Debussy—especially brief minimal, melody-led piano writing—and metal as background influences. Elsewhere he identifies *The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time*, *Final Fantasy VII*, and *Crystal Chronicles* as formative game-score references. “Mantis Lords” deliberately plays on Vivaldian Baroque string virtuosity, showing that historical vocabulary is selected to characterise a place or people rather than applied uniformly. [Larkin Q&A](https://indiegamefans.com/soundtrack-spotlight-hollow-knight/) [Polygon interview](https://www.polygon.com/2024/8/8/24215602/christopher-larkin-hollow-knight-interview/)

Shared vocabulary includes leitmotif, sparse piano melancholy, Gothic organ, reverberant sacred signifiers, orchestral boss escalation, and location-specific colour. More project-characteristic is the combination of those devices with tiny insect-scale characters, an abandoned underground kingdom, unusually intimate sound design, and a restrained-to-epic game-wide arc. “Choir,” “Gothic,” or “minor mode” alone is therefore generic dark-fantasy vocabulary, not a sufficient attribution.

## 3. Production context, collaborators, and attribution

- **Christopher Larkin** composed the score and designed nearly all of the game’s audio. Team Cherry initially called the audio department “an audio department of one,” while also stressing that his Adelaide location allowed close collaboration. [Team Cherry announcement](https://www.teamcherry.com.au/blog/composers-sound-designers-hollow-knights-getting-aural)
- **Ari Gibson and William Pellen / Team Cherry** supplied art, mechanics, builds, animation, direction, review, and implementation context. Larkin specifically credits William’s implementation and later says Pellen configured the in-game reverb zones. [Larkin Q&A](https://indiegamefans.com/soundtrack-spotlight-hollow-knight/) [Native Instruments interview](https://blog.native-instruments.com/christopher-larkin-on-scoring-for-game-film-and-tv/)
- **Timothy Cheel** performed viola on documented parts of the original score; **Amelia Jones** sang soprano on “City of Tears.” These are the original album’s named music performers beyond Larkin. [Official Steam credits](https://store.steampowered.com/app/598190/Hollow_Knight__Official_Soundtrack/)
- In a retrospective comparison, Larkin says the first game was “mostly in the box”: Cheel and Jones were recorded, and otherwise the score used samples. This supports virtual-orchestral/virtual-vocal production, but it does **not** identify any particular commercial choir library or authorise calling Jones a choir. [Bandcamp Daily, 2025](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/christopher-larkin-hollow-knight-silksong-interview)
- Larkin composed additional content-pack music without charging Team Cherry; *Gods & Nightmares* collected it in 2018 and added the live-recorded “Pale Court” arrangement. That later recording must not be misrepresented as evidence that the 2017 score used a live orchestra or choir. [Team Cherry, 2018](https://www.teamcherry.com.au/blog/godmaster-release-date-announced)

### Choir evidence: what can and cannot be said

**High-confidence evidence:** “City of Tears” features **one credited soprano, Amelia Jones**. Larkin also says his production template includes choirs among orchestral libraries, and that the first game apart from Cheel and Jones was sample-based. [Official Steam credits](https://store.steampowered.com/app/598190/Hollow_Knight__Official_Soundtrack/) [Native Instruments interview](https://blog.native-instruments.com/christopher-larkin-on-scoring-for-game-film-and-tv/) [Bandcamp Daily, 2025](https://daily.bandcamp.com/features/christopher-larkin-hollow-knight-silksong-interview)

**Bounded inference:** some climactic textures can reasonably be heard as choral or operatic, and the official album text itself says the score swells to an “operatic scale.” However, the reviewed official credits do not document a live ensemble choir on the 2017 album. “Hollow Choir” should therefore mean **a hollow, distant, wordless choir-like arrangement texture**, with “City of Tears” supplying evidence for exposed solo vocal colour—not a factual claim that a choir performed the cue corpus.

## 4. Evolution through phrase, section, game, and release cycle

### Within a phrase

The primary sources do not publish measures or complete lawful scores, so exact harmonic/voice-leading claims belong in the theory report. Historically supported phrase behaviour is broader:

1. **Expose a simple melodic identity** in piano, viola, organ, or voice.
2. **Sustain space around it** with long tails, ambient beds, or held strings rather than constant contrapuntal filling.
3. **Add colour and weight selectively** as the phrase or encounter intensifies.
4. **Leave room or thin back** so the space and sound effects remain audible.

This follows Larkin’s stated reliance on one or two “super simple themes,” minimal melody-led passages, sparse orchestration, and game mixing in which music and sound design share the environment. [Larkin Q&A](https://indiegamefans.com/soundtrack-spotlight-hollow-knight/) [Native Instruments interview](https://blog.native-instruments.com/christopher-larkin-on-scoring-for-game-film-and-tv/)

### Across a section or area

Larkin explicitly describes paired stems: a full area layer and a pared-back ambient layer. Side or peripheral rooms can fade the full layer away; entering more central or active space can restore it, producing musical progression as the player traverses the zone. This is adaptive **vertical re-orchestration/mixing**, not proof that every note is procedurally generated. [Larkin Q&A](https://indiegamefans.com/soundtrack-spotlight-hollow-knight/)

“City of Tears” itself is documented as an area cue with a rain-evoking ostinato and soprano colour. “Soul Sanctum” maps organ to the sacred/scholarly area, while the same soundtrack suite also serves Soul Master material according to the documented game-use catalogue. That is a progression from place-signifier to encounter intensity, but the detailed suite segmentation comes from community documentation and is medium-confidence. [Larkin Q&A](https://indiegamefans.com/soundtrack-spotlight-hollow-knight/) [Hollow Knight Wiki soundtrack catalogue](https://hollowknight.wiki/w/Soundtrack_(Hollow_Knight))

### Across the game

The official album’s narrative is explicit: soft piano introduces the Hollow Knight theme, then orchestral forces grow as exploration deepens and the kingdom’s fate unfolds. Thus the selected identity is a **trajectory**, not a static preset snapshot:

`solitary/ruined → location-coloured mystery → fuller danger or revelation → late operatic/orchestral crisis`

Recurring simple themes glue places and characters together. Larkin identifies the opening piano theme as returning in several places, and describes the Shade’s tune and processing as an audible identity marker. [Official soundtrack](https://christopherlarkin.bandcamp.com/album/hollow-knight-original-soundtrack) [Native Instruments interview](https://blog.native-instruments.com/christopher-larkin-on-scoring-for-game-film-and-tv/)

### Adaptive versus fixed cue behaviour

- **Adaptive:** paired full/ambient stems fade with central versus peripheral space; reverb changes by game location; arrangements/mixes can respond to player movement and context. [Larkin Q&A](https://indiegamefans.com/soundtrack-spotlight-hollow-knight/) [Native Instruments interview](https://blog.native-instruments.com/christopher-larkin-on-scoring-for-game-film-and-tv/)
- **Fixed/composed:** melodic material, instrumental identities, and boss pieces are authored; the released album presents fixed listening edits. The community cue catalogue identifies many “single track” cues alongside suites and action variants, but it is not an official implementation specification. [Official track list](https://store.steampowered.com/app/598190/Hollow_Knight__Official_Soundtrack/) [Hollow Knight Wiki soundtrack catalogue](https://hollowknight.wiki/w/Soundtrack_(Hollow_Knight))
- **Not supported:** claims of note-by-note generative composition, AI music, real-time reharmonisation, or a universal combat-intensity system across all cues.

### Across the project period

The early score begins from minimal piano/viola and location sketches; the shipped game broadens that palette to woodwinds, strings, brass, organ, voice, and occasional full orchestration. The 2017–2018 content packs extend the project with new boss-specific music, while “Pale Court” is a later live arrangement. The persistent project concern is melancholy and thematic/world coherence; the expansion-specific tendency is a greater concentration of boss and god-scale spectacle. [Game Developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/crafting-an-evocative-score-for-i-hollow-knight-i-) [Team Cherry, 2018](https://www.teamcherry.com.au/blog/godmaster-release-date-announced)

## 5. Persistent traits versus project-specific traits

### Persistent or recurring concerns in Larkin’s documented practice

- Imagining music as a world and assigning sounds to visual/mechanical spaces.
- Starting from simple themes and reusing them where narrative links justify it.
- Balancing literal sound with heightened or fantastical sound design.
- Combining sampled orchestral production with selected live performers when resources permit.
- Treating interactivity, mixing, and communication with developers as part of composition.

These recur across Larkin’s interviews, but they are working methods—not a licence to market a preset as his personal style. [Larkin Q&A](https://indiegamefans.com/soundtrack-spotlight-hollow-knight/) [Native Instruments interview](https://blog.native-instruments.com/christopher-larkin-on-scoring-for-game-film-and-tv/)

### Specific to the bounded *Hollow Knight* corpus

- A fallen underground kingdom and the “dark elegance/melancholy” brief.
- Cave-scale reverb zones and intimate subterranean ambience.
- Rain-like City of Tears piano plus Jones’s soprano.
- Soul Sanctum’s cold Gothic organ/sacred-scholar association.
- The exact Hollow Knight, Shade, character, and location themes.
- Insect-world narrative associations and the original cue-to-area/boss mappings.

### Traits deliberately excluded

Mantis Lords’ Vivaldian virtuosity, Crystal Peak’s glass/kalimba/guitar-harmonic palette, Dung Defender’s comic heroism, Grimm’s theatrical nightmare vocabulary, and later *Silksong* chant/strings are distinct local solutions. Folding them all into “Hollow Choir” would turn purposeful differentiation into a generic Larkin collage.

## 6. Relationship to peers and tradition

Melancholy piano, wordless soprano, organ, modal sacred affect, reverberation, leitmotif, and orchestral escalation all predate *Hollow Knight* and are widely shared across classical, film, anime, and game music. Larkin openly places his work in conversation with Hisaishi, Uematsu, Debussy, Nintendo-era adventure scores, and—on one cue—Vivaldi. The preset should credit the **shared dark-fantasy/game-score tradition first**, then identify the bounded *Hollow Knight* production research as its reference case. [Polygon interview](https://www.polygon.com/2024/8/8/24215602/christopher-larkin-hollow-knight-interview/) [Larkin Q&A](https://indiegamefans.com/soundtrack-spotlight-hollow-knight/)

What is unusually characteristic is not one scale or voicing but the coordination of: restrained melody, room-like emptiness, strongly differentiated area timbres, game-state layering, and long-form escalation from fragile exploration to tragic spectacle.

## 7. Common caricatures and attribution errors to avoid

1. **“The Hollow Knight sound is choir.”** False as a corpus claim. The only officially credited vocalist in the original album evidence reviewed here is solo soprano Amelia Jones on one cue.
2. **“Add huge reverb to minor chords.”** Reverb matters, but Larkin also uses silence, instrumental specificity, thematic recurrence, and sparse/full stem transitions. Reverb alone is an effect preset, not the aesthetic.
3. **“Always epic and Gothic.”** The initial brief stressed minimal instrumentation; full orchestration is reserved for selected dramatic peaks.
4. **“Every cue is adaptive.”** Some area music uses paired layers, while many boss/location cues are authored fixed pieces or suites. Adaptation often occurs in arrangement and mix.
5. **“One sad scale explains the score.”** Sources support melody, timbre, leitmotif, orchestration, and context, not a career- or score-wide single-scale rule.
6. **“Larkin alone made every heard decision.”** He composed/designed audio, but Team Cherry directed against art and mechanics, reviewed the work, implemented it, and established environmental reverb behaviour.
7. **“The original score was live orchestra and choir.”** Larkin says it was mostly in-the-box except recorded viola and voice; “Pale Court” is a later live arrangement.
8. **“Dark Souls in 2D.”** This journalistic shorthand obscures the score’s intimate, curious, and sometimes whimsical side and is not Larkin’s stated compositional thesis.

## 8. Copyright-safe boundaries and disallowed product claims

### Safe boundary

Implement only abstract, reusable behaviours: sparse-to-full density motion; independent, slow wordless-voice-like lines; restrained piano/organ/string roles; broad register and long decay; simple original motives; strategic silence; and context- or performer-triggered thinning/intensification. Use wholly new intervals, rhythms, contours, voicings, and sound patches.

Do **not** transcribe, encode, quote, or closely paraphrase the melodies, bass lines, ostinatos, chord sequence, cue structure, stems, recordings, samples, or sound-design assets of “City of Tears,” “Soul Sanctum,” “White Palace,” “Sealed Vessel,” “Radiance,” the main theme, or any other cue. Do not use game names, character/location names, logos, art, performer recordings, or ripped game assets in shipped content.

### Disallowed claims

Product/UI/marketing copy must not say or imply:

- “Christopher Larkin preset,” “official,” “licensed,” “approved,” or “endorsed”;
- “Hollow Knight preset/soundtrack/choir,” “Hallownest,” “City of Tears,” or any cue/character/location recreation;
- “sounds exactly like,” “recreates,” “clones,” “authentic Larkin harmony,” or guaranteed artist imitation;
- “live choir,” “Amelia Jones vocal,” or “live orchestra” unless the product actually contains separately licensed performances and can substantiate the claim;
- that the reference work uses generative MIDI harmony, AI, or the same engine behaviour as Contrapunk;
- that one mode, chord trick, or vocal patch is Larkin’s whole style.

Safe copy would be: **“An original, dark-fantasy arrangement preset: distant wordless voices move from sparse, ruined-space intimacy toward a restrained choral peak.”** A research note may say it was informed by a bounded study of late-development *Hollow Knight* area and climax scoring, provided it also states that it is unofficial, unendorsed, and does not reproduce the music.

## 9. Concrete, testable implications for synthesis

These are historical/aesthetic requirements for the parent synthesis, not exact theory settings:

1. **Sparse opening:** with a held or slow source gesture, the generated texture begins with no more than one or two clearly separable supporting roles; it must not open at maximum choir density.
2. **Original melodic identity:** generated parts may derive a short new motive from user input, but tests must confirm that no stored *Hollow Knight* melody or cue MIDI exists.
3. **Solo-before-ensemble:** a voice-like upper line or single hollow timbre should be perceptible before any wider choral stack, reflecting the documented solo-soprano evidence rather than inventing a live choir history.
4. **Sectional density arc:** an explicit trigger (time, phrase count, player intensity, or lane state) must support `pared-back → full → pared-back/silence`; random permanent density is insufficient.
5. **Room for ambience:** note occupancy and release tests should preserve audible gaps or sustained space; continuous fast note generation fails the aesthetic.
6. **Reserved climax:** the densest register/doubling appears only after a defined escalation trigger and can return to restraint. “Epic at all times” fails.
7. **Timbre-role differentiation:** if product surfaces expose sound roles, piano/organ/strings/wordless-voice-like layers should remain distinguishable rather than becoming one undifferentiated pad.
8. **Adaptive-claim honesty:** if the engine only reacts to MIDI input or transport, copy must say that. It must not claim the original game’s room-aware Unity stem system.
9. **Attribution check:** automated or manual copy review must reject the disallowed artist, game, cue, endorsement, live-choir, and exact-imitation claims above.
10. **Cross-input robustness:** slow single notes and small original motifs should produce the sparse-to-full arc; arbitrary dense chords should be limited or thinned rather than causing a constant note cloud.

## 11. Confidence and gaps

### High confidence

- The bounded brief: dark elegance, melancholy, mystery, and initially minimal instrumentation.
- Larkin’s composer/sound-designer role and Team Cherry’s close collaborative/implementation role.
- Cheel’s viola and Jones’s **solo soprano** credit; no documented live choir in the reviewed original-album credits.
- The mostly sample-based nature of the original score apart from those recorded performers.
- Area-specific timbral choices, paired full/ambient stems, environmental reverb, simple recurring themes, and the sparse-to-orchestral game-scale arc.

### Medium confidence

- Exact in-game assignment of suite segments and action variants, because the most complete public mapping is community-maintained.
- Hearing selected climactic samples as “choir-like”; the production method supports sampled vocal possibility, but reviewed sources do not name the patch, library, or individual cue stems.
- The exact start date of every cue’s composition within the 2014–2017 period.

### Low confidence / unresolved gaps

- Exact harmonies, modes, voice-leading, measures, stem counts per cue, loop points, and transition conditions; no official score or implementation session was located.
- Whether any uncredited group vocal recording exists. Current official credits and Larkin’s retrospective make that unlikely, but absence in public credits is not absolute proof.
- Precise Unity/PlayMaker transition logic. A defunct interview is reposted elsewhere, but without a verifiable primary page it should not support product claims.
- Exact performer/session details for every viola or vocal passage.

### Recommended next evidence

If implementation decisions require note-level or transition-level specificity, obtain a licensed official score, composer-provided stems/session notes, or a direct clarification from Larkin/Team Cherry. Until then, keep the preset abstract, original, explicitly unofficial, and limited to the testable sparse-to-full, space-conscious arrangement behaviours above.

## Decision

**Historically viable only as a bounded, unofficial arrangement approximation.** The research supports “hollow/distant wordless vocal texture within a sparse-to-climactic dark-fantasy arc.” It does **not** support a live-choir claim, exact imitation, artist-wide style claim, or reproduction of any *Hollow Knight* cue.
