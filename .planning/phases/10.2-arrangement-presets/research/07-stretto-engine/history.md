# Role A History / Primary-Source Report — Preset 07 “Stretto Engine”

**Phase:** 10.2 composer-informed arrangement presets  
**Role:** A — artist/style historian and primary-source researcher  
**Reference:** selected fugues by Johann Sebastian Bach, not “Bach style” generally  
**Retrieval date for all web sources:** 2026-07-19  
**Method boundary:** abstract descriptions only; no themes are reproduced.

## Executive judgment

The catalog sentence **“tonic, fifth, and octave answers enter progressively closer”** can honestly reference a *bounded modern arrangement assembled from historical fugal vocabulary*: entries of one recognizable subject at tonic- and dominant-related pitch levels, octave/register redistribution, and successively shorter entry delays. It must **not** be presented as a rule of Bach’s fugues, as a literal reconstruction of any selected fugue, or as evidence that Bach routinely made stretto progressively denser toward the end. A corpus study of all forty-eight *Well-Tempered Clavier* fugues found no strong support for conclusion, quantity, or progressively smaller-onset hypotheses and directly contradicted the density hypothesis; a detailed analysis of *Art of Fugue*, Contrapunctus 5 likewise describes its global stretto distances as non-progressive. [McDonald 2020](https://uwo.scholaris.ca/items/90e7593a-4d35-4969-a861-833fd36ed166); [Alevizos 2019](https://revuemusicaleoicrm.org/rmo-vol6-n1/parameters-of-stretto/). **Confidence: high.**

The defensible reference corpus is deliberately narrow:

1. **WTC I, Fugue in C major, BWV 846/2** — an unusually concentrated early Leipzig/Cöthen-derived stretto fugue preserved in Bach’s 1722 fair copy, useful for close entries at varied temporal and pitch intervals.
2. **WTC I as a controlled corpus (BWV 846–869)** — not to claim uniform practice, but to locate BWV 846/2 within a pedagogical collection whose autograph was revised over time.
3. ***The Art of Fugue*, Contrapunctus 5, BWV 1080/5** — late, explicitly systematic exploration of stretto with rectus/inversus, dux/comes, multiple entry distances, and carefully managed contrapuntal compatibility.
4. **The forty-eight WTC fugues only as corpus-level negative evidence** — to test, and reject as a generalization, the simple “ever closer = climax” story.

This selection spans a 1722 collection and the late 1740s project only where source records support dating. It does not imply a straight career ladder from “simple” to “complex.”

## 1. Scope and corpus

### 1.1 BWV 846/2: concentrated stretto in WTC I

Bach’s autograph collective manuscript **D-B Mus.ms. Bach P 415** contains WTC I; Bach Digital identifies J. S. Bach as scribe, dates the source exactly to **1722** from title page and handwriting, notes later revision evidence, and lists BWV 846/2 in the source. The institutional item record places BWV 846 on fols. 2v–4r and the fugue heading on f. 3v. [Bach Digital, source 00001361](https://www.bachdigital.de/receive/BachDigitalSource_source_00001361); [Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek, BWV 846 item](https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/4XMMG7JXF6O2WULOOGWJFRRTABJFSQGT). **Confidence: high.**

Score-based summaries agree on the salient procedure: following the exposition, BWV 846/2 presents numerous overlapping entries, at varied time and pitch intervals, with very little non-subject material. Kerman calls it a stretto fugue fitting roughly two dozen entries of a one-and-a-half-bar subject into 27 bars and stresses that the stretti occur at many time and pitch intervals. [Kerman 2005, ch. 1, DOI landing page](https://doi.org/10.1515/9780520962590-004). Teoria’s score analysis locates incomplete/contested subject-like entries at mm. 14, 15, and 20 and warns that the familiar “24 subjects” count depends on what fragments are admitted; it counts 22 complete statements and treats further fragments as analytical choices. [Rodríguez Alvira 2005](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/BWV846/). **Confidence: high for dense stretto and varied intervals; medium for any exact total because entry boundaries are disputed.**

Responsible entry references usable by the synthesis:

- **mm. 1–7:** four-voice exposition, with tonic-level subject and dominant-level answer functions distributed through the voices.
- **from m. 7:** overlapping entries begin; one published score-analysis inventory identifies the m. 7 treble/tenor pair as a tonic-level entry followed by a dominant-level answer in stretto.
- **mm. 14–15 and m. 20:** fragmentary entries demonstrate that “entry” counts are analysis-dependent; do not encode “24” as historical fact.
- **m. 23 and final two measures:** Teoria reports these as the only measures without the subject, reinforcing the work’s exceptional saturation.

The measure references are analytical navigation aids, not a claim that all editions phrase or label fragments identically.

### 1.2 WTC chronology and transmission

WTC I’s title page says the collection was made for the use of inquisitive musical youth and the recreation of those already skilled; this supports an exemplary/pedagogical context, not a public concert-cycle origin. [DDB, P 415](https://www.deutsche-digitale-bibliothek.de/item/RRRM6XIGAGPM7T77HOEB5MEGYWAHLDIP). The 1722 fair copy is an **autograph**, but “1722” is not a safe date for first composition of every constituent movement: the Netherlands Bach Society notes that Part I includes material written during approximately the preceding five years. [Netherlands Bach Society, BWV 848 context](https://www.bachvereniging.nl/en/bwv/bwv-848). P 415 also bears evidence of later attention; therefore “autograph fair copy” should not be flattened into “unchanged first version.” **Confidence: high.**

WTC was circulated in manuscript during Bach’s life; complete printed editions appeared only around **1801**, independently through firms including Simrock, Nägeli, and Hoffmeister/Kühnel. Those prints are reception evidence, not witnesses equal in authority to P 415 for Bach’s 1722 text. [Yo Tomita, Simrock chronology](https://www.qub.ac.uk/tomita/essay/simrock/); [Henle, WTC I publication history](https://blog.henle.de/en/2022/10/31/if-thats-not-reason-enough-to-celebrate-300-years-of-wtc-i/). **Confidence: high.**

### 1.3 Late corpus: *The Art of Fugue*, especially Contrapunctus 5

Bach Digital distinguishes two source states rather than one fixed “final masterpiece”:

- The **autograph-score version**, BWV 1080.1, survives in **D-B Mus.ms. Bach P 200** and is dated **1742–1749** from watermark and handwriting; earliest traces of the subject occur in contrapuntal studies with Wilhelm Friedemann Bach around 1736–39. [Bach Digital, autograph version](https://www.bachdigital.de/receive/BachDigitalWork_work_00001266).
- The **original-print version**, BWV 1080.2, rests on engraving models from about **1747–48**; Bach changed ordering and added material, but the first edition appeared posthumously around the end of **1751**, with a second printing in spring 1752. [Bach Digital, print version](https://www.bachdigital.de/receive/BachDigitalWork_work_00011581).

Thus P 200 is an autograph primary source, while the 1751 print is a posthumous edition with pre-death authorial preparation but an editorial/publication history after Bach’s death. They differ in ordering and content. Contrapunctus 5 is fourth in P 200 but fifth in the original print. Any measure citation must name the movement, not infer an invariant cycle position. **Confidence: high.**

Alevizos’s peer-reviewed score analysis identifies Contrapunctus 5 as an opening into the collection’s concentrated stretto procedures. It analyzes six exposition groups through four independent parameters: entry distance; rectus/inversus orientation; dux/comes function; and voice position/raw material. Crucially, the entry distances do **not** form a single decreasing series: after a very close group in **mm. 33–45**, the following group in **mm. 47–62** is wider; a more continuously decreasing passage begins only around **m. 72**. [Alevizos 2019](https://revuemusicaleoicrm.org/rmo-vol6-n1/parameters-of-stretto/). **Confidence: high for the cited analysis; medium for claims of Bach’s compositional intention beyond what the score demonstrates.**

## 2. What “stretto” historically means

In fugal usage, *stretto* (Italian, “close/narrow”) means that a subsequent statement of the subject or answer begins before the preceding statement has finished, or at a shorter temporal distance than in the opening exposition. The decisive property is **temporal overlap/close imitation of thematic statements**, not simply thick texture, faster tempo, more notes, or voices playing in parallel. Nineteenth-century definitions already distinguish fugal stretto from the tempo-direction sense of *stretto/stringendo*. [Grove’s *Dictionary of Music and Musicians*, “Stretto”](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/A_Dictionary_of_Music_and_Musicians/Stretto); [Prout, *Fugue*, ch. 8](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Fugue_%28Prout%29/Chapter_8). **Confidence: high for the technical distinction; medium as evidence for Bach’s own terminology because these definitions postdate him.**

Bach left no treatise defining his stretto practice. The historically safer approach is to describe score relations—onset distance, overlap, pitch level, subject/answer function, inversion, augmentation/diminution—rather than claim that Bach himself named each passage “stretto.” [Alevizos 2019](https://revuemusicaleoicrm.org/rmo-vol6-n1/parameters-of-stretto/). **Confidence: high.**

## 3. Historical and aesthetic context

Fugue was not a rigid fill-in-the-form template. Its shared vocabulary included a subject, imitative entries, tonic/dominant answer relations, contrapuntal combination, episodes, sequences, cadence, inversion, augmentation/diminution, and sometimes stretto. Stretto is therefore a **technique within fugue**, not a compulsory terminal section.

Within WTC I, BWV 846/2 makes close thematic combination nearly the governing texture. The collection’s title-page pedagogy and systematic traversal of tonalities support hearing this fugue as an exemplary demonstration of contrapuntal possibility. Yet adjacent WTC fugues pursue different formal and affective problems; BWV 846/2 is not a miniature of all Bach fugues.

In *The Art of Fugue*, a common subject is subjected across separate contrapuncti to changing technical problems. Contrapuncti 5–7 intensively explore stretto alongside inversion and, later, augmentation/diminution. In Contrapunctus 5, the dotted rhythmic form helps certain overlapping combinations remain contrapuntally workable; Alevizos shows that not every imaginable offset is usable because particular collisions create unwanted dissonance. This is selective compositional design, not mechanical stacking. **Confidence: high.**

## 4. Evolution without a false ladder

A simple story—“1722 Bach uses basic stretto, late Bach perfects it by entries getting ever closer”—is unsupported.

- **WTC I / BWV 846/2 (1722 fair copy):** already exceptionally concentrated and varied. Its close entries do not await a late-career discovery.
- **WTC across both books:** McDonald’s 48-fugue corpus study reports no strong evidence that stretto passages become more numerous, more crowded, or temporally closer near fugue endings; density results directly oppose the standard hypothesis. [McDonald 2020](https://uwo.scholaris.ca/items/90e7593a-4d35-4969-a861-833fd36ed166).
- **Late *Art of Fugue*:** the project is more explicitly systematic across a cycle, but Contrapunctus 5’s actual onset distances are locally reordered rather than monotonically compressed. Its distinctiveness lies in coordinated exploration of multiple parameters and subject transformations, not merely “more voices sooner.”

The evolution is therefore best described as **changing project context**: concentrated local construction in one WTC fugue; broad variety across a pedagogical collection; and later, collection-wide comparison of contrapuntal procedures in *The Art of Fugue*. **Confidence: high.**

## 5. Persistent traits versus work-specific procedures

### Persistent across the selected evidence

- A recognizable subject remains intelligible under overlapping entry.
- Entry timing and pitch level are structural variables, not random echo settings.
- Independent lines preserve contrapuntal viability; density is constrained by what the subject can combine with.
- Tonic/dominant relations coexist with register redistribution and other tonal levels.
- Stretto can articulate continuation, return, demonstration, or climax; its function depends on placement.

**Confidence: medium-high:** recurring in the selected corpus, but not claimed for every Bach fugue.

### Specific to BWV 846/2

- Near-continuous subject saturation after the exposition.
- Very little episode space and no stable countersubject in the usual sense.
- Entry counts complicated by fragments at mm. 14–15 and 20.

**Confidence: high.**

### Specific to Contrapunctus 5 / *Art of Fugue*

- Coordinated rectus/inversus and dux/comes combinations.
- A dotted subject form whose rhythmic detail enables particular close combinations.
- Six analyzed exposition groups with non-monotonic global entry distances.
- Placement within a larger sequence that later extends stretto into diminution and augmentation.

**Confidence: high for score relations; medium for teleological readings of the cycle.**

## 6. Shared fugue vocabulary versus characteristic choices

| Shared eighteenth-century fugal vocabulary | Characteristic choices in this corpus |
|---|---|
| Tonic subject and dominant answer | BWV 846/2’s unusually sustained, episode-poor thematic saturation |
| Subject transferred among registers/voices | Its many offsets and pitch intervals within only 27 measures |
| Close imitation and invertible counterpoint | Contrapunctus 5’s systematic intersection of entry distance, inversion, and dux/comes |
| Episodes, cadence, modulation | Deliberate omission/reduction of episodes in BWV 846/2; differently grouped expositions in Contrapunctus 5 |
| Inversion, augmentation, diminution | Collection-scale isolation and recombination of these techniques in *The Art of Fugue* |

Calling every tonic/dominant imitation “Bach-like” mistakes common vocabulary for authorship. What is defensibly characteristic here is the **economical, technically tested recombination of a subject with itself under varied constraints**.

## 7. Exact judgment on the catalog claim

### What it can honestly reference

1. **“Tonic … answers”** — repeated subject statements at the tonic level, historically better called subject/dux entries rather than all “answers.”
2. **“Fifth … answers”** — dominant-level comes/answer relations central to fugal exposition and present in BWV 846/2. The engine may use a literal perfect-fifth transposition only as a simplification.
3. **“Octave … answers”** — the same thematic material redistributed to another voice/register at octave equivalence. This describes register/voice placement, not a third harmonic answer category parallel to tonic and dominant.
4. **“Enter progressively closer”** — an audible arrangement trajectory made by shortening successive onset delays; this is compatible with some local stretto progressions and with the historical meaning of “closer.”

### What it must not claim

- That tonic, fifth, and octave are Bach’s fixed three-entry recipe.
- That a tonal answer is always an exact perfect-fifth transposition; tonal answers may alter intervals to preserve tonic/dominant function.
- That “octave answer” is a standard function equivalent to dominant answer.
- That all entries must start on only those pitch classes; Bach’s later entries can occur at varied tonal levels.
- That closer entry distance automatically produces valid Bachian counterpoint. Contrapunctus 5 demonstrates unusable combinations and deliberate rhythmic/harmonic adjustments.
- That Bach’s fugues normally tighten monotonically or reserve stretto for a final climax.
- That a two-to-four-note live motif reproduces the long-range harmonic, voice-leading, inversion, and formal planning of the cited works.

**Recommended honest label text:** “A strict-canon arrangement inspired by Bach’s varied stretto practice: repeated motif entries at tonic-, dominant-, and octave-related placements arrive at shorter delays. It is not a reconstruction of a Bach fugue.”

## 8. Common caricatures and myths to avoid

1. **“Stretto means faster.”** False in fugal usage: it concerns entry spacing/overlap; a separate tempo-direction meaning exists.
2. **“Stretto always comes at the end to create climax.”** A traditional textbook tendency, contradicted by early and distributed stretti and by the WTC corpus result.
3. **“Closer is always more Bachian.”** False. Subject design, dissonance treatment, voice leading, and harmonic function constrain workable combinations.
4. **“Every Bach fugue follows exposition–episode–stretto.”** Fugue is procedurally diverse; BWV 846/2 is exceptional precisely because its subject saturation leaves almost no episode space.
5. **“Twenty-four entries in BWV 846/2 is settled fact.”** The total depends on admitting incomplete statements; Teoria identifies 22 complete entries and several fragments.
6. **“The Art of Fugue was published by Bach as a final fixed sequence.”** False. Autograph and posthumous print versions differ; dating, ordering, and completion history must remain distinct.
7. **“Bach invented tonic/dominant answers and stretto.”** False; these belong to inherited fugal/imitative practice. Bach’s distinction lies in particular constructions, not ownership of the vocabulary.
8. **“Mathematical procedure makes the music automatic.”** False. Alevizos shows multiple simultaneous systems that do not collapse into one simple numerical plan.

## 10. Confidence, disagreements, and gaps

| Claim | Confidence | Qualification |
|---|---:|---|
| Stretto is overlapping/closer thematic entry, not merely tempo or density | High | Terminology is later codified; Bach left no definition treatise |
| P 415 is Bach’s 1722 WTC I autograph fair copy with later revision evidence | High | Individual movements can predate compilation |
| BWV 846/2 is exceptionally saturated with varied stretto | High | Exact entry total depends on fragment criteria |
| “24 entries” is disputed | High | Teoria counts 22 complete; other writers include incomplete entries |
| WTC fugues do not support a general monotonic end-tightening law | High | Based on one recent thesis corpus methodology; replication would strengthen it |
| Contrapunctus 5 has non-progressive global entry distances | High | Alevizos’s grouping is an interpretation, though measure relations are observable |
| *Art of Fugue* autograph and original print are distinct versions | High | Exact intentions for final ordering/completion remain debated |
| Tonic/fifth/octave + shrinking delays is Bach’s recipe | Rejected | It is a modern bounded abstraction only |

**Outstanding gaps:**

1. The NBA critical reports were inaccessible; editorial variants affecting particular BWV 846/2 entry readings were not collated.
2. McDonald’s repository abstract was accessible, but the full tables needed to quote exact onset-distance distributions for BWV 846/2 were not extracted. The report therefore uses the corpus conclusion, not unsupported numeric detail.
3. No claim is made about tempo, articulation, instrumentation, or performance practice; the sources do not prescribe one definitive realization.
4. Contrapunctus 7 was considered but excluded from detailed measure claims because Contrapunctus 5 had the stronger accessible peer-reviewed analysis and already tests the preset’s “progressively closer” premise.
5. Analytical labels such as incomplete subject, exposition grouping, and tonal level can differ among analysts; implementation should test audible motif identity rather than pretend those disagreements are settled.

## Conclusion for parent synthesis

**Historically defensible core:** one motif recognized across independent, overlapping entries; entry delay as a structural parameter; tonic/dominant relationship plus register redistribution; and a bounded movement from spacious to closer imitation.

**Non-negotiable approximation statement:** Preset 07 is a strict-canon interaction inspired by selected Bach stretto procedures. Its literal pitch offsets and progressive delay compression are product rules, not a model of Bach’s general fugue practice. It omits tonal-answer adjustment, subject-specific contrapuntal testing, inversion, harmonic planning, episodes, cadences, and source-specific voice leading.

**Role A decision:** **ready for parent synthesis only if the UI and implementation retain that approximation boundary; otherwise reject as misleading.**
