# Research: Preset 04 “Mensuration Web” — Role B theory and temporal behavior

**Reference:** Johannes Ockeghem (c. 1410/30–1497), especially *Missa prolationum* (second half of the fifteenth century), with narrower comparison to Ockeghem’s canonic practice and broader surviving output.  
**Role:** Independent B — music theory and temporal behavior.  
**Retrieved:** 2026-07-19.  
**Catalog contract:** one exact three-to-six-note motif produces simultaneous proportional-speed layers; current baseline is Free Imitation. This report does not claim to reproduce Ockeghem or a complete Mass.

## Summary

The defensible identity of “Mensuration Web” is not “four echoes with arbitrary speed knobs.” In *Missa prolationum*, notated material is interpreted under different mensurations: paired voices derive from shared written lines, yet differing binary/ternary divisions make their elapsed durations, alignments, and entry points diverge in structured proportions. Ockeghem then controls the resulting vertical intervals, cadences, and registral texture so independent lines remain singable and the complex mechanism is normally heard as continuous, consonance-directed polyphony rather than a rhythmic stunt. [Blue Heron program essay](https://www.blueheron.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/BH_March2019program_WWW.pdf) [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Missa-prolationum) [The Other *Missa Prolationum*](https://doi.org/10.1525/jm.2020.37.3.267)

Contrapunk’s CanonLane can anchor one performed phrase and apply proportional timing to its onsets and durations, which is useful. Its current “Mensuration Quartet,” however, combines four unrelated ratios (1, 2, 1/2, 4), heterogeneous harmony algorithms, serial cascade references, and fixed delayed entries. That is a Free Imitation approximation, not a historically close model of the Mass’s two simultaneous two-voice mensuration canons.

## 1. Scope, corpus, and claim boundary

1. **Work-specific center — high confidence.** *Missa prolationum* is a four-voice Mass cycle whose movements use double mensuration canons: two notated lines each generate a second line through mensural reinterpretation, producing two simultaneous two-part canons. The unifying mechanism spans the Mass, while canonic interval and local realization change by section. [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Missa-prolationum) [Blue Heron](https://www.blueheron.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/BH_March2019program_WWW.pdf)
2. **Source plurality matters — high confidence.** The Chigi Codex presents the work in compact canonic notation, while Vienna 11883 supplies resolved voices in uniform mensurations. Therefore “the work is only an esoteric visual puzzle” is false, and modern realization choices are not entirely self-evident. [The Other *Missa Prolationum*](https://doi.org/10.1525/jm.2020.37.3.267)
3. **Broader Ockeghem — medium confidence.** Long, horizontally conceived lines, relatively continuous texture, subtle motivic connection, and avoidance of obvious block repetition recur in commentary on his Masses, but individual works use strikingly different structural plans. *Missa prolationum*’s exhaustive mensuration-canon cycle is work-specific, not a synonym for Ockeghem’s whole style. [TMM works overview](http://www.medieval.org/music/ccc/aaaa980716.html) [TMM context](http://www.medieval.org/music/ccc/aaaa980702.html)
4. **Period boundary — high confidence.** This is fifteenth-century mensural/modal practice, not common-practice functional tonality, equal-bar loop composition, or later species-counterpoint pedagogy. Mensuration concerns contextual division of note values: modus (long/breve), tempus (breve/semibreve), and prolation (semibreve/minim), each potentially binary or ternary. [Music Theory Online, “Out of the Blue”](https://doi.org/10.30535/mto.29.4.4) [Cambridge, “Measuring measurable music”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-history-of-fifteenthcentury-music/measuring-measurable-music-in-the-fifteenth-century/9316B481F97BA4CD38C580B3CE031D3E)

## 2. Theoretical and temporal findings

### 2.1 Modal/tonal organization

- **Pre-tonal, final-directed organization — high confidence.** Treat pitch organization as modal counterpoint oriented by finals, ranges, species and cadential goals, not as a generated I–IV–V progression. A modern scale choice may constrain pitches, but it must not be advertised as a complete modal analysis.
- **Canonic interval changes are structural — high confidence.** The Mass begins with close canonic relationships and expands the interval between canonic voices through later sections; the Blue Heron analytical table identifies Kyrie I at unison and Christe at the upper second, with later sections continuing the expansion. This makes “same transposition everywhere” a poor work-specific model. [Blue Heron](https://www.blueheron.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/BH_March2019program_WWW.pdf)
- **Ficta and interval quality are contextual — medium confidence.** Shared written material at another interval does not guarantee identical semitone content; hexachordal placement and singers’ ficta can differentiate the sounding version. Hard quantization to a single modern seven-note scale is therefore an approximation.
- **No independent tonal centers per generated line — high confidence.** The paired canons participate in one contrapuntal field. Assigning each lane an unrelated key would contradict the scoped model.

### 2.2 Consonance, dissonance, and sonority

- **Consonance-directed vertical control — high confidence.** Perfect unisons, fifths and octaves and imperfect thirds/sixths organize structural simultaneities; exposed perfect consonances need controlled approach, while seconds, sevenths and fourths above a bass-like support require passing, neighbor, syncopated, or other context rather than becoming static chord colors.
- **Dissonance is temporal, not a pitch-class blacklist — high confidence.** Because proportional readings continually shift alignments, a note may be consonant at one structural point and dissonant while passing at another. A test must inspect beat position, preparation/continuation, and resolution rather than ban every vertical second.
- **Cadences locally override undifferentiated flow — medium-high confidence.** Cadential contrapuntal motions coordinate voices into perfect sonorities, commonly with stepwise directed motion and possible ficta. The preset should create a recognizably thinner/longer phrase ending, not merely stop four queues at arbitrary times.
- **Triadic chord-stack emission is misleading — high confidence.** The historical object is four interacting melodic parts, not each canonic voice expanded into its own two-to-four-note chord. CanonLane’s per-voice HarmonyEngine stack can multiply a four-line conception into eight or more pitches and obscure independence.

### 2.3 Melody, motive, and paired voice behavior

- **Singable shared material — high confidence.** Source gestures should be short, rhythmically exact, chiefly stepwise cells with one controlled leap. The identity comes from recognizable relation under changed mensural reading, not from randomized reharmonization.
- **Transformation preserves event order and interval succession — high confidence.** Within each derived pair, pitch order and melodic contour should remain recognizably shared unless transposition requires contextual adjustment. Contrary-motion substitution is not mensuration canon.
- **Paired derivation, four-part independence — high confidence.** The closest computational abstraction is two source/pair relationships sounding together: A and A′ derive from one line; B and B′ derive from another. The four sounding parts are independent in placement and duration, but not independent compositions or metronomes.
- **Entry is an outcome of interpretation — medium-high confidence.** Under different mensurations, voices may begin together in notation yet sounding attacks separate as the shared line unfolds; some sections also imply rests/entry distances. A fixed echo delay alone captures only entry displacement, not mensural process. [Musica Nova Lyon](https://www.musicanova-lyon.fr/en/discography-teaching/ockeghem-missa-prolationum/)
- **Persistent versus work-specific.** Horizontal fluency and coordinated cadential arrival are defensible broader traits (medium confidence); an entire Mass made from double mensuration canons and systematically expanding canonic interval is specific to *Missa prolationum* (high confidence).

### 2.4 Mensuration, prolation, and proportional duration

**Required conceptual distinction:**

1. A **mensural reinterpretation** applies contextual binary/ternary division rules to the same written note signs. For example, the same semibreve/minim succession can occupy different elapsed time when one reading gives two minims per semibreve and another gives three. Relative attacks and durations follow from the notation’s hierarchy.
2. A **naive time-stretch** multiplies every timestamp and duration in a performed recording by one scalar. CanonLane currently does this phrase-relatively; it preserves ordered proportions but does not understand note-value levels, imperfection/alteration, coloration, tactus equivalence, or mensuration changes.
3. An **arbitrary rate knob** chooses any continuous factor (CanonLane permits 0.125–8.0). Historical plausibility needs a small, named ratio set derived from binary/ternary mensural relationships, not “0.73 because it sounds complex.”
4. **Independent metronomes** accumulate separate clock drift. The Mass’s voices are proportionally related within one temporal system; all generated layers must use one transport epoch and rational mappings so expected realignments are deterministic.

The four common tempus/prolation combinations yield binary/ternary nesting (2×2, 2×3, 3×2, 3×3 at the relevant levels), but their performed tactus and equivalence are interpretive questions, not four universally valid modern BPM multipliers. The Vienna resolutions demonstrate that historically informed realization can normalize notation while preserving the metric result. [The Other *Missa Prolationum*](https://doi.org/10.1525/jm.2020.37.3.267) [Music Theory Online](https://doi.org/10.30535/mto.29.4.4)

**Implementation-safe initial ratio vocabulary:** `{1:1, 3:2, 2:1}` within a shared phrase clock, optionally reciprocal `{2:3, 1:2}` only when the direction is clearly labeled. This is an abstraction, not a claim that these five scalars transcribe every section of the Mass.

### 2.5 Phrase, section, density, register, and texture

- **Phrase attack:** expose the source or one pair clearly; avoid all generated stacks attacking as a block.
- **Continuation:** proportional separation produces new vertical combinations; density can grow to four single-note lines, while entries remain audibly related.
- **Cadence:** reduce new entries, lengthen destination notes, converge toward a perfect consonance, then clear the phrase state.
- **Silence:** silence is structural input. The catalog’s requested space after a three-to-six-note motif is what lets the web unfold and supplies a safe phrase boundary.
- **Register:** four differentiated, overlapping but non-crossing vocal regions are preferable to octave duplication. Ockeghem is often associated with a full/low sonorous span, but “put everything in the bass” is a caricature; register must preserve line legibility.
- **Across sections:** retain the proportional-canon principle while changing canonic interval, registral pair, density, and cadence span. Do not simply turn the speed knobs farther each section.
- **Dynamics/articulation:** not primary notated identity here, but moderate legato and non-accented continuation support audible line independence. Heavy velocity accents on every rational collision falsely turn the texture into modern polymetric groove.

## 3. Testable stylistic invariants

1. **One clock:** all scheduled events derive from the same transport beat origin; pause freezes them and reset/stop cancels them with matched NoteOffs.
2. **Rational relation:** every temporal variant uses an allow-listed named ratio; no free-running clock or cumulative floating-point phase drift.
3. **Phrase-relative transformation:** for source events at offsets `x_i` and durations `d_i`, a scalar approximation must emit `anchor + delay + r*x_i` and duration `r*d_i`; event order cannot change.
4. **Shared-line identity:** within a pair, scale-degree interval succession remains identical after a constant diatonic transposition, except explicitly logged boundary/ficta handling.
5. **Bounded texture:** target four sounding melodic subjects, not four chord stacks. At least 75% of active generated attacks in the baseline should be single-note lane emissions.
6. **Paired organization:** four lines should be audibly/groupably two derivational pairs, rather than a single serial cascade A→B→C→D.
7. **Consonance-directed structural beats:** phrase opening and final stable attacks use unison/octave/fifth/third/sixth relations; transient dissonances must move by step or release before the cadential destination.
8. **Cadential lifecycle:** after the source rest trigger, accept no new phrase events; pending valid events finish according to a bounded phrase horizon, destination notes release, and all queues/held maps return empty.
9. **Register legibility:** no voice crossing introduced solely by preset octave processing; avoid Mirror octave multiplication.
10. **Determinism:** replaying the same motif at the same transport position and settings produces identical on/off beats and pitches.
11. **Section variation preserves identity:** changes to interval/register/density may occur only at phrase boundaries, never by mutating ratios beneath held notes.
12. **Honest label:** UI/help says “proportional free-imitation approximation inspired by *Missa prolationum*,” not “authentic Ockeghem mensuration.”

## 4. Parameters that may vary without losing identity

- Tempo: approximately 48–84 modern quarter-note beats/minute, provided all layers share transport.
- Source motif: 3–6 single notes; mostly steps, up to one fourth/fifth leap; 2–6 beats total.
- Pair transposition: unison through octave, selected at phrase boundary; close intervals early and wider intervals later are work-informed.
- Named temporal relation: 1:1, 3:2, or 2:1 (and labeled reciprocals where supported).
- Entry rest/delay: 0–2 beats, but pair members should not all receive unrelated offsets.
- Register: pair A upper/middle and pair B middle/lower, with approximately a third-to-octave separation and no forced octave mirroring.
- Density: two-line opening → three/four-line continuation → two-line or unified cadential close.
- Cadence horizon: one phrase or bar after final source release, preferably inferred from motif completion rather than hard-coded globally.
- Mode/final: may follow user-selected diatonic mode; modal interchange should be off by default because random borrowing weakens shared-line identity.

## 5. Misleading behaviors / rejected shortcuts

1. Four continuously adjustable rates advertised as the four Renaissance prolations.
2. Four `setInterval`/thread metronomes that drift independently.
3. Merely lengthening NoteOffs while leaving onset positions unchanged.
4. Per-note delay from the latest player attack instead of one phrase anchor.
5. RandomBelow, ContraryMotion, BachChorale, or functional-chord substitution inside lines claimed to be the same subject.
6. Serial cascades in which V3 transforms V2’s already transformed pitch and V4 transforms V3; this erases two-pair shared-line derivation.
7. Every canon voice generating a chord stack; eight-plus pitches are not the scoped four-voice texture.
8. Continuous dense keyboard input with no phrase rest; it prevents anchor reset and cadence.
9. Calling every vertical second an error, or conversely treating unresolved seconds/sevenths as stable color.
10. Functional dominant-tonic timelines, swing, randomized humanization, microtiming drift, or accent-heavy polymeter.
11. Treating *Missa prolationum* as Ockeghem’s whole career style or using “mystical mathematical genius” as the only musical explanation.
12. Assuming the Chigi notation has one uncontested modern performance solution; Vienna 11883 and current scholarship expose competing realizations.

## 6. Temporal state sketch and triggers

```text
IDLE
  -- NoteOn while transport running --> CAPTURE (set phrase_anchor once)
CAPTURE
  -- collect 3–6 ordered notes; schedule all pair transforms from anchor
  -- source silence > 2 beats OR explicit phrase release --> UNFOLD
UNFOLD
  -- no new source admitted to current phrase
  -- pair A audible first; pair B joins; rational alignments separate/rejoin
  -- pending count reaches density threshold --> DENSITY_PEAK
DENSITY_PEAK
  -- final scheduled source-derived onset OR phrase horizon approaches --> CADENCE
CADENCE
  -- suppress new followers; favor held/stepwise destination and bounded release
  -- all NoteOffs emitted and queues empty --> IDLE
IDLE at section boundary
  -- completed phrase count / explicit next-section trigger --> widen canonic interval
     or swap register/pair ratio, then CAPTURE
```

**Triggers:** transport must be running before capture; a gap strictly greater than CanonLane’s current 2-beat silence threshold resets its anchor; Stop/Reset, preset switch, disabling lane/Companion, or voice-vector replacement must force cleanup rather than advance to CADENCE. Pause should freeze beat time, not flush by wall-clock time. A new phrase arriving before the prior phrase has fully released needs either rejection or a generation ID so old and new notes cannot share one `held[note]` entry.

## 7. Novel abstract scale-degree / beat examples

These examples are invented and do not copy Ockeghem melodies. `b` is transport beat from phrase anchor; durations are in the same beat unit. Accidentals are omitted deliberately.

### Example A — binary/ternary pair, same pitch order

Source A: `1@b0 d1, 2@b1 d1, 4@b2 d2`.  
Follower A′ at upper unison/ratio 3:2: `1@b0 d1.5, 2@b1.5 d1.5, 4@b3 d3`.  
**Tests:** order `1–2–4` is unchanged; onset gaps and durations are exactly ×1.5; both use the same anchor; no independent clock.

### Example B — two derived pairs, not a serial cascade

Written line A: `5@b0 d1, 4@b1 d1, 2@b2 d2`; A′ = same degrees +0 at ratio 1:1.  
Written line B: `1@b0 d2, 3@b2 d1, 2@b3 d1`; B′ = B transposed +4 degrees at ratio 2:1, yielding abstract `5@b0 d4, 7@b4 d2, 6@b6 d2`.  
**Tests:** B′ derives from B, never from A′; four lines share b0; only named rational ratios occur; long B′ continuation raises density without extra chord tones.

### Example C — delayed close interval and cadence release

Source: `3@b0 d0.5, 4@b0.5 d0.5, 5@b1 d1, 2@b2 d1, 1@b3 d2`.  
Follower: upper second, delay `0.5`, ratio `3:2`: `4@b0.5 d0.75, 5@b1.25 d0.75, 6@b2 d1.5, 3@b3.5 d1.5, 2@b5 d3`.  
**Tests:** a source release after b5 must not Cancel the final follower if phrase completion is selected; the final release must occur by b8 and leave no pending notes.

### Example D — across-section expansion

Section 1 motif: `1@b0, 2@b1, 3@b2, 1@b3`; pair answer at unison and 3:2.  
After complete silence/queue drain, Section 2 repeats the abstract contour with answer at upper fourth and 2:1, placed one register lower.  
**Tests:** interval/ratio change happens only after section boundary; no held note changes pitch or duration retroactively; contour remains `+1,+1,-2` in both answers.

## 8. Exact current Free Imitation / CanonLane mapping

### Current UI template used as the baseline

`ui/src/lib/components/CompanionPanel.svelte` defines **Mensuration Quartet** (`id: mensuration-quartet`) and `applyTemplate()` forces `imitativeForm = free_imitation`, writes the voices, then enables Companion and CanonLane.

| Voice | delay_beats | transpose_degrees | time_ratio | harmony_mode | reference_voice |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| V1 | 0.5 | 0 | 1.0 | StrictCounterpoint | Player/null |
| V2 | 1.0 | +4 (diatonic fifth) | 2.0 | BachChorale | V1/0 |
| V3 | 1.5 | −3 (diatonic fourth below) | 0.5 | ContraryMotion | V1/0 |
| V4 | 2.0 | +7 (octave) | 4.0 | BachChorale | V2/1 |

No template values are supplied for `voice_count`, `voice_position`, voice-leading enable/style, octave mode, species, strictness, or Hold override; they therefore inherit/default through CanonLane’s snapshot logic. `applyTemplate()` also does not set key, scale, BPM, time signature, target, Hold, or transport state.

### What CanonLane actually does

- Numeric bounds: delay `[0,16]`, diatonic transpose `[-7,7]`, ratio `[0.125,8]`, max eight voices.
- One phrase anchor is created on first input and reset after a gap **greater than 2 beats**.
- For an input onset at `now`, `relative_on = now − anchor`; each voice schedules `fire_at = anchor + delay + relative_on × time_ratio`.
- NoteOff schedules at the voice onset plus `source_duration × time_ratio`.
- A valid `reference_voice` points only backward. Current mapping is Player→V1; V1→V2 and V3; V2→V4.
- The subject is diatonically transposed with `harmonize_smart`, then passed through a per-voice HarmonyEngine. It may therefore emit a stack, not one line.
- Per-voice engines synchronize key, scale mode, mode, voice count/position, voice-leading settings, octave mode, species/strictness, and transport beat phase from the shared engine unless overridden.
- Emission happens on `tick()` against `transport.total_beats()`, not on the next player note.
- Pending events and held state are cleared when disabled, runtime-reset, or voices are replaced.

### Named gaps between current mapping and the research target

1. **Wrong derivational topology:** one serial/branching source chain, not two written-line pairs.
2. **Wrong transformations:** ContraryMotion and BachChorale alter melodic/harmonic identity; mensuration does not imply inversion or chorale harmonization.
3. **Extreme/non-specific ratios:** 1×, 2×, 0.5×, 4× are augmentation/diminution effects, not a documented realization of the four mensurations in a selected Mass section.
4. **Scalar stretch only:** no note-value hierarchy, binary/ternary contextual reading, alteration/imperfection, coloration, tactus-equivalence policy, or mensuration change.
5. **Chord-stack inflation:** every subject may be harmonized by a mini-engine; baseline can exceed four melodic voices.
6. **No captured second written line:** live input supplies only one stream; genuine double canon requires two source lines or a bounded derived second source.
7. **No cadence/section FSM:** fixed 2-beat phrase anchor reset is not cadence recognition; no widening interval sequence across sections.
8. **No named rational ratio model:** arbitrary float ratios invite ahistorical settings and drift/rounding ambiguity.
9. **No explicit register-pair plan:** transposition is not the same as stable vocal register assignment.
10. **Pitch substitution on failure:** `harmonize_smart` and stateful engines may change output for scale/interchange/range reasons, weakening exact shared-line tests.
11. **Retrigger identity risk:** held state is keyed only by MIDI note, so repeated same-pitch inputs before prior lifecycle completion can overwrite phrase data even though pending queues retain events.
12. **Baseline naming mismatch:** “Mensuration Quartet … (Ockeghem)” overstates fidelity unless UI copy calls it proportional free imitation.

**Minimum honest reuse:** configure four **single-note PassThrough** canon voices; use two pairwise references rather than a full cascade; allow only named rational ratios; disable modal interchange, octave Mirror, and random humanization; keep one transport; present it as a scalar proportional-canon approximation. A truer implementation needs a two-source phrase model and mensural event interpreter, not more speed knobs.

## 9. Transport, Hold, and lifecycle requirements

1. **Transport is mandatory.** CanonLane schedules from `total_beats`; the preset must refuse or clearly remain armed while stopped. Starting transport after capture must not compress overdue events into one tick.
2. **Single epoch.** Capture the phrase anchor once from shared transport. BPM changes must preserve a documented musical-beat policy and must not rescale already elapsed wall time inconsistently between voices.
3. **Pause.** A pause must freeze sample/beat progression and all pending on/off deadlines. No wall-clock scheduler may continue behind it.
4. **Stop.** Tauri `transport_stop` stops transport and requests router All Notes Off (CC123). Companion runtime state must also reset before replay so stale pending NoteOns cannot fire after restart.
5. **Reset.** Reset rewinds sample position and requests All Notes Off. It must clear CanonLane queues/held/anchor atomically; otherwise old absolute beat deadlines can become future events in the new timeline.
6. **Hold precedence.** Effective Hold is `voice override → lane override → global`. For this preset, choose **PhraseEnd** at lane level: released source notes may finish events inside the bounded phrase, but no indefinite `Forever` tail.
7. **Hold semantics:** `Cancel` is safe but destroys slow derived lines; `NearFuture` truncates ratios according to a tail knob; `Forever` can create long/stuck tails; current `PhraseEnd` uses `anchor + beats_per_bar`, which is only a bar proxy and can wrongly cut a 3:2 or 2:1 motif. The target needs `phrase_end = max scheduled transformed off-beat`, capped for safety.
8. **Matched offs.** Cancelled pending ons must have paired offs removed; already-emitted ons must still receive offs. Existing CanonLane C1/C2 logic addresses orphan-off removal and duplicate pending offs, but acceptance must cover this preset’s longest ratio.
9. **Mutation safety.** Ratio, transposition, Hold, key/mode, voice vector, preset, and register changes apply only at an empty/phrase boundary or must drain/diff all sounding notes. Never reinterpret a held phrase in place.
10. **Disable/adapter destroy/device loss.** Flush internal synth and all MIDI targets, clear pending/held/anchor state, and make repeated cleanup idempotent.
11. **Completion invariant.** After Stop/Reset/disable or after the bounded phrase tail, active generated note count, pending_on, pending_off, and held count must all be zero.

## 10. Competing interpretations and confidence

- **Mensuration sign → performed ratio (medium confidence):** binary/ternary division is secure; exact tactus equivalence and modern duration mapping can differ by editorial/performance interpretation. Do not hard-code a universal “circle = 1.5×” rule without section-specific source work.
- **Entry model (medium confidence):** descriptions emphasizing simultaneous starts explain the principle, while resolved sections include rests/entry distances and canonic entries. Product language should say “shared notation yields proportionally displaced sounding lines,” not insist every sounding voice attacks together.
- **Symbolic expansion (low-to-medium confidence):** recent scholarship relates expanding canonic intervals to theological/geometrical symbolism, but fifteenth-century practical treatises are silent on such rhetoric. It is unsuitable as an engine invariant. [Cambridge/Boydell chapter summary](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/BC292C27331DAC2FC7E10EF8A2DFF25D/9781805430506c5_p101-124_CBO.pdf/concealment-revealed.pdf)
- **Broad “Ockeghem sound” (medium confidence):** low sonority, continuity, and long lines are useful performance tendencies, but surviving works differ substantially and attribution/editorial questions remain. They should tune defaults, not become hard validity rules.
- **Modern strict-counterpoint engine (low confidence as historical proxy):** avoiding parallels and preferring consonance is directionally relevant, but Fux-style species and the engine’s scoring are later abstractions. PassThrough paired lines plus bounded vertical validation is more honest than calling StrictCounterpoint “Ockeghem mode.”

## 11. Gaps and next research steps

1. Obtain an accessible critical/resolved score and encode at least Kyrie I, Christe, and one later expanding-interval section to verify exact signs, entry rests, canonic intervals, cadence points, voice ranges, and sounding duration ratios measure by measure.
2. The paywalled full argument of *The Other Missa Prolationum* was not available here; only its abstract was verified. Review its statistical metrical findings before defining a mensuration interpreter.
3. No machine-readable corpus analysis was run for interval/dissonance rates, phrase length, or register. Current claims are qualitative.
4. Exact ficta differs by source/editor/performer; no preset should automate “authentic accidentals” without an explicit editorial policy.
5. The current code lacks two independent captured source lines, cadence detection, rational-time representation, and section state; parent synthesis must decide whether the honest baseline stays scalar Free Imitation or the preset remains research-pending.

## Findings

1. **The identity is paired mensural reinterpretation within one clock, not four arbitrary stretches.** [Blue Heron](https://www.blueheron.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/BH_March2019program_WWW.pdf)
2. **The Mass is a work-specific double-canon cycle and should not stand for all Ockeghem.** [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Missa-prolationum) [TMM works overview](http://www.medieval.org/music/ccc/aaaa980716.html)
3. **Source transmission supports multiple realizational perspectives.** Chigi’s canonic notation and Vienna’s resolutions warn against pretending one scalar mapping is definitive. [The Other *Missa Prolationum*](https://doi.org/10.1525/jm.2020.37.3.267)
4. **Current Mensuration Quartet is only Free Imitation.** Its exact 1×/2×/0.5×/4× cascade with StrictCounterpoint/BachChorale/ContraryMotion is materially different from shared-line mensuration.
5. **A bounded, transport-driven single-note approximation is possible now**, but cadence, two-pair source identity, rational mensural rules, and lifecycle cleanup must be explicit acceptance conditions.
