# Phase 10.2 Preset Research Pack Template

A public reference is not permission to reduce an artist to one scale or chord trick. Each operational preset needs three independent reports and a parent synthesis showing what the referenced style actually does, how it changes through time, and which parts Contrapunk can represent honestly.

## Required research roles

### A. Artist/style historian and primary-source researcher

Study authoritative sources, score-based analyses, institutional archives, treatises, and composer interviews where available.

Required sections:

1. **Scope and corpus** — name the works, recordings, scores, periods, or interviews used. Do not claim a whole-career style from one famous piece.
2. **Historical and aesthetic context** — what problem or tradition the artist was engaging with.
3. **Career/style evolution** — early, middle, late, project-specific, or medium-specific changes. State which period the preset references.
4. **Persistent traits versus period traits** — separate recurring concerns from techniques used only in selected works.
5. **Relationship to peers/tradition** — what is shared vocabulary and what is unusually characteristic.
6. **Common caricatures to avoid** — simplistic claims, genre stereotypes, or fan shorthand unsupported by evidence.
7. **Primary evidence** — cited URLs/publications and relevant work/measure/timestamp references where possible.
8. **Confidence and gaps** — high/medium/low confidence by claim.

### B. Music-theory and temporal-behavior researcher

Translate the scoped style into musical behavior, especially how it develops and moves rather than a static chord/scale snapshot.

Required dimensions:

- tonal center, modality, chromaticism, tuning;
- interval and chord vocabulary, spacing, inversion, doubling, tension/resolution;
- melodic contour, motivic construction, repetition, transformation, ornament;
- voice motion, independence, imitation, contrary/oblique/parallel behavior;
- rhythm, meter, subdivision, swing, accent, phase, polymeter;
- phrase length, density, silence, cadence, formal pacing;
- register, texture, orchestration/voice-role relationships;
- dynamics and articulation when they affect arrangement behavior;
- interaction between layers;
- **within-phrase evolution:** how the texture changes from attack to continuation to cadence;
- **across-section evolution:** how material accumulates, fragments, modulates, thins, returns, or transforms;
- **career/period evolution:** which theoretical rules belong to the selected period.

Deliverables:

1. A concise set of testable stylistic invariants.
2. Parameters that may vary without losing identity.
3. Behaviors that would make the result misleading.
4. A temporal state sketch, such as `Sparse → imitation → density peak → cadence`, with triggers.
5. Two or more short abstract input/output examples in scale degrees and beats; do not copy copyrighted melodies.
6. Mapping to existing HarmonyEngine/Lane capabilities and clearly named gaps.
7. Citations, confidence, and competing interpretations.

### C. Performer and interaction researcher

Determine how a live guitarist or keyboardist can evoke the researched behavior through Contrapunk rather than expecting the preset to manufacture it from arbitrary input.

Required sections:

1. Best source gesture: single notes, motif, riff, sustained tone, or chord.
2. Suggested register, tempo range, note lengths, articulation, dynamics, density, and silence.
3. Transport dependency and rhythmic precision requirements.
4. How the player should develop material over a phrase or section.
5. Listening/responding instructions: what generated layer to wait for before continuing.
6. Guitar-specific constraints under the clean-monophonic claim.
7. Keyboard-specific opportunities and chord-density limits.
8. Failure gestures that create mud, note storms, or stylistic contradiction.
9. Plain-language **Play it like** prompt plus expanded guidance.
10. A 30–60 second user acceptance exercise.

## Parent synthesis (`synthesis.md`)

The parent reads all three reports and records:

1. **Referenced scope** — artist/work/period actually represented.
2. **Style thesis** — one paragraph describing the sound and its temporal movement.
3. **Invariants** — rules the implementation must preserve.
4. **Evolution model** — phrase and section states, transitions, and triggers.
5. **Caricatures/rejected shortcuts** — tempting but misleading implementations.
6. **Contrapunk mapping** — exact scale, harmony, voice, Hold, lane, mix, timing, and capability values.
7. **Approximation statement** — what the engine cannot reproduce and how UI copy remains honest.
8. **Performer contract** — final short and expanded Play guidance.
9. **Acceptance examples** — abstract test inputs, expected relationships/evolution, and lifecycle expectations.
10. **Research traceability** — cited sources, report paths, confidence, and unresolved disagreements.
11. **Decision** — `ready_for_implementation`, `needs_more_research`, or `reject_as_misleading`.

## Multiple-reference presets

When a preset cites multiple artists, reports must not blend them indiscriminately. Identify:

- each artist's relevant period and technique;
- the documented intersection that motivates the preset;
- meaningful differences deliberately excluded;
- why the resulting preset is a coherent technique rather than a generic average.

## Research quality gate

A report fails when it:

- treats one scale, instrument, or famous work as an artist's entire style;
- lacks authoritative citations;
- describes only static harmony and not temporal evolution;
- copies protected melodic material rather than using abstract examples;
- cannot translate claims into performer guidance or testable engine behavior;
- contradicts another report without surfacing the disagreement;
- claims exact imitation when the implementation is only a bounded arrangement approximation.
