# Phase 10.2 — Accepted 50-Preset Catalog

Built-in titles use techniques or imagery. Composer names appear only as references/influences, not claims of imitation. Every catalog record must carry the result, performance guidance, references, feature requirements, and an honest availability state.

This catalog is a product brief, not sufficient research evidence. Before a preset becomes operational, three fresh read-only researchers must produce cited historical/primary-source, music-theory, and performer/interaction reports. The parent synthesizes them into stylistic invariants, caricatures to avoid, acceptable approximations, exact configuration, and acceptance examples under `research/NN-slug/`.

**Input default:** clean monophonic notes. Chords are accepted only when a preset says so. Timing-dependent presets require transport.

## Classical — 20

1. **Cloister Organum** — References: Léonin, Pérotin. Result: open fourths, fifths, and octaves around a chant line. Play: slow held stepwise notes with clear phrase rests. Needs: explicit interval stacks.
2. **Modal Linework** — Reference: Palestrina. Result: four smooth modal voices with controlled dissonance. Play: legato scale-shaped melody, moderate pace, few large leaps. Current engine baseline.
3. **Venetian Galleries** — References: Andrea and Giovanni Gabrieli. Result: separated choirs answer and combine. Play: short declarations followed by one or two bars of silence. Needs: multiple stable groups and antiphonal routing.
4. **Mensuration Web** — Reference: Ockeghem. Result: one motif at simultaneous proportional speeds. Play: exact three-to-six-note motif with space afterward. Current Free Imitation baseline.
5. **Crabwise Reflection** — Reference: Bach's Crab Canon. Result: a completed phrase returns backward. Play: clean four-to-eight-note phrase, then stop for the reverse answer. Needs: phrase capture and reverse.
6. **Mirror Canon** — References: Bach and Renaissance inversion canons. Result: equal interval inversion around a tonal axis. Play: recognizable angular intervals, no chords. Needs: true axis inversion.
7. **Stretto Engine** — Reference: Bach fugues. Result: tonic, fifth, and octave answers enter progressively closer. Play: repeat a strong two-to-four-note subject with consistent rhythm. Current Strict Canon baseline.
8. **Suspension Garland** — References: Fux, Palestrina. Result: prepared suspensions resolve across strong beats. Play: long legato notes crossing beats and barlines. Current Species IV baseline; transport required.
9. **Ground-Bass Theatre** — References: Monteverdi, Purcell. Result: repeating bass under evolving counterpoint. Play: record a simple bass figure, then a freer upper melody. Needs: recordable ostinato lane.
10. **Alberti Clockwork** — References: Mozart, Clementi. Result: low-high-middle-high accompaniment. Play: sustained melody or simple harmony changes on downbeats. Needs: beat-synchronized pattern lane.
11. **Motive Forge** — Reference: Beethoven. Result: a small motif is fragmented, displaced, and expanded. Play: repeat one emphatic three-to-five-note idea and leave development space. Needs: motif recognition and transforms.
12. **Planed Cathedral** — Reference: Debussy. Result: whole-tone or pentatonic parallel chord planes. Play: slow spacious line, sustained notes, few tonal cadences. Current harmony baseline.
13. **Axis Mirror** — Reference: Bartók. Result: symmetrical reflections around a recurring center. Play: alternate steps and bold leaps around one anchor note. Needs: pitch-class axis mapping.
14. **Color-Mode Windows** — Reference: Messiaen. Result: rotating whole-tone, octatonic, and augmented colors. Play: sparse strongly articulated modal notes. Current symmetric-scale baseline; chord rotations are an extension.
15. **Micropolyphonic Cloud** — Reference: Ligeti. Result: many micro-delayed lines blur into a shifting mass. Play: very few long tones, gentle bends, gradual dynamics. Needs: micro-delays, detune, density, and more bounded voices.
16. **Tintinnabuli Halo** — Reference: Arvo Pärt. Result: a melody is shadowed by nearest tonic-triad tones. Play: slow plain stepwise line with meaningful silence. Needs: tintinnabuli harmony strategy.
17. **Phase Lattice** — Reference: Steve Reich. Result: repeated patterns drift out of phase and realign. Play: repeat an exact three-to-eight-note cell for several bars. Needs: phrase loop and fine time ratios; transport required.
18. **Broken Consort** — Reference: Caroline Shaw. Result: fragments pass, align, and break apart. Play: irregular two-to-five-note gestures separated by breaths. Needs: fragment capture and recombination.
19. **Kinetic Blocks** — Reference: Anna Meredith. Result: accented rhythmic blocks collide with bright repetitions. Play: staccato repeats, octave jumps, and exact accents. Needs: accent patterns and polymeter.
20. **Spectral Bloom** — References: Grisey, Murail, Saariaho. Result: harmony expands from overtone relationships. Play: long expressive notes with velocity, bend, vibrato, and register changes. Needs: partial-derived microtonal voicing.

## Jazz — 15

21. **Sectional Velvet** — Reference: Duke Ellington. Result: low, middle, and high groups act like contrasting sections. Play: lyrical medium-tempo line with dynamics and response space. Needs: arrangement subgroups and articulation.
22. **Stride Engine** — References: James P. Johnson, Fats Waller. Result: alternating bass and chord punches. Play: clear swinging melody or chord tones; do not provide a busy bass. Needs: bass/chord pattern lane.
23. **Sixth-Diminished Conveyor** — Reference: Barry Harris. Result: sixth chords alternate with diminished passing harmony. Play: connected bebop eighths, chromatic approaches, clear resolutions. Current engine baseline.
24. **Angular Cells** — Reference: Thelonious Monk. Result: tritones, clusters, displaced responses, and negative space. Play: short dry motifs with surprising accents and long rests. Needs: bounded clusters and displacement.
25. **Bebop Chase** — References: Charlie Parker, Dizzy Gillespie. Result: fast phrases are pursued by shortened delayed answers. Play: clean four-to-eight-note bursts with gaps. Current Free Imitation baseline.
26. **Rootless Glass** — Reference: Bill Evans. Result: smooth rootless ninth, eleventh, and thirteenth voicings. Play: ballad lines, sustained upper tones, gentle chromatic approaches. Needs: extensions, root omission, guide-tone targeting.
27. **Quartal Colossus** — Reference: McCoy Tyner. Result: forceful fourth stacks in Dorian or pentatonic space. Play: assertive modal riffs with rhythmic repetition. Current harmony baseline.
28. **Third-Cycle Labyrinth** — Reference: John Coltrane. Result: tonal centers rotate through major thirds. Play: repeat a compact motif while harmony changes beneath it. Needs: harmonic timeline and key cycle.
29. **Shout Counterpoint** — Reference: Charles Mingus. Result: bass ostinato, gospel response, and unruly counterline. Play: forceful two-bar blues calls followed by a full response opening. Needs: pattern, response, and multiple groups.
30. **Free Quartet** — Reference: Ornette Coleman. Result: independent contours and tonal centers. Play: exploratory lines with wide leaps and pauses to answer the ensemble. Needs: per-lane tonal centers and controlled independence.
31. **Side-Slip Matrix** — Reference: Herbie Hancock. Result: quartal harmony slips a semitone outside and returns. Play: simple inside-key modal motif; let the preset supply outside motion. Needs: timed side-slip timeline.
32. **Harmonic Maze** — Reference: Wayne Shorter. Result: ambiguous movement among diatonic, hexatonic, whole-tone, and octatonic regions. Play: sustained unresolved cells that avoid obvious cadences. Needs: weighted harmonic-state graph.
33. **Metric Labyrinth** — References: Tigran Hamasyan, Vijay Iyer. Result: percussive counterpoint across uneven groupings. Play: repeat an exact accented figure in 5, 7, or grouped 4/4. Needs: odd-meter pattern lanes; transport required.
34. **Chamber Sky** — References: Maria Schneider, Kenny Wheeler. Result: lyrical voices expand into airy ensemble harmony. Play: breath-shaped arcs, wide expressive intervals, soft attacks, long releases. Needs: groups, registers, and gradual entries.
35. **Elastic Counter-Groove** — References: Esperanza Spalding, Brad Mehldau, Robert Glasper. Result: syncopated harmony alternates with contrapuntal answers. Play: concise one- or two-bar riffs with deliberate holes. Needs: groove-aware response detection.

## Video game and interactive — 15

36. **Pixel Trio** — References: early console writing, Koji Kondo, Manami Matsumae. Result: melody, bass, and one counterline obey three-channel economy. Play: compact memorable single-note hooks with clear repeated rhythm. Current engine baseline.
37. **Fractured Crystal** — Reference: Disasterpeace, especially FEZ's constraint-driven linear writing. Result: a tiny motif fractures into limited-voice ostinatos and modal echoes. Play: repeat a three-to-five-note motif slowly, alter one note, and leave substantial air. Needs: bounded motif memory, sparse ostinato, and voice stealing.
38. **Summit Pulse** — Reference: Lena Raine. Result: intimate improvisatory melody grows into rhythmic upper layers. Play: begin softly with hesitant fragments and increase register, velocity, and repetition. Needs: intensity sensing and adaptive scenes.
39. **Adaptive Pilgrim** — References: Austin Wintory, Jessica Curry, Gareth Coker. Result: arrangement follows the emotional intensity arc. Play: isolated sustained notes, then longer/louder phrases, then withdraw. Needs: continuous intensity analysis and scene morphing.
40. **Ash and Ember** — References: Darren Korb, Ashley Barrett. Result: rough modal riffs receive acoustic, metallic, and vocal-like answers. Play: gritty short riffs, strong downbeats, slides, dramatic pauses. Needs: riff-response patterns; sound remains a separate suggestion.
41. **Blocks at Dusk** — Reference: C418. Result: sparse figures accumulate soft extended harmony. Play: two or three gentle notes repeated with small variations and long decay space. Needs: sparse-loop lane and probabilistic entry.
42. **Clockwork Stars** — Reference: Ben Prunty. Result: recurring motifs reappear across registers and counter-rhythms. Play: repeat a clean four-to-eight-note motif while preserving its rhythm. Needs: motif identity and recurrence tracking.
43. **Hollow Choir** — Reference: Christopher Larkin. Result: dark minor melody expands into distant chorale and counterline. Play: singable minor phrases, held destinations, long pauses. Current engine baseline; register groups improve it.
44. **Memory Weave** — Reference: Toby Fox. Result: two learned themes return transformed and combined. Play: teach two clearly distinct motifs, repeating each twice with a pause. Needs: named motif slots and combination.
45. **Neon Noir** — Reference: Shoji Meguro. Result: minor jazz-funk harmony with chromatic passing motion and rhythmic responses. Play: tight syncopated riffs, repeated notes, short chromatic runs. Needs: groove pattern, approaches, bass response.
46. **Forest Layer Unfold** — Reference: David Wise. Result: quiet modal material gains naturalistic layers as density rises. Play: begin sparse and pentatonic, then add repeats and higher notes gradually. Needs: adaptive layer system.
47. **Time-Cross Rhythm** — Reference: Yasunori Mitsuda. Result: compound-meter melody meets duple counter-rhythms. Play: repeat a six- or twelve-beat cell with strong internal accents. Needs: simultaneous meters; transport required.
48. **Crystal Chorale** — Reference: Nobuo Uematsu. Result: memorable melody grows into harmonic-minor chorale and octave answers. Play: clear four- or eight-bar phrases with held cadence notes. Current engine baseline.
49. **Kingdom Counterline** — Reference: Yoko Shimomura. Result: lyrical lead gains urgent inner lines and dramatic harmonic turns. Play: a memorable theme with expressive leaps, dynamics, and phrase-end rests. Needs: motif-aware counterlines and harmonic trajectory.
50. **Rust and Static** — Reference: Akira Yamaoka. Result: fragile fragments meet unstable clusters, shadows, and empty space. Play: isolated notes, slides, tritones, muted attacks, unpredictable pauses. Needs: bounded stochastic clusters and density safety.
