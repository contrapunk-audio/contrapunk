# Arrangement Ensemble Contracts

## Product promise

Every operational Arrangement preset must feel like a distinct playable ensemble or composite instrument, even through Contrapunk's fixed-sine Elixir renderer with Standard tuning, Slide off, and no external effects.

A preset is not complete because it demonstrates a harmony mode. It is complete when the player can recognize its relationship, timing, register, restraint, and development without seeing its name.

## Bank-wide contract

- **Simple entrance, advanced response:** a three-to-six-note monophonic gesture must produce a useful result immediately.
- **Player foreground:** input gain remains `1.0`; every generated role is quieter by default.
- **Density by design:** normally no more than three simultaneous notes. Sixth-Diminished Conveyor may use four because the block section is its identity. No preset may reach five.
- **One clear relationship:** every generated role has a distinct job—partner, answer, support, shadow, or composite resonance.
- **Behavior before quantity:** depth comes from contour, history, phrase boundaries, gaps, timing, register, voice leading, and bounded tails—not more voices.
- **Finite tails:** generated parts stop cleanly after the player's gesture; no unbounded accumulation.
- **Deterministic:** the same gesture and transport state produce the same result. Randomness is not a substitute for character.
- **Renderer-neutral identity:** optional Slide or sound suggestions may enhance a preset, but the preset must remain identifiable with Slide off and the fixed sine renderer.
- **Honest controls:** the compact player shows one short first gesture and any transport requirement. Setup retains complete editing controls.
- **Exact ownership:** every generated NoteOn has one stable role/slot owner and one matching NoteOff across panic, preset changes, and transport stop.

## Listening acceptance

For every preset:

1. Play the same eight-note phrase at two registers.
2. Repeat it with one longer rest and one held arrival.
3. Confirm the ensemble changes in the way its contract promises.
4. Confirm the player remains foreground and the peak density stays within the preset ceiling.
5. Release, stop transport, apply another preset, and confirm no old note or delayed event returns.
6. Perform a five-minute exploratory playtest. The preset must reveal more than its first cycle without requiring an instruction-heavy performance recipe.

No two presets may share the same combination of topology, entry timing, register shape, response rule, and phrase evolution.

## Minimal shared behaviors

Use existing harmony, counterpoint, canon, pattern, hold, mix, octave, and voice-leading controls first. Add only the smallest shared behavior a contract proves necessary:

- **Phrase-relative pattern pitch:** a pattern may anchor to the current key or the first pitch class of the current phrase while retaining its designed register.
- **Gap-aware role:** a pattern may skip or release its event while the player is holding a note, allowing a real answer instead of a continuous loop.
- **Bounded phrase reset:** silence closes the current pattern window and the next phrase chooses a fresh anchor.

Do not add adaptive scenes, probability, motif recognition, or a general preset scripting engine during the first pass.

## Ensemble contracts

### 01 — Cloister Organum

- **Identity:** cantor with one hovering organal partner.
- **Player role:** the chant-bearing lower voice.
- **Immediate signature:** one restrained fourth, fifth, or octave above the line.
- **Deep behavior:** the partner favors continuity and lets arrivals breathe; register changes alter openness without adding a third part.
- **Density ceiling:** 2.
- **Never:** a chord pad or florid imitation engine.

### 02 — Modal Linework

- **Identity:** three-part vocal consort moving around the player.
- **Player role:** the middle singer, not a trigger for a block chord.
- **Immediate signature:** two quiet independent lines find consonant positions around the melody.
- **Deep behavior:** history-aware voice leading and beat phase create changing inner motion across a phrase.
- **Density ceiling:** 3.
- **Never:** four equal close voices on every attack.

### 04 — Mensuration Web

- **Identity:** elastic clockwork trio whose parts experience time differently.
- **Player role:** source line and phrase clock.
- **Immediate signature:** two single-line followers enter separately at contrasting proportional rates.
- **Deep behavior:** phrase boundaries reset the web; a changed opening pitch or rhythm creates a recognizably changed weave.
- **Density ceiling:** 3.
- **Never:** player plus three simultaneous full-level copies.

### 07 — Stretto Engine

- **Identity:** compact fugal relay.
- **Player role:** subject leader.
- **Immediate signature:** two answers enter with contracting gaps at distinct pitch levels.
- **Deep behavior:** short subjects produce clear pursuit; longer or denser input causes the answers to yield rather than accumulate.
- **Density ceiling:** 3.
- **Never:** an indefinite four-line pile-up.

### 08 — Suspension Garland

- **Identity:** tension-and-resolution counter-singing duo.
- **Player role:** free melodic partner, not operator of a prescribed exercise.
- **Immediate signature:** the companion enters consonantly, holds across a compatible change, and resolves by step.
- **Deep behavior:** it waits for stable opportunities, rests after resolving, and may choose the open register opposite the player.
- **Density ceiling:** 2.
- **Never:** require one exact interval sequence merely to demonstrate Species IV.

### 12 — Planed Cathedral

- **Identity:** one resonant composite instrument whose body blooms outward.
- **Player role:** striker and phrase shaper.
- **Immediate signature:** a sparse whole-tone plane opens in stages rather than arriving as a uniform block.
- **Deep behavior:** register and held arrivals change the spacing and perceived weight while the total note count stays fixed.
- **Density ceiling:** 3.
- **Never:** become an independent choir or repetitive three-note preset demo.

### 14 — Color-Mode Windows

- **Identity:** crisp prismatic instrument that opens and closes modal color windows.
- **Player role:** selects and articulates each color.
- **Immediate signature:** two tones establish the window; one brief upper glint completes it only when space permits.
- **Deep behavior:** register and scale degree produce contrasting window shapes with clear silence between them.
- **Density ceiling:** 3.
- **Never:** four equal diminished tones on every attack.

### 23 — Sixth-Diminished Conveyor

- **Identity:** dry four-part jazz block section.
- **Player role:** lead voice of the section.
- **Immediate signature:** sixth-family and diminished passing blocks alternate under the line.
- **Deep behavior:** voice continuity and beat placement make passing motion feel directed toward arrivals.
- **Density ceiling:** 4—the sole default exception.
- **Never:** add a canon, pattern, or fifth voice to prove complexity.

### 25 — Bebop Chase

- **Identity:** two-horn trading duo.
- **Player role:** caller who leaves a response window.
- **Immediate signature:** one complete, quieter answer returns after the call.
- **Deep behavior:** call length and phrase boundary control the answer window; the partner never talks over a new call.
- **Density ceiling:** 2.
- **Never:** a permanent octave doubler.

### 27 — Quartal Colossus

- **Identity:** wide three-tier power instrument with the player inside its voicing.
- **Player role:** central physical voice.
- **Immediate signature:** dry fourth-derived support appears on both sides of the played register.
- **Deep behavior:** contour causes the outer tiers to move with contrasting weight while preserving open spacing.
- **Density ceiling:** 3.
- **Never:** four equal upward-stacked notes with the player always at the bottom.

### 36 — Pixel Trio

- **Identity:** lead, phrase-relative bass, and gap-filling counterpulse.
- **Player role:** hook author and conductor of available space.
- **Immediate signature:** the bass adopts the first pitch class of the phrase; a counterline answers the player's latest note only in holes.
- **Deep behavior:** source contour redirects each answer, silence closes the pattern window, and a new opening pitch moves the bass without changing the compact role economy.
- **Density ceiling:** 3, with two voices being the normal state.
- **Never:** two key-rooted loops that ignore the player's phrase.

### 43 — Hollow Choir

- **Identity:** distant three-part choir shadow.
- **Player role:** exposed solo line.
- **Immediate signature:** two soft lower voices settle beneath the melody with restrained continuity.
- **Deep behavior:** harmonic history darkens or releases the shadow across a phrase without adding a fourth voice.
- **Density ceiling:** 3.
- **Never:** a full-level forced SATB block on every note.

### 48 — Crystal Chorale

- **Identity:** music-box lead, harmonic halo, and delayed glass answer.
- **Player role:** immediate melodic foreground.
- **Immediate signature:** one quiet harmonic partner supports the note; one delayed single line answers later.
- **Deep behavior:** held arrivals let the halo settle while phrase gaps expose the answer.
- **Density ceiling:** 3.
- **Never:** four-part harmony plus echo, or any five-note peak.

## One-at-a-time rollout

Each preset is its own bounded slice:

1. Approve its contract and identify the smallest missing shared behavior.
2. Implement only that preset and genuinely shared support.
3. Add behavioral density, timing, and cleanup checks.
4. Run focused Rust/UI checks and the relevant cross-surface checks.
5. Perform a manual play/smoke pass.
6. Commit and push before starting the next preset.
7. Have the user play it; retain, tune, or revert before moving on.

First: **Pixel Trio**. Second: **Suspension Garland**. The remaining order is chosen only after those two establish the feel standard.
