# Phase 10.2 — Preset 14 “Color-Mode Windows”
## Role A: independent history and primary-source report (Olivier Messiaen)

**Research role:** artist/style historian and primary-source researcher  
**Preset claim under review:** “rotating whole-tone, octatonic, and augmented colors,” for sparse, strongly articulated modal notes.  
**Scope decision:** The defensible reference is Messiaen’s **early-to-middle harmonic language as codified retrospectively in _Technique de mon langage musical_ (1944)**, with later works used to show how that vocabulary was retained, recombined, and exceeded. It is not a whole-career simulator.

**Bottom line:** A bounded Messiaen reference can use short, clearly differentiated windows drawn chiefly from Modes 2 and 3, with tonal/common-tone anchoring, characteristic chords, register, rhythm, resonance, and contrast doing as much identity work as the pitch collection. The catalog wording is historically unsafe if “augmented” means the common six-note augmented/hexatonic scale, or if “rotating” means mechanically cycling scale roots: Messiaen’s Mode 3 is a **nine-note** collection, and he explicitly regarded isolated Mode 1 (whole tone) as exhausted by Debussy and Dukas. Whole-tone → octatonic → augmented-scale rotation is therefore rejected as a model of his style.

## 1. Scope and corpus

### Principal primary/documentary corpus

1. **Olivier Messiaen, _La Technique de mon langage musical_, 2 vols. (Alphonse Leduc, 1944; John Satterfield English translation, 1956), especially chapters I, V, XIII–XIX and musical Examples 312–357.** This is the controlling primary source. It is a retrospective account of techniques developed in works up to the early 1940s, not a forecast of Messiaen’s later language. Chapter XVI defines seven modes of limited transposition and demonstrates modal chord successions, contrary motion, complete-mode sonorities, cadence formulas, pedal groups, resonance notes, and changes between transpositions; chapters XVII–XIX relate them to tonality, modulation, and polymodality. [Messiaen, _The Technique of My Musical Language_ (scan)](https://monoskop.org/images/5/50/Messiaen_Olivier_The_Technique_of_My_Musical_Language.pdf) The Philharmonie de Paris catalog confirms the original 1944 Leduc publication and two-volume text/examples format. [Philharmonie catalog record](https://mediatheque.philharmoniedeparis.fr/mediatheque/doc/ALOES/0077526/technique-de-mon-langage-musical-texte-avec-exemples-musicaux-olivier-messiaen)
2. **_Préludes_ for piano (1928–29), especially “La colombe,” and the motet _O sacrum convivium!_ (1937).** These early comparators show modal color emerging within audible tonal/triadic behavior rather than replacing it wholesale. A score-based dissertation finds all three early specimens it studies—including “La colombe” and _O sacrum_—combine tonal behavior with foreground harmonic sonority. [Lee, _Sonority and Linear Structure in Three Early Works of Olivier Messiaen_](https://digitalcommons.du.edu/etd/1207)
3. **_La Nativité du Seigneur_ (1935), especially the composer’s preface and “La Vierge et l’Enfant” / “Les Bergers.”** The preface is the first public tabulation of the modes and calls the modes, enlarged pedals, added dot, progressively augmented/diminished rhythms, and Greek/Hindu rhythmic materials principal technical means. It already demonstrates that the modes belong to a conjunction, not a scale-only style. [Konewko, score/preface-based study](https://epublications.marquette.edu/comm_fac/594) _Technique_ later points to Mode 2’s cadence in “La Vierge et l’Enfant” and to Modes 5/6 in these organ movements.
4. **_Quatuor pour la fin du Temps_ (1940–41), especially “Liturgie de cristal,” “Louange à l’Éternité de Jésus,” and “Danse de la fureur.”** This wartime chamber work tests the 1944 vocabulary in distinct textures: independent cycles and birdsong, extremely slow tonal/modal praise, and rhythmically exact unison. Its identity cannot be recovered by scale choice alone. Anthony Pople’s Cambridge handbook is the principal scholarly work-specific comparator; Benitez cites Pople’s careful suggestion that Mode 3 can be related analytically to older hexatonic practice while noting there is no evidence Messiaen consciously derived it that way. [Benitez, note 19](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.02.8.2/mto.02.8.2.benitez.html)
5. **_Visions de l’Amen_ (1943) and _Vingt Regards sur l’Enfant-Jésus_ (1944).** These are the immediate mature context of _Technique_: cyclic themes, massive chordal blocks, resonance, added notes, birdsong, irregular/nonretrogradable rhythm, theological symbols, and tonal affirmations coexist with the modes. The dates and place of these works among the major 1940s scores are institutionally confirmed by the Philharmonie. [Philharmonie program document](https://edutheque.philharmoniedeparis.fr/player.aspx?id=0075918)
6. **Messiaen, _Conférence de Notre-Dame_ (1977), _Musique et couleurs_ interviews with Claude Samuel (1986), and the posthumous _Traité de rythme, de couleur, et d’ornithologie_ (written 1949–92; published 1994–2002).** These later primary sources clarify that his colored hearing responded principally to **chords, not isolated notes**, and give color associations and later harmonic procedures absent from the 1944 book. Benitez provides exact bibliographic references and connects the claims to score passages. [Benitez, paragraphs 1.1–1.6 and notes 1–4](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.02.8.2/mto.02.8.2.benitez.html)
7. **_Catalogue d’oiseaux_ (1956–58), _Chronochromie_ (1959–60), _Couleurs de la cité céleste_ (1963), _Des canyons aux étoiles…_ (1971–74), and _Saint François d’Assise_ (1975–83).** These middle/late comparators prevent the 1944 codification from becoming a false lifetime summary. _Catalogue_ foregrounds transformed birdsong and landscape; later orchestral works intensify timbre, color labels, special chords, layers, and complementary/contrasting pitch fields. In _Saint François_, Modes 2 and 3 remain active, but within tonal backgrounds, birdsong, non-modal invented chords, resonance, registral fields, and instrumental-color layers. Benitez gives rehearsal-specific evidence below. [Benitez 2002](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.02.8.2/mto.02.8.2.benitez.html)
8. **Late boundary: _Petites Esquisses d’oiseaux_ (1985–87), _Un vitrail et des oiseaux_ (1986), and _Éclairs sur l’Au-Delà…_ (1987–91).** These show continued religious, avian, coloristic, and harmonic concerns after _Saint François_, but also a distinct late economy and a vocabulary well beyond _Technique_. IRCAM confirms the dates and instrumentation of _Petites Esquisses_. [IRCAM work record](https://brahms.ircam.fr/fr/works/work/10606/) Dingle’s study specifically argues for a discernible late change across the seven completed post-opera works. [Dingle, _Understated Charm_ record and thesis](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/10233/)

### What this report does not claim

It does not treat every use of Modes 2 or 3 as interchangeable, reproduce Messiaen’s personal color perceptions, or infer a fixed algorithm from poetic/theological language. It does not use birdsong, serialism, organ registration, orchestral timbre, or theology as decorative “extras”; each can determine a work’s actual identity and therefore marks the limit of a scale-centered preset.

## 2. Historical and aesthetic context

Messiaen’s project joined Catholic theology, color hearing, acoustical resonance, birdsong, plainchant contour, Greek meters, rhythmic materials taken through French scholarship on Indian _deçî-tâlas_, and a modern French/Russian harmonic inheritance. The governing phrase in _Technique_ is the **“charm of impossibilities”**: a mode reaches an identity-producing limit because transposition eventually reproduces the same pitch classes; a nonretrogradable rhythm similarly remains the same backward. The symmetry is aesthetically meaningful because it creates closure, suspension, and what Messiaen hears as several tonal atmospheres at once—not because symmetry itself automatically sounds like Messiaen. [Modern companion, Chapter XVI theory](https://music-comp.github.io/tmml-mc/part2/03-harmony/ch16-theory.html)

The modes are explicitly twelve-tone equal-tempered pitch collections built from repeated interval groups. Messiaen distinguishes them from Indian, Chinese, ancient Greek, and plainchant modal systems, which do not derive their identity from limited transposability. Marketing them as generic “world modes” would therefore contradict his own definition. [_Technique_, ch. XVI; companion summary](https://music-comp.github.io/tmml-mc/part2/03-harmony/ch16-theory.html)

The modes also preserve contact with tonality. _Technique_ says they can inhabit the atmosphere of several tonalities and chapters XVII–XIX demonstrate modal transposition, relation to major tonality, and superposition. Later practice confirms this is not merely theoretical: in the Angel’s discourse in _Saint François_, an A-major background is colored by selected transpositions of Modes 2 and 3 that contain A-major triads; the modes intensify rather than erase the center. [Benitez, §§5.2–5.7](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.02.8.2/mto.02.8.2.benitez.html)

## 3. The three catalog-relevant modes: facts that must be explicit

### Mode 1 — whole-tone, six notes, two distinct transpositions

- Pitch-class form from C: **C–D–E–F♯–G♯–B♭**, interval pattern 2–2–2–2–2–2; set class 6-35.
- It has **two distinct pitch-class transpositions**; the next semitone shift returns to one of those two collections after the appropriate cycle.
- Crucially, Messiaen says Debussy used it extensively and Dukas remarkably; he finds it exhausted and avoids it except when **concealed in a superposition**. It is therefore historically wrong to make whole-tone the default Messiaen identifier. [_Technique_, ch. XVI; companion Mode 1 summary](https://music-comp.github.io/tmml-mc/part2/03-harmony/ch16-mode1.html)
- Late exception does not overturn the rule: _Saint François_, scene 5, R89:1–16 uses **both** Mode-1 collections as two strands inside an eight-scheme, tonally centered, chromatically saturated pitch-timbre design. This is a sophisticated conjunction, not an isolated whole-tone wash. [Benitez, §§6.3–6.10](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.02.8.2/mto.02.8.2.benitez.html)

### Mode 2 — octatonic, eight notes, three distinct transpositions

- From C in Messiaen’s first transposition: **C–C♯–D♯–E–F♯–G–A–B♭**, alternating semitone–tone; set class 8-28.
- It has **three distinct transpositions**. Starting on another scale degree reverses the surface order to tone–semitone but does **not** create a new pitch-class collection.
- _Technique_ Examples **312–328** show the three transpositions, repetition at the fourth, parallel and contrary chord successions, a characteristic chord, complete-mode sonority, cadence formulas, and passages that change modal transposition. This evidence rejects “play the scale up and down” as the primary compositional behavior. [Companion Mode 2 summary](https://music-comp.github.io/tmml-mc/part2/03-harmony/ch16-mode2.html)
- Messiaen openly acknowledges predecessors: traces in Rimsky-Korsakov’s _Sadko_, more conscious use by Scriabin, and transient use by Ravel and Stravinsky. Thus octatonicism is **shared vocabulary**, not his signature by itself.

### Mode 3 — nine notes, four distinct transpositions

- From C in Messiaen’s first transposition: **C–D–E♭–E–F♯–G–A♭–B♭–B**, interval pattern tone–semitone–semitone repeated three times; set class 9-12.
- It contains **nine notes** and has **four distinct transpositions**. The fifth chromatic transposition reproduces the first collection. Beginning on another member only rotates the interval ordering inside the same collection.
- _Technique_ Examples **329–340** demonstrate four transpositions, parallel succession, contrary motion, a characteristic chord, all-nine-note sonority, cadence formulas, a repeating pedal-group chord cluster, resonance notes foreign to the mode, and movement among transpositions. [Companion Mode 3 summary](https://music-comp.github.io/tmml-mc/part2/03-harmony/ch16-mode3.html)
- Mode 3 is **not** the common six-note “augmented scale” or hexatonic collection. Scholars can relate it to hexatonic/augmented-triad systems—Pople calls it a possible extension of older hexatonic practice—but that analytical relationship does not make the two collections identical, and Pople finds no evidence that Messiaen consciously derived Mode 3 from that tradition. [Benitez, note 19](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.02.8.2/mto.02.8.2.benitez.html)

## 4. Career/style evolution and selected period

### Early formation, c. 1928–35

The _Préludes_ already show the young composer moving beyond Debussy and Ravel while retaining their resonance, pedals, chordal color, and modal resources. The modes emerge alongside tonal centricity and triadic sonority; they are not yet the whole language. _La Nativité_ is the decisive early public synthesis: its preface names the modes but places them beside added values/dots, enlarged pedals, rhythmic growth/decay, Greek and Hindu rhythmic materials, organ color, and theology. This is the first suitable boundary for a preset reference.

### Codified mature early language, c. 1935–48 — **primary preset period**

From _La Nativité_ through the _Quatuor_, _Visions de l’Amen_, and _Vingt Regards_, Messiaen coordinates Modes 2 and 3 especially with tonal poles, characteristic chords, resonance/added notes, pedals, cyclic themes, stark register, irregular or nonretrogradable rhythm, very slow contemplation, violent dance, birdsong, and theological form. _Technique_ (1944) codifies this already-developed practice and cites his own works repeatedly. It should be read as a historically situated self-analysis, not as a neutral scale manual.

This period best fits “Color-Mode Windows” because the modes are explicit, audible, and documented, while a bounded digital harmonizer can at least suggest collection changes, common tones, characteristic verticals, and articulation. Even here, the honest referent is **modal-harmonic color from the 1935–44 language**, not “Messiaen in general.”

### Experimental/ornithological expansion, c. 1949–69

The postwar “experimental” works—_Mode de valeurs et d’intensités_ (1949), _Livre d’orgue_ (1951), and related pieces—systematize parameters and engage serial procedures; the often-told story that Messiaen simply became and then ceased being a serialist is disputed by scholarship. [Reconsidering Messiaen as Serialist](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1468-2249.2011.00293.x) From _Réveil des oiseaux_ (1953) and _Oiseaux exotiques_ (1955–56) through _Catalogue d’oiseaux_, birdsong and landscape become structural protagonists. IRCAM dates _Réveil_ to 1953. [IRCAM](https://brahms.ircam.fr/fr/works/work/10615/)

Modes persist, but increasingly sit beside permutations, twelve-tone fields, highly transformed birdsong, special chords, and explicit sound-color projects such as _Chronochromie_ and _Couleurs de la cité céleste_. A simple modal preset should not claim this ornithological/serial middle period.

### Monumental color-timbre synthesis, c. 1970–83

_Des canyons aux étoiles…_ and _Saint François_ expand instrumental effects, registral fields, percussion, birdsong, and layered pitch-timbre. Benitez reports Messiaen regarded _Saint François_ as containing all his harmonic procedures and chord colors. Modes 2 and 3 still matter, but they are foreground colors within tonal backgrounds or one layer among non-modal invented chords and complementary aggregates. [Benitez, §§1.4–1.8, 3.1–3.5](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.02.8.2/mto.02.8.2.benitez.html)

### Late economy and retrospection, 1984–91

The seven completed works after _Saint François_ include miniatures and the vast _Éclairs sur l’Au-Delà…_. Dingle identifies a discernible late stylistic change rather than mere repetition of the 1944 system. Persistent concerns—Catholic revelation, birds, color, resonance, juxtaposed blocks—remain, but textures, scale, and technical selection change. [Dingle](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/10233/) The late period is evidence of continuity-through-renewal, not permission to collapse fifty years into three scales.

## 5. Persistent traits versus period/project traits

### Persistent or broadly recurring traits

1. **Harmony as color, but color belongs chiefly to sonorities and contexts.** Messiaen’s 1977 statement locates his colored hearing in chords rather than isolated pitches. Register, doubling, added notes, and timbre can change the perceived color. [Benitez, note 1](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.02.8.2/mto.02.8.2.benitez.html) **Confidence: high.**
2. **Plural pitch organization.** Modal collections, major triads/tonal poles, added-note chords, resonance-derived sonorities, birdsong harmonization, and later invented chords coexist. **High.**
3. **Juxtaposition and contrast.** Blocks or layers gain identity by contrast—stillness/action, unison/chordal mass, tonal center/modal foreground, different pitch-timbres—rather than by smooth root-cycle rotation alone. _Saint François_ makes this explicit through simultaneous-contrast designs. **High.**
4. **Symmetry linked across parameters.** Limited-transposition modes and nonretrogradable rhythms are parallel forms of “impossibility”; pitch symmetry without rhythmic/formal counterpart captures only one component. **High for the 1944 conception; medium as a rule for every later passage.**
5. **Tonal focus can survive chromatic richness.** A major or C major may function as center/background while modal or non-modal sonorities color it. **High.**
6. **Renewal through new source domains.** The early modal-rhythmic synthesis, postwar parameter experiments, birdsong works, and late timbral synthesis show continuous expansion rather than one frozen technique. **High.**

### Traits specific to the 1935–44 target

- Particularly explicit foreground use of Modes 2 and 3, often with characteristic chords, cadential formulas, pedals, added/resonance notes, parallel or contrary chord motion, and shifts between modal transpositions.
- Slow ecstatic chordal contemplation and forceful, sharply articulated rhythmic blocks as contrasting expressive states.
- Organ/piano/chamber textures whose registral placement and resonance strongly condition the harmony.
- The 1944 terminology’s close coupling of harmonic and rhythmic symmetry.

### Work- or project-specific traits, not defaults

- _Quatuor_: prison-camp circumstances, its unique instrumentation, movement-specific cycles/unison, and end-of-time theology.
- _Vingt Regards_: twenty-movement piano architecture and named cyclic theological themes.
- _Mode de valeurs_: parameter-assignment experiment; not a universal later style.
- _Catalogue d’oiseaux_: species/place/time-specific birdsong-landscape dramaturgy.
- _Couleurs de la cité céleste_: explicit color labels and its particular theological/orchestral scheme.
- _Saint François_: operatic drama, enormous orchestra, three Ondes Martenot, spatial/timbral layers, and complex invented chords.
- _Éclairs_: late monumental retrospection; not interchangeable with the 1944 piano/organ idiom.

## 6. Relationship to peers and tradition

### Shared vocabulary

- **Mode 1 / whole tone:** strongly associated before Messiaen with Debussy and Dukas; Messiaen himself says so and generally avoids it exposed.
- **Mode 2 / octatonic:** nineteenth-/early-twentieth-century Russian and French precedent includes Rimsky-Korsakov, Scriabin, Ravel, and Stravinsky. Historical octatonic scholarship likewise treats it as a broad lineage, not proprietary vocabulary. [Taruskin, “Catching Up with Rimsky-Korsakov”](https://doi.org/10.1525/mts.2011.33.2.169)
- **Mode 3 relationships:** augmented triads and hexatonic systems occur in Liszt, Wagner, and Brahms; this is an analytical/historical neighborhood, not identity with Messiaen’s nine-note mode.
- **Added-note/resonant harmony and parallel chords:** Debussy is a major source. Recent scholarship argues Messiaen’s conscious recomposition of Debussy is career-long and more central than older originality narratives allowed. [Fallon, “Messiaen the Borrower”](https://doi.org/10.1525/jams.2016.69.3.699)
- **Rhythmic and modal sources:** plainchant, Greek meter, Stravinsky, and Indian rhythmic scholarship all contribute materials, though Messiaen transforms and reframes them within a Catholic/coloristic system.

### Messiaen’s characteristic conjunction (not exclusive ownership)

What is unusually characteristic is the **coordination** of a chosen modal transposition with particular chordal subsets, added/resonance notes, tonal poles, registral and instrumental color, symmetrical or additive rhythm, theological/dramatic symbolism, birdsong, and sharply contrasted blocks or layers. Benitez’s late evidence is exact: in _Saint François_, scene 3, R62:1–12 and R69:2–18, Modes 2/3 color an A-major background; scene 5, R89:1–16 combines modal ostinatos, birds, both whole-tone collections, Ondes glissandi, and C-major centricity. [Benitez, §§5–6](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.02.8.2/mto.02.8.2.benitez.html)

A bare octatonic melody is shared modernist vocabulary. A mechanically transposed whole-tone scale is more likely to evoke generic Debussy shorthand. A six-note augmented scale is not Messiaen’s Mode 3. Identity lies in conjunction and temporal contrast.

## 7. Common caricatures to avoid

1. **“Messiaen equals symmetric scales.” Reject.** The modes are one family within a language of tonality, special chords, resonance, rhythm, birdsong, color, timbre, and theology.
2. **“Rotate whole-tone, octatonic, and augmented scales.” Reject as the whole-style model.** Mode 1 was normally avoided in exposure; Mode 3 has nine notes, not six; modal changes in the treatise use common tones, chord types, cadences, pedals, contrary/parallel motion, and changes of transposition—not a generic root carousel.
3. **“Mode 3 = augmented scale.” Reject.** They are distinct pitch-class collections. “Augmented color” is acceptable only if product copy defines a bounded Mode-3-derived color, not the common hexatonic scale.
4. **“Any octatonic passage is Messiaen.” Reject.** Rimsky-Korsakov, Scriabin, Stravinsky, Ravel, Bartók, and jazz diminished-scale practice share the collection.
5. **“Color means assign one RGB hue to each note or scale.” Reject.** Messiaen’s reports concern complex inward color phenomena triggered chiefly by chords; even he said a score page could evoke too many colors for literal stage reproduction. [Benitez, note 53](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.02.8.2/mto.02.8.2.benitez.html)
6. **“Symmetry means static sameness.” Reject.** He uses transposition changes, cadence formulas, opposing layers, rhythmic asymmetry, register, accumulation, and sudden contrasts to create motion.
7. **“Nonfunctional means atonal.” Reject.** Modal fields can polarize major triads and tonal centers; _Saint François_ supplies explicit A- and C-major examples.
8. **“More chromatic notes make a more authentic color.” Reject.** Mode 3 already has nine notes, and late textures can reach aggregate saturation, but identity depends on selection, voicing, register, layer, and contrast. Undifferentiated saturation is mud, not stained glass.
9. **“Birdsong is random ornament.” Reject.** It became a researched, transformed, species- and landscape-linked compositional practice, especially after 1950.
10. **“Religious titles are optional branding.” Reject.** Theology shapes form, symbols, time, and affect. A secular preset may responsibly abstract a technical conjunction, but must not claim to reproduce the works’ full meaning.

### Historically honest bounded thesis

A sparse source line is placed for a limited span inside **one stable transposition of Mode 2 or genuine nine-note Mode 3**, voiced to retain a focal pitch, common tone, pedal, or triadic implication. The next window changes collection or transposition only after enough time/space for contrast to register. Character comes from characteristic vertical subsets, resonance/register, articulation, and contrast. A short concealed/subordinate Mode-1 episode may provide historical contrast, but exposed whole-tone cycling is not the identity.

### What a Contrapunk preset may and may not claim

**Plausibly representable, subject to capability verification:** collection-constrained harmony; Mode 2 and nine-note Mode 3 pitch membership; stable transposition for a bounded phrase/window; common-tone or pedal retention; sparse-to-denser contrasted chord states; parallel or contrary voice motion; tonal/focal-note anchoring; respect for source rests.

**Likely outside a baseline symmetric-scale harmonizer:** Messiaen’s chord-specific synesthetic colors; authentic organ registration/orchestration; independent pitch-timbre layers; birdsong transcription and landscape form; nonretrogradable/additive rhythmic processes; special non-modal chords; theological symbolism; phrase-aware transition among formal blocks; late aggregate/complement designs.

Therefore the honest product claim is a **bounded early/middle Messiaen-informed modal-harmonic window**, not imitation, not “Messiaen mode,” and not a representation of _Catalogue_, _Saint François_, or _Éclairs_. No final configuration is proposed here.

## 9. Confidence, disagreements, and gaps

### Confidence by claim

- **High:** Mode 1 = six-note whole-tone/two transpositions; Mode 2 = eight-note octatonic/three; Mode 3 = nine-note/four; the interval patterns and _Technique_ example ranges; Messiaen’s stated reluctance to use exposed Mode 1.
- **High:** _Technique_ is a 1944 retrospective codification and does not encompass the later total language; Modes 2 and 3 remain present in late works alongside non-modal chords, tonal centers, birdsong, and layered timbre.
- **High:** octatonic and whole-tone collections predate and exceed Messiaen; bare collection membership is not stylistic ownership.
- **High–medium:** 1935–44 is the best operational scope for this preset. It is a product-historical judgment grounded in source fit, not a period label imposed by Messiaen.
- **Medium:** the exact degree to which every early tonal hearing should dominate over modal/set hearing; analytical methods differ by passage.
- **Medium:** generalized claims about late economy. Dingle argues a post-_Saint François_ change, but a detailed movement-by-movement late study was beyond this retrieval.
- **Low/not claimed:** reproduction of Messiaen’s personal colors, theology, birdsong, rhythm, or orchestral identity from scale selection; exact universal color names for a generated chord.

### Live disagreements / necessary cautions

1. **Tonal versus set/modal analysis.** Early and late passages can support both tonal centricity and collection-based readings. The safest implementation principle is observable: retain focal pitches/triads and modal constraints rather than declaring the music either simply tonal or atonal.
2. **Mode 3 and hexatonic ancestry.** Pople’s “extension” relationship is analytically useful, but evidence of Messiaen’s conscious historical derivation is absent. Do not rename Mode 3 “augmented scale.”
3. **Serial interlude versus continuing serial thought.** The conventional 1949–52 episode narrative has been challenged by research finding serial procedures later. This dispute does not change the bounded preset decision; it reinforces that the post-1944 language cannot be reduced to the modes.
4. **Color correlation.** Messiaen’s color hearing is well documented, but literal predictive mappings remain personal and analytically contested. Benitez recommends studying interaction/contrast rather than treating named hues as a codebook.
5. **Borrowing versus originality.** Messiaen openly credits predecessors for Modes 1/2, while scholarship increasingly shows deep Debussy recomposition. His distinctiveness is synthetic/conjunctive, not invention of every component.
