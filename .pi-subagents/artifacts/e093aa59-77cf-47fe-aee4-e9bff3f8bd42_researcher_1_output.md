# Quartal Colossus — Music-Theory and Temporal-Behavior Research

**Phase:** 10.2, preset 27  
**Reference:** McCoy Tyner  
**Role:** Independent music-theory / temporal-behavior report  
**Decision:** **Implementable now only as a bounded, player-shaped Dorian fourth-stack study.** It must not claim to generate Tyner-style comping, riffs, phrasing, bass movement, chromatic resolution, or ensemble interaction.

## Summary

A defensible current preset can transform each clean, in-collection monophonic attack into a deterministic four-note Dorian stack generated by repeated movement of three scale indices (`DiatonicFourths`, four voices, source in the bottom slot). That is a narrow reference to the open fourth-based color prominent in selected Tyner performances, not a model of his style: scholarship and Tyner's own explanations show that his 1960s language also retained bebop syntax, dominant-chord movement, pedals, independent melodic/harmonic layers, resolution, and active rhythm.[^linna][^tepfer][^satterthwaite]

The engine can preserve NoteOn/NoteOff correspondence for one non-overlapping source note at a time. It cannot enforce the needed input collection, create rhythmic cells or accents, infer phrases, separate an independent bass, or safely promise polyphonic/retrigger ownership; those behaviors must remain a performer contract or a capability gap.

## 1. Scope and bounded corpus

### Selected period and recordings

This report references a **bounded 1960–67 trajectory**, not Tyner's whole career:

1. **Early Coltrane-quartet modal language (1960–62):** “Equinox,” “Village Blues,” “My Favorite Things,” and especially “Liberia,” where Sami Linna identifies an early vamp with voicings distinctly built from fourths. Linna also shows that pedals, Dorian color, parallel movement, blues practice, and functional motion coexist in this repertory.[^linna]
2. **Mature classic-quartet / immediate post-quartet sound (1964–67):** *A Love Supreme* (“Acknowledgement,” “Resolution”) and *The Real McCoy* (“Passion Dance”). Fuentes Valverde's doctoral-project abstract selects “Resolution” (1965) and “Passion Dance” (1967) as pivotal examples of Tyner's modern approach.[^fuentes]
3. **Counterexample corpus (1962–63):** Gregory Satterthwaite's study explicitly argues that early Tyner balances bebop syntax with pentatonic melody and quartal harmony; it warns against making fourths and pentatonics the whole account.[^satterthwaite]
4. **Primary testimony:** Tyner describes fourth-based sounds as a personal language developed through playing, but also describes open voicing as a means to move among connected sounds and emphasizes how material resolves. He separately describes form as melody, embellishment, a beginning/middle/repeat, and an intended harmonic concept.[^tepfer]

### Why this scope

The catalog promises “forceful fourth stacks in Dorian or pentatonic space.” The selected corpus supports fourth-based, modal/open color, emphatic physical attack, repeated material, and player-shaped development. It does **not** support reducing Tyner to a scale/chord recipe. Linna's 29 complete and 34 partial solo transcriptions conclude that Tyner continually added elements during the 1960s, often combining scale-based ideas with parallel dominant chords, functional progressions, and independence between left-hand harmony and right-hand melody.[^linna]

## 2. Evidence-led theory translation

### 2.1 Tonal center, modality, collection, chromaticism, tuning

- **Center:** one persistent user-selected tonic; the arrangement preset must preserve tonic under the Phase 10.2 contract. The preset therefore means “Dorian on the current tonic,” not a fixed key.
- **Primary collection:** heptatonic Dorian, degrees `1 2 ♭3 4 5 6 ♭7`, semitone offsets `[0,2,3,5,7,9,10]`. Linna treats the characteristic natural 6 and its half-step approach from ♭7 as important to Dorian motion, while warning that Tyner often derives motion from chordal/II–V thinking rather than merely running a modal scale.[^linna]
- **Pentatonic relation:** pentatonic cells are historically relevant, but **MinorPentatonic is not suitable for the current `DiatonicFourths` implementation**. In a five-note collection, advancing three scale indices does not reliably mean a fourth (from degree 1 it reaches degree 5, a perfect fifth). Pentatonic must remain input guidance inside Dorian—not the configured scale—unless an explicit semitone/degree stack capability is added.
- **Chromaticism:** selected Tyner practice permits chromatic or dominant-derived tension only when it moves or resolves. Tyner explicitly says open voicing permits movement, that sounds connect, and that what matters is how notes resolve.[^tepfer] The baseline preset cannot model that context, so chromatic source attacks are outside its acceptance corpus.
- **Tuning:** twelve-tone equal-tempered MIDI only. No claim is made about piano tuning, acoustic resonance, or historical/performer-specific intonation.

### 2.2 Exact fourth-stack vocabulary

For an in-scale source degree `d`, current `DiatonicFourths` chains each new voice from the previous voice by **+3 scale indices**. With four voices and source at the bottom, the abstract stack is:

`[d, d+3, d+6, d+9]` (indices modulo seven, with octave continuation).

In Dorian this yields the following adjacent-semitone vectors:

| Input degree | Four-note degrees, low→high | Adjacent semitones | Relative semitone vector | Acceptance |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | `1–4–♭7–♭3'` | `5,5,5` | `[0,5,10,15]` | exact P4 stack |
| 2 | `2–5–1'–4'` | `5,5,5` | `[0,5,10,15]` | exact P4 stack |
| ♭3 | `♭3–6–2'–5'` | `6,5,5` | `[0,6,11,16]` | contains A4 |
| 4 | `4–♭7–♭3'–6'` | `5,5,6` | `[0,5,10,16]` | contains A4 |
| 5 | `5–1'–4'–♭7'` | `5,5,5` | `[0,5,10,15]` | exact P4 stack |
| 6 | `6–2'–5'–1''` | `5,5,5` | `[0,5,10,15]` | exact P4 stack |
| ♭7 | `♭7–♭3'–6'–2''` | `5,6,5` | `[0,5,11,16]` | contains A4 |

Thus `Dorian + DiatonicFourths` **can produce a defensible fixed all-perfect-fourth stack only for source degrees 1, 2, 5, and 6**. It produces a deterministic *diatonic fourth-derived* stack on all seven degrees, but degrees ♭3, 4, and ♭7 each include one augmented fourth. Product copy must say “Dorian fourth-derived stacks,” not “every note becomes identical stacked perfect fourths,” unless Play guidance limits the source cell to `1,2,5,6`.

### 2.3 Voicing, position, register, doubling, tension

- **Recommended current voicing:** four total voices; `voicePosition: 3`; `octaveMode: None`; voice leading off. The source note is the lowest arrangement slot and three chained voices are generated above it.
- **Spacing:** compact open stack over 15–16 semitones. Do not use `Spread`: it adds index-scaled octave displacement and ceases to be the researched compact quartal structure.
- **Inversion:** no inversion. Changing `voicePosition` makes separate upward/downward chains around the source; that is not equivalent to inverting one fixed four-note voicing.
- **Doubling:** none. `Mirror` duplicates harmony voices at ±octaves and falsely inflates density.
- **Register:** acceptance range approximately MIDI 48–84 (C3–C6), with the source normally around C3–C5. This avoids extreme-register fallback and leaves the generated top within a plausible keyboard field.
- **Tension:** the all-P4 vector is intentionally ambiguous/open. The one-A4 variants are legitimate diatonic tensions but should be used as color, not described as a fixed P4 identity. No automatic resolution follows.

### 2.4 Bass separation and layer roles

Tyner's practice cannot be represented honestly as a single undifferentiated four-note block. Linna describes pedals/focal bass pitches, moving harmony over them, and independence between left-hand harmony and right-hand melody as central to tension and release.[^linna] Tyner likewise describes setting a root, keeping the voicing open, moving the bass, and resolving connected sounds.[^tepfer]

Current bounded role assignment is therefore:

- **source:** lowest note of the generated stack, not an autonomous bass line;
- **generated voices:** three simultaneous upper chord tones;
- **no independent melody layer, bass pedal, bass ostinato, or right/left-hand independence.**

The core engine has `suppress_bass_register` and a threshold, but these fields are absent from `ArrangementHarmonyConfig`; this preset cannot serialize or apply them. `voicePosition: 3` prevents generated voices below the player, but it does not create bass separation in the historical sense.

### 2.5 Melodic contour, cells, repetition, and transformation

The preset must not generate material. The performer supplies a compact cell:

- two to four source attacks;
- preferably degrees `1,2,5,6` for exact-P4 output;
- mostly repeated notes, steps, fourths, or fifths;
- one controlled transposition or one-note mutation on repetition;
- no claim that these notes reproduce a Tyner phrase.

Tyner's own description supports developing a germ through embellishment and returning to an opening part, but does not justify hard-coding a generic “Tyner pattern.”[^tepfer] Satterthwaite's conclusion that bebop syntax and quartal/pentatonic devices are blended further rules out generating context-free pentatonic clichés.[^satterthwaite]

### 2.6 Rhythm, meter, accents, articulation, density, and silence

These are **performance constraints**, not generated behaviors:

- **Tempo:** 88–144 BPM; acceptance default 112 BPM. Transport optional because the harmony mapping is attack-synchronous and beat-independent.
- **Meter:** 4/4 for the acceptance exercise only. No engine meter logic is used.
- **Subdivision:** quarters and eighths; optional eighth-note triplet, but no automatic swing.
- **Cell:** 2–4 attacks within one or two beats, followed by at least one beat of rest.
- **Accent:** one clear first or offbeat attack per cell; velocity variation comes solely from the player. The engine does not create accents.
- **Articulation:** firm, short-tenuto/non-legato attacks; fully release before repeating the same MIDI pitch or starting the next source attack for lifecycle safety.
- **Density:** exactly one physical source note at a time and one resulting four-note block at a time; 2–4 blocks per clause, 2–3 clauses per phrase.
- **Silence:** at least one beat between clauses and one full bar after a 4–8 bar phrase. Silence is structural breathing and lifecycle verification, not a lane-generated event.
- **Dynamics:** player-shaped `medium → strong peak → medium/stop`; no automatic crescendo.

Tyner described the classic quartet's intensity as emerging from mutual listening rather than loudness for its own sake, with Elvin Jones actively reducing dynamics when needed.[^tepfer] “Forceful” therefore means deliberate attack and controlled peak, not constant maximum velocity.

### 2.7 Voice motion and interaction

On each accepted source attack, all generated voices move as a chained diatonic block. Across attacks this is predominantly parallel/planed motion; there is no independent contrary or oblique voice behavior and no contextual voice leading. Turning voice leading on would revoice pitch classes across time and destroy the exact stack invariant.

There is likewise no listening agent, call-and-response, bass interaction, or adaptive density. The only temporal interaction is the player's decision to repeat, vary, intensify, or leave silence after hearing each block.

## 3. Temporal behavior

### 3.1 Within-phrase evolution

`Space → State cell → Repeat/shift → Peak → Release → Silence`

| State | Trigger | Player behavior | Engine behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| Space | all prior notes off | 1 beat silence | no output |
| State cell | first attack | 2–3 notes, medium velocity | one deterministic block per attack |
| Repeat/shift | clean release | repeat rhythm; alter one degree or register | same stateless mapping |
| Peak | second/third clause | one stronger or higher 2–4-note cell | velocity/register merely follow input adapter behavior; no inferred peak |
| Release | final destination | hold 0.5–1 beat, then release | matching generated NoteOff set |
| Silence | final NoteOff | rest one bar | zero active notes expected |

No state transition is implemented by HarmonyEngine. This is a testable performer score for a static mapping.

### 3.2 Across-section evolution

For a 30–45 second acceptance form:

1. **Sparse statement (4 bars):** one two-note cell, then space.
2. **Building (4 bars):** repeat the cell twice; alter one degree or move it an octave.
3. **Full but bounded (4 bars):** one 3–4-note clause at stronger velocity; never overlap blocks.
4. **Withdrawal (4 bars):** return to the opening cell at lower density; final full-bar silence.

This captures accumulation, one controlled transformation, peak, return, and thinning without pretending the software has a phrase/motif lane. There is no automatic modulation, fragmentation, side-slip, cadence, or sectional scene.

### 3.3 Cadence and resolution

The engine has no cadence model. A bounded player-supplied close is `2 → 1`, `5 → 1`, or repeated `1`, followed by full release and silence. This is an abstract closure signal, not a claim about Tyner's dominant-resolution practice, which is materially richer.[^linna][^tepfer]

## 4. Testable stylistic invariants

1. Current tonic is preserved; scale character becomes Dorian.
2. Exactly one accepted in-scale source NoteOn produces exactly four output notes when safely in MIDI range.
3. Source occupies the lowest arrangement position; three generated voices lie above it.
4. Voice construction is chained `+3` Dorian scale indices, with no voice-leading or octave post-processing.
5. Source degrees `1,2,5,6` produce the exact relative semitone vector `[0,5,10,15]`.
6. Degrees `♭3,4,♭7` are disclosed as deterministic diatonic variants containing one A4, not silently called exact P4 stacks.
7. One clean source NoteOff returns the source plus the exact three generated pitches stored at NoteOn.
8. The preset supplies harmony only: rhythm, accent, motif, phrase arc, density changes, and silence come from the performer.
9. One physical note/block at a time; no overlap, pedal, ringing string, or unreleased same-pitch retrigger in acceptance input.
10. Every phrase and final exercise ends with zero sounding notes.

## 5. Allowed variation without losing identity

- current tonic/key;
- source register within the safe middle range;
- source degrees, with exact-P4 corpus (`1,2,5,6`) preferred and disclosed A4 variants permitted;
- cell length from two to four attacks;
- even eighth, triplet, quarter-note, and syncopated placement supplied by the player;
- one-note mutation, octave relocation, dynamic rise/fall, and longer/shorter silence;
- tempo within the stated range;
- tone/sound preset chosen separately.

Changing voice count, voice position, Dorian collection, DiatonicFourths mode, or enabling voice-leading/octave spread loses the acceptance identity.

## 6. Misleading or rejected behaviors

The following should fail review or remain outside the performer contract:

1. **Artist imitation claim:** “sounds/plays like McCoy Tyner.” The result is a bounded fourth-stack study only.
2. **Fourth-only caricature:** presenting quartal harmony and pentatonics as Tyner's complete language. Both Linna and Satterthwaite document broader bebop, blues, dominant, functional, and layered practice.[^linna][^satterthwaite]
3. **Contextual comping claim:** the engine does not listen to bass/chords, choose voicings from harmonic context, or decide when to strike.
4. **Pattern-generation claim:** no motif capture, riff generation, accent pattern, swing, or phrase evolution exists in this configuration.
5. **Automatic chromatic tension claim:** an out-of-Dorian note is not rejected by the engine; `harmonize_smart` substitutes a preferred consonant chromatic interval landing in-scale, then continues the chain. This breaks the exact fourth vector and is not context-aware resolution.
6. **Pentatonic configuration shortcut:** a five-note `MinorPentatonic + DiatonicFourths` configuration does not preserve fourths.
7. **Constant fortissimo:** historical force is interactive and dynamically responsive, not undifferentiated maximum velocity.[^tepfer]
8. **Dense input:** chords, legato overlap, sustain pedal, ringing guitar strings, same-pitch retrigger before release, or rapid continuous runs risk mud and lifecycle ambiguity.
9. **Independent bass claim:** the source-as-lowest-voice arrangement is not a pedal, walking bass, or separate bassist.
10. **Guaranteed four voices at MIDI extremes:** directed mode has a wrap fallback near range edges; acceptance must remain in the middle register.

### Rejection behavior available now

There is **no typed preset field for “reject out-of-scale input.”** The current core actively harmonizes chromatic inputs rather than rejecting them. Therefore:

- UI guidance must say “use the shown Dorian collection”;
- automated stylistic tests must treat chromatic inputs as outside corpus;
- product copy must not promise strict collection rejection;
- true rejection/snap policy would require a new capability and should not be invented for this baseline.

## 7. Abstract, non-copyrighted acceptance examples

All examples are newly constructed in scale degrees and beats. Assume current tonic **D**, Dorian collection `D E F G A B C`, four voices, source position 3, no voice-leading, no octave mode.

### Example A — exact fixed stack and clean release

| Beat | Input | Expected output, low→high | Relative vector | Lifecycle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | NoteOn `1` (D3) | `1–4–♭7–♭3'` = D3–G3–C4–F4 | `[0,5,10,15]` | four NoteOns |
| 1.5 | NoteOff `1` | same four pitches | — | four NoteOffs |
| 2.0–4.0 | rest | none | — | zero active |

### Example B — repeated two-note rhythmic cell, no generated rhythm

Input on beats `1.0, 1.5, 3.0, 3.5`, with every note released before the next:

- cell 1: `1 → 2`
- cell 2: `1 → 5`

Expected blocks:

- `1 → [1,4,♭7,♭3']`, vector `[0,5,10,15]`;
- `2 → [2,5,1',4']`, vector `[0,5,10,15]`;
- `1` repeats identically;
- `5 → [5,1',4',♭7']`, vector `[0,5,10,15]`.

The engine must not add attacks in the holes at beats 2 or 4. After the beat-3.5 block releases, active-note state must be empty.

### Example C — disclosed diatonic tension variant

| Beat | Input | Expected output | Vector | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | `♭3` (F3) | `♭3–6–2'–5'` = F3–B3–E4–A4 | `[0,6,11,16]` | A4 at bottom, not exact all-P4 |
| 2.0 | `2` (E3), after full release | E3–A3–D4–G4 | `[0,5,10,15]` | return to exact stack |
| 3.0–4.0 | rest | none | — | closure by release/silence |

### Example D — rejection/corpus boundary

Input `♭2` (E♭ over D Dorian) must **not** be used to prove the preset's quartal invariant. Current code may output a consonant in-scale target (for this register/direction, the first generated relation can be a major third rather than a fourth) and then chain further diatonically. Acceptance should record this as “processed but outside stylistic corpus,” not as rejection and not as a valid fixed stack.

## 8. Current Contrapunk capability audit

### 8.1 Catalog/type surface

`ArrangementPresetV2` can currently encode:

- `scaleMode: 'Dorian'`;
- `mode: 'DiatonicFourths'`;
- numeric `voiceCount` and `voicePosition`;
- voice-leading on/off and style;
- octave mode/intensity;
- interchange on/off;
- Companion enable/disable and mix gains;
- guidance text, approximation, reference metadata, research state, and capability requirements.

Validation constrains voice count to 1–8 and requires `voicePosition < voiceCount`. The existing preset 27 remains a research-pending draft with requirement `['harmony']`; no operational `QUARTAL_COLOSSUS_PRESET` record yet exists.

Important omissions for this research translation:

- no accepted-source-degree mask;
- no out-of-scale rejection policy;
- no minimum/maximum register;
- no serialized bass-suppression threshold;
- no velocity/accent map;
- no chord gate, legato policy, or overlap limit;
- no phrase state, motif, density, or silence detector;
- no explicit semitone interval-stack field.

### 8.2 HarmonyEngine pitch behavior

The implementation supports this narrow mapping now:

1. `DiatonicFourths` dispatches to `diatonic_fourths_directed`.
2. In-scale input requests `+3` scale indices for an above voice.
3. Multi-voice generation is chained: every next voice is generated from the previous voice.
4. `voicePosition: 3` with four voices creates only the above chain, placing the source in the bottom arrangement slot.
5. The returned vector lists the source first, then generated voices; `last_arrangement_indices` / port maps preserve arrangement-slot routing independently of return order.
6. With voice-leading off and octave mode `None`, the pitch stack remains deterministic.

**Exact answer:** yes, `DiatonicFourths + Dorian + voiceCount 4 + voicePosition 3` gives an exact, defensible fixed `[0,5,10,15]` stack for input degrees `1,2,5,6` in a safe register. No, it does not give that same interval vector for every Dorian degree; `♭3,4,♭7` include one augmented fourth. No, it cannot enforce which degrees the performer uses.

### 8.3 NoteOn/NoteOff ownership

What works:

- `harmonize_note_on` stores the generated harmony vector under the source MIDI number.
- `harmonize_note_off` removes that entry and returns the original source plus those exact stored pitches.
- deterministic quartal mode therefore releases the same generated pitches it attacked for a clean, single source-note lifecycle.
- panic/clear can clear maps; parameter setters queue held source pitches for router-level reharmonization.

Limits that must be explicit:

1. Ownership is keyed by **source MIDI number**, not a unique note instance. A same-pitch NoteOn before its earlier NoteOff overwrites the entry.
2. Different source notes can produce shared output pitches; HarmonyEngine has no per-output-pitch reference count.
3. The map stores one vector per source pitch, not a stack of overlapping instances.
4. Clean release through parameter changes depends on the surrounding router draining reharmonization/panic queues correctly; it is not proven by this read-only report.

**Exact answer:** clean NoteOn/NoteOff ownership is defensible only for the acceptance contract of one non-overlapping physical source note at a time, full release before retrigger, no pedal/ringing strings, and no configuration change while a note is held. Broader polyphonic or overlapping ownership is not guaranteed by `HarmonyEngine` alone.

### 8.4 Temporal and interaction capability

No Companion lane is needed or justified. The shared engine reacts synchronously to attacks and has no contextual comping, pattern generation, motif memory, accent sequencer, phrase detector, adaptive section logic, independent bass, or chord-following context in this configuration. Transport should remain untouched and `transportRequired` should be false.

Suggested approximation statement:

> A fixed four-voice Dorian fourth-derived block inspired by one open-voicing feature prominent in selected 1960–67 McCoy Tyner recordings. It does not generate Tyner's phrases, rhythm, touch, pedals, bass movement, dominant substitutions, chromatic resolutions, comping decisions, ensemble interaction, or formal development. Your monophonic cell, accents, register, dynamics, repetition, and silence supply the temporal shape; exact all-perfect-fourth stacks occur on displayed source degrees 1, 2, 5, and 6.

## 9. Confidence and competing interpretations

| Claim | Confidence | Basis / qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Fourth-based open voicings are relevant to the selected corpus | High | Linna's transcription study, Rinzler bibliography record, Tyner interview.[^linna][^rinzler][^tepfer] |
| Dorian/pentatonic color is relevant but insufficient as a whole-style account | High | Linna and Satterthwaite explicitly challenge the reductive modal-only account.[^linna][^satterthwaite] |
| Exact current-engine vectors and voice placement | High | Direct code audit and arithmetic from Dorian offsets `[0,2,3,5,7,9,10]`. |
| Forceful repeated cells are suitable performer guidance | Medium | Consistent with selected mature recordings and physical/interactive descriptions, but the engine does not embody touch or rhythm. |
| Proposed phrase arc is historically “Tyner form” | Low / not claimed | It is an acceptance exercise derived from Tyner's general comments on development and return, not a transcription or style generator.[^tepfer] |
| Chromatic notes should be rejected by the current engine | False | Current `harmonize_smart` supplies chromatic fallback harmony; rejection is unavailable. |

### Competing interpretation to preserve

The common interpretation foregrounds quartal voicing, modes, and pentatonics. Linna's larger 1960s transcription corpus instead argues that dominant chords, II–V/functional motion, pedal relationships, and independence of melody/harmony are fundamental, and quotes Tyner calling his music an extension of bebop.[^linna] This report adopts quartal/Dorian color only because it is the bounded part the current engine can represent; the approximation text must preserve the competing, broader account.
