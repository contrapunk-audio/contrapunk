# Research: Preset 14 — Color-Mode Windows (Messiaen), music theory and temporal behavior

## Summary

A defensible scope is Messiaen's **wartime/early-1940s language, 1940–44**, anchored in *Quatuor pour la fin du Temps* (1940–41), *Visions de l'Amen* (1943), *Vingt Regards sur l'Enfant-Jésus* (1944), and the contemporaneous codification in *Technique de mon langage musical* (1944). In that corpus, limited-transposition modes are harmonic and melodic color fields, but identity also depends on pedals/centers, characteristic chord spacings and added tones, compact transformed motives, juxtaposed layers, extreme registers/dynamics, and rhythmic procedures that suspend ordinary metric expectation.

The current engine can represent a **static, fixed-transposition Mode-2 window** exactly at the pitch-collection level, and a four-voice `DiatonicThirds` stack over it gives a mathematically exact chain of parallel diminished-seventh chords. That is an honest narrow color study, not a representation of Messiaen's full chord vocabulary or temporal practice. `AugmentedHex` is a six-note set, **not** Messiaen's nine-note Mode 3, and no current shared HarmonyEngine/Lane/`ArrangementPresetV2` capability automatically rotates collections or their transpositions.

## Scope and corpus

**Selected period:** 1940–44, not the whole career.

- *Quatuor pour la fin du Temps* (1940–41): chamber differentiation, birdsong-like solo writing, isorhythmic/ostinato layering, non-retrogradable and additive rhythm, immense slow melody, and forceful rhythmic unison.
- *Visions de l'Amen* (1943): two-piano contrasts of resonance, registral mass, rhythmic layers, thematic recurrence, and monumental arrivals.
- *Vingt Regards sur l'Enfant-Jésus* (1944): cyclic themes, modal/triadic coexistence, chordal color, repetition, extreme dynamics/register, and large-scale return.
- Messiaen, *Technique de mon langage musical* (1944): primary theoretical witness for the language he chose to codify at this point—limited-transposition modes, added values, non-retrogradable rhythms, rhythmic characters, melodic contour, added-note harmony, resonance, pedals, and connections between modality and tonality. [Treatise scan](https://monoskop.org/images/5/50/Messiaen_Olivier_The_Technique_of_My_Musical_Language.pdf) [Philharmonie catalogue record](https://mediatheque.philharmoniedeparis.fr/mediatheque/doc/ALOES/0077526/technique-de-mon-langage-musical-texte-avec-exemples-musicaux-olivier-messiaen)

Anthony Pople's movement-by-movement Cambridge study confirms the *Quatuor*'s eight-movement design and supplies a scholarly work-specific frame rather than treating one famous device as the entire style. [Pople, *Messiaen: Quatuor pour la fin du temps*](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139166928)

## Findings

### 1. Pitch centers, exact modes, transpositions, chromaticism, and tuning

1. **Symmetric collection does not mean absence of center (high confidence).** In the scoped works, centers can be projected by pedals, bass fundamentals, triadic sonorities, registral emphasis, repetition, and dynamic/formal arrival even when the active collection is symmetric. Messiaen explicitly discusses the modes' relationship with tonality rather than presenting them as a compulsory replacement for it. A preset must therefore distinguish **collection root used for computation** from **heard tonal center**, which the performer and voicing may reinforce. [Messiaen, *Technique*, chs. XVI–XVIII](https://monoskop.org/images/5/50/Messiaen_Olivier_The_Technique_of_My_Musical_Language.pdf)
2. **Mode 1 is whole tone (high).** Relative to C, `M1¹ = {0,2,4,6,8,10}`; transposition by one semitone gives the only other distinct set, and transposition by two reproduces the first. Thus it has **two** distinct transpositions. Current `ScaleMode::WholeTone` is exact at T0.
3. **Mode 2 is octatonic, alternating semitone and whole tone (high).** Using C as the first degree and pitch classes relative to C:
   - `M2¹ = {0,1,3,4,6,7,9,10}` (H–W repeating)
   - `M2² = T1(M2¹) = {1,2,4,5,7,8,10,11}`
   - `M2³ = T2(M2¹) = {0,2,3,5,6,8,9,11}` after sorting modulo 12
   - `T3(M2¹) = M2¹`; therefore there are exactly **three** distinct transpositions.
   Current `DiminishedHalfWhole` at C is `M2¹`; current `DiminishedWholeHalf` at C is `M2³`. `M2²` is available by transposing the half-whole collection up one semitone (for example, selecting key D-flat). The key selector exposes twelve labels, but only three pitch-class collections are distinct. [Messiaen, *Technique*, ch. XVI](https://monoskop.org/images/5/50/Messiaen_Olivier_The_Technique_of_My_Musical_Language.pdf) [Pople, modes appendix, pp. 96–99](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139166928.011)
4. **Mode 3 is a nine-note collection, not an “augmented hexatonic” scale (high).** At the corresponding T0 orientation, `M3¹ = {0,2,3,4,6,7,8,10,11}`, generated by W–H–H repeated. It has **four** distinct transpositions; T4 reproduces T0. By direct code inspection, current `ScaleMode::AugmentedHex = {0,3,4,7,8,11}`: six pitch classes, alternating minor-third/semitone. It is a subset of the stated `M3¹`, but it omits pitch classes `{2,6,10}` and is therefore categorically **not Messiaen's Mode 3**. Calling it Mode 3 would fail a cardinality test (`6 != 9`) before any stylistic judgment.
5. **“Rotating colors” is not ordinary modal rotation (high).** Messiaen's numbered *transpositions* move an entire symmetric set by semitone; changing the degree treated as first within the same set does not create a new pitch-class collection. Product language must not confuse (a) degree rotation, (b) transposition among the two/three/four distinct M1/M2/M3 sets, and (c) switching between mode families.
6. **Chromaticism often comes from collection juxtaposition, superposition, added notes, and special chords—not random out-of-scale correction (high).** A chromatic pitch may be structural, an appoggiatura/added value in pitch, part of another mode or triad, or a layer against the current field. Current consonant fallback/modal interchange is not an adequate model of this behavior.
7. **Tuning is conventional chromatic semitone space for this preset (high).** The piano/chamber corpus and treatise examples support twelve-note pitch-class modeling; quarter-tone discussion in the treatise is comparative, not a requirement for this bounded preset. No microtonal approximation is needed.

### 2. Chords, spacing, inversion, doubling, tension, and resolution

8. **The modal collection is a reservoir, not a chord recipe (high).** Messiaen uses complete/partial modal sonorities, triads within or against modes, added sixths and other added notes, chords of resonance, dominant-derived sonorities, appoggiaturas, and pedals. Selecting Mode 2 does not specify which notes sound together. [Messiaen, *Technique*, chs. XIII–XVII](https://monoskop.org/images/5/50/Messiaen_Olivier_The_Technique_of_My_Musical_Language.pdf)
9. **A four-note every-other-degree Mode-2 stack is exact but narrow (high).** For `M2¹` degrees `[0,1,3,4,6,7,9,10]`, chained `+2 scale degrees` yields:
   - degree 1: `[0,3,6,9]`
   - degree 2: `[1,4,7,10]`
   - degree 3: `[3,6,9,0′]`, etc.
   Every adjacent chord member is 3 semitones apart; each result is a diminished-seventh chord. Moving the source by one Mode-2 degree translates every voice by the same alternating `+1,+2` semitone step. With a fixed register layout and voice leading disabled, chord quality and inversion remain fixed. This is a valid **Mode-2 diminished-seventh plane**, not evidence that Messiaen harmonized every melodic note this way.
10. **Three voices give diminished triads, not the full characteristic field (high).** The same chain with three total voices yields `[d,d+2,d+4]`, e.g. `[0,3,6]`. Four total voices complete `[0,3,6,9]`. Five repeats the first pitch class at the octave. Therefore voice count is musically consequential and must be explicit in any acceptance test.
11. **Spacing and inversion are color parameters (medium-high).** The scoped works juxtapose close, hard-edged chords with widely separated bass, resonant upper notes, octave masses, and register-specific instrumental identities. A default close diminished-seventh stack is only one window. `OctaveMode::Spread` mechanically adds an increasing octave to successive voices and `Mirror` triples harmony notes; neither encodes a researched fixed Messiaen voicing or controlled doubling.
12. **Doubling is deliberate and often structural (medium-high).** Octaves, unisons, bass reinforcement, and registral doubling may intensify a theme or arrival. Constant automatic doubling would erase the contrast between bare line, colored chord, and monumental tutti.
13. **Tension/resolution is not reducible to dominant–tonic voice leading (high).** Resolution may be produced by register, duration, thinning, recurrence of a central triad/theme, pedal release, or modal change. Conversely, a consonant triad can function as luminous color rather than closure. Bach/Palestrina voice-leading penalties would suppress purposeful parallels, tritones, semitones, and chord blocks.

### 3. Melody, motive, ornament, and voice motion

14. **Melody combines compact cells with extreme expansion (high).** The period includes short recurring themes, interval cells, birdsong-like flourishes, and extraordinarily slow, long-breathed praise melodies. Messiaen's treatise discusses contour, preparation of a melodic summit, enlargement/elimination of intervals, and transformation rather than an undifferentiated scalar stream. [Messiaen, *Technique*, chs. VIII–XII](https://monoskop.org/images/5/50/Messiaen_Olivier_The_Technique_of_My_Musical_Language.pdf)
15. **Repetition is identity-bearing, not compositional failure (high).** Exact or near-exact return can stabilize a theme while harmony, register, dynamics, or accompaniment changes around it. *Vingt Regards*' cyclic themes unify a very diverse long form; scholarship specifically identifies modes, form, and recurring/cyclic themes as structural agents. [Rogosin, *Aspects of structure in Vingt Regards*](https://doi.org/10.14288/1.0087247)
16. **Ornament can be a separate behavioral layer (medium-high).** Birdsong-like rapid contours contrast with slow chordal or sustained material. A random harmony note below each attack would not create this relationship; it would merely thicken the source.
17. **Voice motion alternates among blocks and genuine independence (high).** Relevant behaviors include exact parallel chord motion, oblique motion over a pedal/ostinato, rhythmic unison, registrally separated independent lines, and superposed cycles that do not share the same period. There is no single global preference for contrary motion or shortest-path voice leading.
18. **Instrument roles remain distinct (high).** In *Quatuor*, violin/cello long lines, clarinet birdsong/abyssal solo space, piano chordal resonance/ostinato, and ensemble unison are not interchangeable “four harmony voices.” Pople's separate movement analyses support treating orchestration and texture as formal behavior. [Pople contents and movement studies](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/messiaen-quatuor-pour-la-fin-du-temps/D44E3EC1E1EC9DA32B73ECB0B21AB8E4)

### 4. Rhythm, meter, accent, phase, phrase, and form

19. **Additive rhythm disturbs regularity by a small value (high).** Messiaen's “added value” may be an added short duration, dot, or rest; the point is a perceptible asymmetry inside otherwise graspable material. Random timing jitter is not equivalent. [Messiaen, *Technique*, ch. III](https://monoskop.org/images/5/50/Messiaen_Olivier_The_Technique_of_My_Musical_Language.pdf)
20. **Non-retrogradable rhythm is exact temporal symmetry (high).** A duration sequence reads identically forward and backward, producing a bounded “charm of impossibility.” It requires phrase/pattern memory and exact scheduling; no current lane constructs it automatically. [Messiaen, *Technique*, ch. V](https://monoskop.org/images/5/50/Messiaen_Olivier_The_Technique_of_My_Musical_Language.pdf)
21. **Independent cycles can suppress a common downbeat (high).** In “Liturgie de cristal,” repeating rhythmic/harmonic cycles of different lengths coexist with independent upper lines; the experience is not ordinary groove accompaniment. The current live harmony engine follows note attacks and has no general isorhythmic multi-cycle pattern lane. [Pople, “Liturgie de cristal,” pp. 17–27](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/messiaen-quatuor-pour-la-fin-du-temps/liturgie-de-cristal/92BECDBD97B16BC547A450BF862BF332)
22. **The period also uses forceful exact unison and immense slowness (high).** “Danse de la fureur” foregrounds coordinated rhythmic unison; the two *Louanges* stretch melody over very slow accompaniment. Therefore “Messiaen rhythm = irregular busy notes” is false. [Pople, movement chapters 5, 6, and 8](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/messiaen-quatuor-pour-la-fin-du-temps/D44E3EC1E1EC9DA32B73ECB0B21AB8E4)
23. **Phrase length is elastic and often sectional (medium-high).** Short incantatory cells may accumulate or alternate with broad spans and silence. Cadence can be a held resonant sonority, registral/dynamic culmination, abrupt block boundary, or thematic return rather than a fixed scale-degree formula.
24. **Form proceeds by juxtaposition, recurrence, accumulation, and transformation (high).** Across a movement or cycle, a theme can return enlarged, reharmonized, transferred in register, surrounded by denser figuration, or reduced to a residue. A static scale selector represents none of these temporal relations by itself.

### 5. Register, orchestration, dynamics, articulation, and layer interaction

25. **Register is structural (high).** Low pedals, middle-register chord blocks, brilliant high resonance, isolated extreme notes, and wide gaps create distinct color planes. Range must be bounded deliberately; generic octave spreading is not sufficient.
26. **Dynamics articulate form (high).** The scoped corpus ranges from near-immobile softness to abrupt, monumental fortissimo. Crescendo may reveal/accumulate layers; withdrawal may leave one sustained line or central sonority. Fixed output velocity cannot manufacture this arc, but it should preserve performer velocity.
27. **Articulation is sharply contrasted (high).** Sustained praise melody, dry/forceful unison, ringing chord attack, detached birdsong, and silence each imply different note lifecycles. A useful static preset should not add autonomous fills into rests or blur every attack with long Hold behavior.
28. **Layers may share a collection without sharing rhythm or role (high).** Pedal, ostinato, chord plane, slow melody, and ornament can interact by superposition. Current harmony voices all derive immediately from one source onset; that is chordal thickening, not independent-layer counterpoint.

## Evolution over time

### Within a phrase

A representative bounded trajectory is:

`Exposed pitch/center → colored reiteration → registral/dynamic enlargement → held or cut-off arrival → silence/residue`

- **Attack:** a single strongly profiled note/cell or resonant block states center/register.
- **Continuation:** exact/near repetition; change one dimension at a time—register, duration, chord density, or dynamic weight.
- **Intensification:** widen register, reinforce a pedal/octave, or present a fuller modal sonority; do not add random notes.
- **Cadential event:** sustain a central chord/note, reach an apex, abruptly terminate a rhythmic block, or thin to a residue.
- **Breath:** silence is preserved; no generated fill.

This is one transferable behavior, not a claim that every 1940–44 phrase follows one arch.

### Across sections

`Static color field → contrasting window → accumulated/superposed return → extreme culmination → transformed recollection`

A “window” may change collection/transposition, texture, tempo character, or instrumentation abruptly while a cyclic theme or center supplies continuity. Returns are often recognizable but recolored by harmony, register, density, or rhythmic setting. The catalog word **rotating** should therefore mean a deliberate section-level sequence, not per-note random scale changes.

### Career/period evolution

- **Before the selected period:** limited-transposition modes and theological/organ color were already established (for example, *La Nativité du Seigneur*, 1935); the 1944 treatise codifies an existing practice rather than inventing it at publication.
- **1940–44 target:** modal color coexists with clear thematic recurrence, birdsong precursors, added/non-retrogradable rhythm, triads/pedals, monumental slow lines, rhythmic unison, and sharply contrasted blocks.
- **Immediately after:** *Mode de valeurs et d'intensités* (1949) systematizes parameters in a way that helped catalyze European total-serial thought; this is not a rule for the selected wartime preset.
- **Later:** increasingly detailed birdsong transcription, larger color-chord taxonomies, and later orchestral practice should not be back-projected wholesale onto the 1940–44 target.

Confidence is high in this broad chronology, medium in drawing a hard stylistic boundary at 1944: the techniques overlap periods and Messiaen repeatedly revisited earlier materials.

## Testable stylistic invariants

1. A fixed Mode-2 state emits only pitch classes from one declared `M2¹`, `M2²`, or `M2³` collection for in-collection input.
2. The active Mode-2 transposition is explicit and stable within a window; it does not change per note or through random/modal-interchange fallback.
3. If the narrow four-voice `DiatonicThirds` mapping is used, every in-collection source attack yields four distinct pitch classes separated by three semitones cyclically: interval vector as an ordered close stack `[0,3,6,9]` from the source degree.
4. Under that mapping, consecutive Mode-2 degrees translate all four voices together by the source step (`+1` or `+2` semitones alternately), with voice leading and automatic octave redistribution disabled.
5. Rests remain rests; no layer generates unsolicited filler in the static approximation.
6. Source articulation, duration, and velocity remain perceptible; the harmony mapping must not flatten every phrase into constant loud legato.
7. The preset description names a **fixed Mode-2 color window inspired by a selected 1940–44 technique**, not “Messiaen's harmony” or a reconstruction of a work.
8. `AugmentedHex` is never labeled Mode 3; a Mode-3 capability test requires exactly nine unique pitch classes and four distinct transpositions.
9. Whole-tone, octatonic, and Mode-3 family changes are never claimed unless an explicit timeline/state mechanism schedules them.
10. No Palestrina/Bach voice-leading rule penalizes parallel blocks, tritones, or semitone relations in the bounded modal plane.

## Safe variables

- Any of the three distinct Mode-2 transpositions, explicitly chosen.
- Source register within safe MIDI bounds; fixed close or deliberately tested open layout.
- Three total voices (diminished triad) or four (diminished seventh), provided result copy and tests match the selected density.
- Source-note tempo from very slow sustained tones to moderate articulated cells; transport is unnecessary for static pitch mapping.
- Phrase duration, rests, dynamic arch, and articulation supplied by the performer.
- A fixed Mode-1 alternative can be honest if separately named/configured; it should not auto-switch during the phrase.
- Direction/voice position may vary if fixed for the window and range-tested; changing it mid-phrase risks inversion discontinuity.

## Misleading behaviors

- **Blocker:** advertising current `AugmentedHex` as Messiaen Mode 3.
- **Blocker:** claiming “rotating whole-tone, octatonic, and augmented colors” when the preset loads one static `scaleMode` and no lane/timeline rotates it.
- **Blocker:** random collection/transposition per note; this produces incoherence, not section-level color windows.
- **High:** treating `DiminishedWholeHalf` and `DiminishedHalfWhole` as two unrelated Messiaen modes. They are orientations/transpositions of Mode 2; key choice introduces redundant labels.
- **High:** enabling interchange or chromatic consonant fallback and describing the result as exact Mode-2 membership.
- **High:** calling the fixed diminished-seventh plane Messiaen's general chord practice; it excludes added-note, resonance, triadic, pedal, and special-chord behavior.
- **High:** generic shortest-path, Bach, or Palestrina voice leading; it may change inversion/register and suppress intentional parallel motion.
- **High:** `OctaveMode::Mirror` as a default; it mechanically triples harmony notes and destroys bounded density.
- **Medium:** continuous rapid input with no rests; each attack produces a dense chord and can become a note storm.
- **Medium:** random timing jitter sold as added-value rhythm, or a mirrored MIDI delay sold as non-retrogradable rhythm.
- **Medium:** auto-key detection on a symmetric set; it can invent unstable tonic changes where the collection itself does not identify a unique center.

## Temporal state sketch and triggers

`Bare center → Mode-2 color → Expanded resonance → Cut/held threshold → New window or recollection`

- **Bare center:** preset load or at least 2 beats/1–2 seconds of silence; source alone or sparse 2–3-note color, soft-to-moderate.
- **Mode-2 color:** trigger = 2–4 deliberate in-collection attacks with consistent register; fixed transposition and voicing.
- **Expanded resonance:** trigger = performer raises velocity/register or sustains a destination; optional fourth pitch class/octave reinforcement, strictly bounded.
- **Cut/held threshold:** trigger = long source hold, abrupt release after an accented cell, or phrase rest; output either sustains exactly with the source or releases exactly—no autonomous continuation.
- **New window:** future trigger = bar/phrase boundary or explicit scene command; switch to another declared M2 transposition or M1/M3 collection, then reset voicing state.
- **Recollection:** return to the earlier theme/center with changed register/density, not random new material.

**Current-engine reality:** only performer-driven bare/color/expand/cut behavior is available in one static collection. There is no shared phrase/intensity sensor, harmonic timeline, collection sequencer, non-retrogradable pattern generator, or automatic window rotation. The first honest mapping must remain static and say so.

## Original abstract degree/beat examples

Notation: `M2.n` is degree n of `M2¹`; brackets are simultaneous outputs; apostrophe indicates octave continuation. These are synthetic and quote no melody.

1. **Exact four-note Mode-2 plane (4/4):** input `b1 M2.1 (quarter), b2 M2.2 (quarter), b3–4 rest` → output `b1 [M2.1,M2.3,M2.5,M2.7] = [0,3,6,9]`; `b2 [M2.2,M2.4,M2.6,M2.8] = [1,4,7,10]`; then silence. Every voice moves +1 semitone, every chord remains a close diminished seventh, and no output occurs during the rest.
2. **Alternating Mode-2 translation and apex (3/4):** input `b1 M2.3 (quarter, mp), b2 M2.4 (quarter, mf), b3 M2.5 (quarter, f and held into next bar)` → close-stack pitch offsets `[3,6,9,12] → [4,7,10,13] → [6,9,12,15]`. Whole chords move `+1`, then `+2`; the final duration/velocity—not a functional cadence—marks the apex.
3. **Added-value target showing a timing gap:** underlying attacks at eighth-note units `[2,2,2]` become `[2,3,2]` by adding one sixteenth-equivalent unit to the middle duration. Pitch output may remain the same fixed M2 chord. Current note-following harmony can reproduce this only if the player performs it; no current lane derives the added value.
4. **Non-retrogradable target showing a phrase-memory gap:** duration cell on beats/subbeats `[1,2,3,2,1]` with a fixed M2 degree sequence chosen independently. Expected temporal symmetry is exact. Current engine does not capture and generate a palindromic duration phrase.
5. **Section-window target showing a collection gap:** bar 1 uses `M2¹` plane on degrees `1 → 2`; after a full-beat rest, bar 2 uses `M2²` plane on degrees `1 → 3`, with the second window retained until the next boundary. Current preset can load either state manually, but cannot schedule the boundary or rotate automatically.

### Honest evaluation of a minimal fixed Mode-2 mapping

A minimal candidate can use one exact Mode-2 collection, `DiatonicThirds`, four total voices, fixed voice position, voice leading off, octave processing off, interchange off, and player-supplied sparse articulation. Its strongest virtues are deterministic pitch membership, transparent interval math, no new engine branch, and an audible octatonic color. It also respects silence and can be tested exhaustively over eight degrees.

Its ceiling is low: every accepted note becomes the same diminished-seventh shape; there are no added-note/resonance chords, pedal layer, rhythmic transformation, collection change, cyclic superposition, orchestration, or automatic temporal evolution. It risks sounding like generic diminished planing if UI copy and performance guidance overclaim. This report therefore evaluates the mapping as **honest only when labeled a static fixed-transposition Mode-2 diminished-seventh window**. It does **not** choose the final product key, voice position, register, mix, or whether preset 14 should ship static versus remain gated; that belongs to synthesis/product review.

## Concrete review findings

- **blocker — `crates/contrapunk-harmony/src/config.rs`, `ScaleMode::AugmentedHex`:** `[0,3,4,7,8,11]` has six notes; Messiaen Mode 3 has nine. Never alias the existing enum to Mode 3.
- **blocker — `ui/src/lib/arrangement/catalog.ts`, preset 14 draft wording:** “Whole-tone, octatonic, and augmented colors rotate” is not realized by the draft's `requirements: ['harmony']` plus static safe config. Activation requires either narrower static copy or a real `harmonic_timeline`/equivalent capability.
- **high — current shared runtime:** no HarmonyEngine, Canon Lane, Counterpoint Lane, or V2 field rotates scale collections/transpositions automatically. Typed future capability names are gates, not implementations.
- **high — `modes.rs` / chromatic harmonization path:** out-of-set input can receive fallback harmony rather than strict Mode-2 mapping. Acceptance gestures must be in-collection unless a strict policy is added.
- **medium — directed range-wrap behavior:** close-stack interval invariants can break in absolute register near MIDI limits even when pitch classes remain related. Test the selected voice position over the supported source range.
- **medium — product terminology:** reserve “Mode 3” for the nine-note set and “transposition” for distinct set movement; avoid treating degree rotation as a new collection.

## Competing interpretations and confidence

- **Tonal versus atonal hearing:** symmetric collections weaken unique scale-degree hierarchy, yet pedals, triads, themes, bass, and formal arrival can produce strong centers. Confidence high that both modal symmetry and centric/tonal reference coexist; low in any blanket claim that the period is simply atonal.
- **Mode as color versus exhaustive pitch source:** Messiaen's own codification foregrounds modes, while score-based scholarship shows triads, special chords, added notes, and superposition. Confidence high that “one scale generates everything” is a caricature.
- **Static versus directed time:** non-retrogradable rhythm and independent cycles can suspend teleology, but the corpus also contains cumulative climaxes, thematic returns, and powerful arrivals. Confidence high that static and directional processes coexist.
- **How representative is diminished-seventh planing?** The mathematics is exact and reproducible; its stylistic representativeness is only medium-low because it captures a property of Mode 2, not the broader chord practice.
- **Mode-3 interval spelling:** pitch-class cardinality, W–H–H periodicity, and four transpositions are high confidence. Exact enharmonic spellings vary by transposition and source notation; implementation tests should use pitch classes, not force one spelling.
- **Career boundary:** high confidence that 1940–44 predates the 1949 duration/intensity experiment and later systematic birdsong period; medium confidence in assigning techniques exclusively to one period because Messiaen reused and expanded them.

## Gaps and residual risks

1. This report does not supply measure-by-measure pitch inventories for all three anchor works; synthesis should avoid claims that a particular fixed M2 transposition dominates the entire corpus.
2. Enharmonic spellings and Messiaen's color associations are not encoded; MIDI pitch classes cannot represent notation-specific spelling or synesthetic description.
3. The selected source register and `voicePosition` need exhaustive runtime tests across all eight M2 degrees and range edges before activation.
4. Out-of-collection source behavior remains unsafe for an “exact” claim unless performer guidance constrains input or the engine gains a strict pass/reject policy.
5. Automatic mode/transposition windows require a real shared harmonic timeline plus lifecycle tests; no current shared capability does this.
6. Mode 3 requires a new nine-note scale representation before any M3 product claim. Reusing `AugmentedHex` is not an approximation—it is the wrong set.
7. Performance-practice details differ across piano and chamber contexts; a separate performer report should set final tempo, articulation, range, and acceptance exercise.

## Research decision

`static_mapping_is_possible_but_scope_must_narrow_or_capability_remains_gated`

The existing engine is sufficient for a deterministic fixed Mode-2 diminished-seventh window. The catalog's plural, rotating M1/M2/M3 result is not currently supported, and Mode 3 itself is absent. Final product configuration is intentionally left to parent synthesis.
