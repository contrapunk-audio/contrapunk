# Research: Preset 23 — Sixth-Diminished Conveyor (Barry Harris teaching scope)

## Summary

This preset can honestly reference **Barry Harris’s mature, explicitly taught sixth-diminished harmonic method**, not Harris’s whole performing career and not bebop in general. The documented method treats major/minor sixth chords and related diminished sevenths as a **scale of chords** from which voicing movement, borrowing, tension, and resolution can be generated; reducing it to an eight-note “bebop scale” or an automatic chord toggle removes the harmonic and pedagogical point.

The defensible corpus is Harris’s teaching from the mid-1980s through his final teaching years—especially *Passing It On* (1986), Howard Rees’s *Barry Harris Workshop* materials (1994/2005), Harris’s 2010 Smithsonian oral history, and a documented 2016 Bilbao workshop—set against his much longer formation in Detroit bebop and continuous teaching career. The product should therefore offer a bounded live-arrangement behavior inspired by this one taught device, with no claim to reproduce Harris’s improvisation, touch, time, repertory, or seven-decade style.

## 1. Scope and corpus

### Exact referenced corpus and teaching period

The preset should be scoped to Harris’s **mature codification and transmission of the sixth-diminished system, c. 1986–2021**, with the strongest documented technical evidence concentrated in 1994–2016:

- **1986:** *Passing It On: A Musical Portrait of Barry Harris*, a documentary showing Harris as performer and teacher; the scholarly review notes Harris playing Chopin with Sophia Rosoff and using classical piano study in his teaching context. [García-Valdecasas 2020, pp. 110–11](https://jazz-hitz.musikene.eus/index.php/jazz-hitz/article/download/70/42/)
- **1994 and 2005:** Howard Rees, *The Barry Harris Workshop*, Parts 1 and 2. The published scholarship relies on these authorized workshop books for notated sixth-diminished, borrowed-note, and standard-tune examples; Rees’s official description characterizes Part 2 as chord movement for accompaniment, arranging, and composition rather than scale recitation. [García-Valdecasas 2020, pp. 121–25, 140–41](https://jazz-hitz.musikene.eus/index.php/jazz-hitz/article/download/70/42/) [Howard Rees/Jazz Workshops](https://jazzworkshops.com/the-barry-harris-workshop-video-part-2/)
- **20 August 2010:** Harris’s Smithsonian Jazz Oral History interview with Aaron Graves. On transcript p. 25, Harris rejects the interval-only “half-step, whole-step” account and describes the diminished scale through two diminished structures and the dominant family they generate. [Smithsonian transcript, p. 25](https://americanhistory.si.edu/sites/default/files/file-uploader/Barry-Harris-Transcription-2020.pdf)
- **2016:** Harris’s Jazz Cultural Theatre of Bilbao workshop, documented in transcriptions of borrowed-note and Chopin examples. [García-Valdecasas 2020, pp. 123, 125–26](https://jazz-hitz.musikene.eus/index.php/jazz-hitz/article/download/70/42/)
- **Late continuity:** Harris taught weekly in person through 2019 and via Zoom until two weeks before his death in 2021. This supports treating the method as a sustained late-career pedagogy, not a one-off historical lecture. [Barry Harris Institute memorial program, pp. 5–6](https://barryharrisinstituteofjazz.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Barry_Harris_Memorial_Program.pdf)

### Supporting score and repertory evidence

Published notation tied to the Rees materials shows Harris applying the framework to **“Body and Soul”** and the bridge of **“Cherokee”**: the former assigns sixth-diminished resources to changing harmonic regions, while the latter uses borrowed diminished notes inside an E6 field. These examples show contextual movement and borrowing, not an invariant chord on every eighth note. [García-Valdecasas 2020, pp. 124–25](https://jazz-hitz.musikene.eus/index.php/jazz-hitz/article/download/70/42/)

The same article documents Harris using Chopin’s Étude op. 10 no. 4, bars 41–44, and Nocturne op. 37 no. 2, bars 7–8, to argue for voice-leading and diminished-family relationships. Those examples establish the breadth of Harris’s explanation, but **do not authorize calling the preset Chopin-like or classically imitative**. [García-Valdecasas 2020, pp. 125–33](https://jazz-hitz.musikene.eus/index.php/jazz-hitz/article/download/70/42/)

### Explicit exclusions

This report does **not** treat any single Harris recording as a complete realization of the system, nor infer that every voicing Harris played can be reverse-engineered from it. The NEA discography—e.g. *Chasin’ the Bird* (1962), *Barry Harris Plays Tadd Dameron* (1975), *For the Moment* (1984), and *Live at Maybeck* (1990)—is useful career evidence, but no sufficiently authoritative, measure-level analysis located here proves that the preset’s alternating behavior characterizes all of those recordings. [NEA biography](https://www.arts.gov/honors/jazz/barry-harris)

## 2. Historical and aesthetic context

Harris formed in Detroit’s 1940s modern-jazz community, absorbed Thelonious Monk, Charlie Parker, and Bud Powell, worked as house pianist at the Blue Bird, and accompanied musicians across swing and bebop generations. He began teaching his bebop theories by 1956, before jazz pedagogy was broadly institutionalized. [NEA biography](https://www.arts.gov/honors/jazz/barry-harris)

His method addressed a practical transmission problem: how to teach the **generative grammar** of bebop harmony and improvisation rather than merely hand students chord symbols, modal labels, or copied licks. The Institute’s memorial describes him as putting virtuoso improvisation “under a microscope” to identify scales, chords, and chromatic passing tones, while centering split-second musical choices, joy, surprise, and rhythmic twists. [Barry Harris Institute memorial program, pp. 3–5](https://barryharrisinstituteofjazz.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Barry_Harris_Memorial_Program.pdf)

The sixth-diminished idea belongs inside this larger project. Harris’s own 2010 objection to a mere half-step/whole-step definition is historically important: he wanted the diminished collection heard through **harmonic origins and relationships**, not memorized as an interval pattern. [Smithsonian transcript, p. 25](https://americanhistory.si.edu/sites/default/files/file-uploader/Barry-Harris-Transcription-2020.pdf) The contemporary Barry Harris Institute likewise separates but connects “creating movement in accompaniment,” sixth chords, sixth-diminished scales, borrowed notes, related diminished families, melody harmonization, and improvisational phrase rules. [Barry Harris Institute course outline](https://barryharrisinstituteofjazz.org/spring-2026-a-six-part-zoom-series/)

## 3. Career and style evolution

1. **Formation and performing language (1940s–1950s).** Harris’s church and classical piano background preceded immersion in Parker, Gillespie, Powell, Monk, and Fats Navarro. Detroit jam-session and house-pianist work put the language into practice before its later codification. [NEA biography](https://www.arts.gov/honors/jazz/barry-harris) [Barry Harris Institute biography](https://barryharrisinstituteofjazz.org/about-barry-harris/)
2. **New York performer/arranger and continuing teacher (1960s–1970s).** After moving to New York in 1960, Harris worked with Cannonball Adderley, Yusef Lateef, Coleman Hawkins, Charles McPherson, and others, while composing and arranging, including for strings. This varied work warns against defining his musicianship by one harmonic device. [NEA biography](https://www.arts.gov/honors/jazz/barry-harris)
3. **Community institution and explicit pedagogy (1982–1987).** The Jazz Cultural Center/Theater combined workshops, education, and performance. It made teaching a public, communal practice rather than a late add-on to his performing career. [NEA biography](https://www.arts.gov/honors/jazz/barry-harris)
4. **Codified international teaching (1986–2019).** Documentary, Rees’s workshop series, and international workshops preserve increasingly explicit explanations of chord movement, scale-of-chords thinking, borrowed notes, and melody harmonization. The 2016 Bilbao evidence shows the system still being demonstrated decades after the early workshop materials. [García-Valdecasas 2020, pp. 110–25](https://jazz-hitz.musikene.eus/index.php/jazz-hitz/article/download/70/42/)
5. **Final online transmission (2020–2021).** Harris moved weekly teaching online during the pandemic and continued almost until his death. This supports continuity of mission, but the current evidence does not establish that every technical formulation remained verbally identical across all decades. [Barry Harris Institute memorial program, pp. 5–6](https://barryharrisinstituteofjazz.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Barry_Harris_Memorial_Program.pdf)

**Preset period:** stages 3–5—the mature, explicitly taught method—not a simulation of Harris’s early Detroit playing, a particular album, or his entire career.

## 4. Persistent traits versus period-/project-specific traits

### Persistent, well-supported traits

- **Bebop as a living language to be transmitted.** Teaching began by 1956 and remained central through 2021. [NEA biography](https://www.arts.gov/honors/jazz/barry-harris) [Barry Harris Institute memorial program, pp. 4–6](https://barryharrisinstituteofjazz.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Barry_Harris_Memorial_Program.pdf)
- **Theory joined to practical phrase and voicing choices.** Rees’s workshop description and the Institute curriculum frame the materials as tools for accompaniment, arranging, composition, improvisation, and melody harmonization. [Howard Rees/Jazz Workshops](https://jazzworkshops.com/the-barry-harris-workshop-video-part-2/) [Barry Harris Institute course outline](https://barryharrisinstituteofjazz.org/spring-2026-a-six-part-zoom-series/)
- **Movement and resolution rather than static labeling.** Borrowed notes move between sixth and diminished resources and create tensions that call for resolution; published examples show different inversions and contextual harmonic regions. [García-Valdecasas 2020, pp. 119–25](https://jazz-hitz.musikene.eus/index.php/jazz-hitz/article/download/70/42/)
- **Continuity between ear, repertory, and theory.** Harris connected bebop practice with standards and selected classical passages rather than presenting a context-free scale chart. [García-Valdecasas 2020, pp. 124–33, 140](https://jazz-hitz.musikene.eus/index.php/jazz-hitz/article/download/70/42/)

### Bounded or period-specific traits

- The **formal “sixth-diminished” terminology and workbook-style diagrams** are best evidenced in the mature workshop corpus. They should not be projected backward as a complete account of every 1950s performance.
- The Chopin analogies are documented teaching examples, including specific passages Harris cited in 2016; they are not evidence that all Harris accompaniment derived from Chopin. [García-Valdecasas 2020, pp. 125–33](https://jazz-hitz.musikene.eus/index.php/jazz-hitz/article/download/70/42/)
- Strings, choral projects, compositions, solo piano, and trio accompaniment involved additional aesthetic decisions outside this preset’s scope. The NEA biography explicitly notes Harris’s arranging and diverse performance settings. [NEA biography](https://www.arts.gov/honors/jazz/barry-harris)

## 5. Relationship to bebop peers and tradition

### Shared vocabulary

Harris explicitly belonged to the classic bebop lineage of Parker, Gillespie, Powell, Monk, and Hawkins. Chromatic approaches, dominant motion, swing phrasing, standard-song harmony, and rapid improvisational decision-making are therefore **shared tradition**, not uniquely Harris inventions. [Barry Harris Institute biography](https://barryharrisinstituteofjazz.org/about-barry-harris/) [NEA biography](https://www.arts.gov/honors/jazz/barry-harris)

### Distinctive contribution in this scope

What is unusually characteristic is Harris’s **systematic pedagogical organization**: deriving chord families from diminished structures; privileging major and minor sixth chords; combining a sixth chord with its related diminished seventh as a scale of chords; and teaching borrowing and resolution as practical movement. The academic review summarizes four pillars—dominant seventh, major sixth, minor sixth, and dominant seventh-flat-five—generated by controlled changes to diminished-seventh tones. [García-Valdecasas 2020, pp. 112–21](https://jazz-hitz.musikene.eus/index.php/jazz-hitz/article/download/70/42/)

Harris also transmitted peer knowledge rather than claiming isolation. The review records his adoption of Monk’s hearing of a half-diminished seventh as a minor-sixth chord with its sixth in the bass. [García-Valdecasas 2020, p. 116](https://jazz-hitz.musikene.eus/index.php/jazz-hitz/article/download/70/42/) This is a useful warning: the preset references Harris’s taught synthesis inside a communal Black American jazz tradition, not a proprietary “secret scale.”

## 6. Common caricatures to avoid

1. **“It is just the bebop major scale.”** Disallowed. The added pitch is only the surface collection; Harris explicitly rejected a merely intervallic diminished-scale definition, and the taught method concerns chord families, inversions, borrowing, movement, and resolution. [Smithsonian transcript, p. 25](https://americanhistory.si.edu/sites/default/files/file-uploader/Barry-Harris-Transcription-2020.pdf)
2. **“Play a sixth chord, then a diminished chord, forever.”** Disallowed. Straight alternation is a basic harmonization exercise, not the complete musical behavior. The source corpus also includes borrowed notes, contrary/partial motion, melody on top, changes of harmonic region, dominant families, and selective resolution. [García-Valdecasas 2020, pp. 119–25](https://jazz-hitz.musikene.eus/index.php/jazz-hitz/article/download/70/42/)
3. **“Every melody note dictates an automatic parity-based chord.”** Disallowed. The published standard examples assign resources according to tune harmony and melodic context; accompaniment involves choices, not just even/odd scale-degree switching. [García-Valdecasas 2020, pp. 124–25](https://jazz-hitz.musikene.eus/index.php/jazz-hitz/article/download/70/42/)
4. **“This recreates Barry Harris’s playing.”** Disallowed. It omits touch, swing, rhythmic placement, voicing selection, repertory knowledge, interaction, phrase construction, and improvisational judgment.
5. **“This is all of bebop harmony.”** Disallowed. Parker, Gillespie, Powell, Monk, Dameron, and their peers shared and individually shaped a much larger language.
6. **“Harris invented diminished harmony or sixth chords.”** Disallowed. His distinctive achievement here is a practical, generative organization and pedagogy; he himself connected it to earlier tonal and classical practices.
7. **“Conveyor” means mechanical inevitability.** Avoid in copy. The title can describe audible passing motion only if Result/Play guidance stresses user-led phrases, selective tension, and clear resolution.

## 8. Claim-level confidence and gaps

| Claim | Confidence | Basis / limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Harris’s system is harmonic movement, not merely an eight-note scale | **High** | Harris’s 2010 primary interview, Rees materials, official Institute curriculum, and published analysis converge. |
| Sixth and related diminished chords form a “scale of chords” supporting inversions and borrowing | **High** | Notated derivation and examples in García-Valdecasas pp. 118–25; corroborated by estate teaching outline. |
| Mechanical alternation alone is an inadequate representation | **High** | Sources explicitly include borrowing, melody harmonization, chord-region mapping, dominant families, and resolution beyond basic alternation. |
| The mature teaching corpus is the proper preset scope | **High** | Directly documented from 1986 through 2021; exact technical evidence is strongest from 1994–2016. |
| The method exactly describes Harris’s accompaniment on all recordings | **Low / rejected** | No authoritative recording-by-recording, measure-level corpus supports this generalization. |
| A fixed algorithm can reproduce Harris’s phrasing or aesthetic judgment | **Low / rejected** | Primary/estate accounts stress improvisational choice, rhythm, joy, surprise, and lifelong repertory study. |
| The formal system was unchanged from Harris’s 1940s formation through 2021 | **Medium-low** | Long pedagogical continuity is clear, but surviving sources here do not establish identical terminology or sequencing across every decade. |
| The Chopin comparison proves direct historical derivation | **Low / rejected** | It demonstrates an analytical/pedagogical analogy, not a documented genealogy of bebop from Chopin. |

### Unresolved gaps

- The complete Rees video/workbooks and Institute member archive were not openly available for direct timestamp-by-timestamp checking; the report relies on the official description and authorized published reproductions.
- The Smithsonian PDF was intermittently verification-gated during retrieval; page 25 was confirmed through the indexed transcript result and corroborating scholarly discussion, but a preservation copy or archive reading-room check would strengthen quotation-level auditability.
- No authoritative transcription study was found that surveys a statistically meaningful set of Harris recordings for sixth-diminished usage. Therefore the preset must remain a **teaching-method reference**, not a performance-frequency claim.
- The exact date when Harris first adopted the phrase “sixth-diminished scale” remains unestablished by this corpus.

## Concrete implications for a bounded live arrangement preset

1. **Reference statement:** “Inspired by Barry Harris’s mature sixth-diminished teaching: sixth-chord stability animated by related diminished passing motion.” Do not say “plays like Barry Harris.”
2. **Behavioral minimum:** preserve an audible stable-sixth → tension/motion → stable-sixth relationship. Alternation may be one bounded mechanism, but it must resolve and must not run independently forever.
3. **User remains the phrase author:** input establishes tonal region, contour, timing, and cadence. The preset may harmonize connected notes and chromatic approaches; it must not claim to generate authentic bebop lines from arbitrary input.
4. **Context over parity:** choose movement relative to the active tonal/chord region and melodic destination, not solely by whether a note falls on an even or odd scale step.
5. **Selective density:** permit stable sixth sonorities to persist; use diminished motion as passing/approach tension. Avoid revoicing every note if that produces an unbroken mechanical texture.
6. **Resolution contract:** phrase-end or held destination notes should settle into the applicable major/minor sixth field when musically valid; panic/release must stop all generated movement.
7. **Honest capability label:** if the current engine only provides literal sixth/diminished alternation and cannot do borrowed-note voice-leading, chord-region interpretation, melody-top voicing, or phrase-aware resolution, label the preset an **approximation of the basic scale-of-chords exercise**, not the full Harris system.
8. **UI guidance:** retain the catalog’s connected bebop eighths/chromatic approaches/clear resolutions, but add space and destination emphasis. “Conveyor” must describe passing harmonic motion, not autonomous endless chord production.

## Disallowed product claims

- “The Barry Harris sound,” “authentic Barry Harris,” or “recreates Barry Harris’s playing.”
- “Barry Harris’s bebop scale” as the complete explanation.
- “Automatically alternates the correct chord under every note.”
- “Represents Harris’s whole career” or “the harmony of all bebop.”
- “Harris invented sixth chords/diminished harmony.”
- “Based on Chopin” without clarifying that Harris used selected Chopin passages as teaching/analytical analogies.
- Any claim that the preset supplies swing feel, phrase grammar, improvisational judgment, or ensemble interaction unless those behaviors are independently implemented and tested.
