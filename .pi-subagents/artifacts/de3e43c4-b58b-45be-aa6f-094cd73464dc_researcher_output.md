# Research: Preset 07 “Stretto Engine” — Role B theory and temporal behavior

**Retrieval date:** 2026-05-12  
**Scope:** selected fugues only: *WTC I* C-major Fugue, BWV 846; *WTC I* C-sharp-minor Fugue, BWV 849, as a contrasting real-answer/multi-subject case; and the late *Art of Fugue*, BWV 1080, especially Contrapunctus 5. This is not a claim about every Bach fugue or his whole career. Bach Digital dates the fair copy of *WTC I* to 1722 and the autograph *Art of Fugue* to 1742–49, so these witnesses span a pedagogical keyboard collection and a late systematic contrapuntal project, not one homogeneous period ([WTC I](https://www.bach-digital.de/receive/BachDigitalWork_work_00011162); [Art of Fugue](https://www.bachdigital.de/receive/BachDigitalWork_work_00001266)).

## Summary

Stretto is overlapping **recognizable subject/answer entries whose entry distance is shorter than in the opening exposition**, not merely a delayed echo, arbitrary canon, or a thicker chord stack. In the selected works, identity survives changes of entry pitch, tonal/real answer treatment, inversion, fragmentation, and sometimes rhythmic transformation; the overlap is made viable by contrapuntal planning, controlled dissonance, register, and cadence rather than by blindly copying every incoming note.

The current CanonLane can schedule several beat-delayed, diatonically transposed streams and therefore can produce a **bounded overlapping-answer approximation**. Fixed full-stream/full-subject delays are only that approximation: without motif capture/identity, subject-boundary detection, entry-plan state, tonal-answer rewriting, texture/cadence control, and pairwise vertical validation, the same configuration is equally capable of generic echo/canon and misleading note/chord accumulation.

## Findings

### 1. Definition and selected-work evidence

1. **Stretto is a temporal relation between thematic entries.** Prout defines it as subject/answer entries following at a shorter time distance than in the first exposition; later entries may begin before the earlier subject finishes. This distinguishes it from ordinary imitation after completion and from `stringendo` tempo acceleration ([Prout, ch. 8](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Fugue_%28Prout%29/Chapter_8)). **Confidence: high.**
2. **A canon may overlap continuously, but stretto remains fugal and thematic.** A canon mechanically maintains an imitative rule; stretto can choose only particular subject/answer entries, truncate or invert them, vary pitch and distance, interleave episodes, and aim at a density/formal peak. In BWV 846, post-exposition entries are almost continuous and some are incomplete (m.14 lacks the last four notes; m.15 gives only the first seven), yet their heads make them perceptible as subject entries ([Teoria BWV 846](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/BWV846/)). **Confidence: high for the reported entry inventory; medium on disputed total (22 complete versus counts including incomplete entries).**
3. **BWV 846 is the compact, subject-saturated model.** It is four-voice, its exposition order is S–A–A–S in functional labels (subject–answer–answer–subject), and numerous uninterrupted stretti follow; only m.23 and the final two measures lack subject material according to the cited analysis. Its answer is classified as real in the UCSB survey ([BWV 846 analysis](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/BWV846/); [UCSB answer survey](https://rothfarb.faculty.music.ucsb.edu/courses/103/subject_answer.html)). **Confidence: medium-high.**
4. **BWV 849 is a limit/contrast, not the primary preset model.** The five-voice C-sharp-minor fugue begins subject in bass (mm.1–4), real answer in tenor (mm.4–7), then subject in alto; it is also a multi-subject fugue, so later density cannot safely be reduced to “more copies of one hook” ([analysis](https://tonic-chord.com/bach-prelude-and-fugue-no-4-in-c%E2%99%AF-minor-bwv-849-analysis/); [UCSB](https://rothfarb.faculty.music.ucsb.edu/courses/103/subject_answer.html)). Exact later entry labels are analytically disputed, so this report does not encode its complete form. **Confidence: high on opening real answer; medium on the secondary analysis’s bar labels.**
5. **Contrapunctus 5 shows that distance need not decrease monotonically.** Its exposition uses rectus/inversus forms in 12-beat overlap; later pairs occur at 2, 6, and 4 beats and the close combines the two forms simultaneously. Alevizos likewise finds six exposition groups and explicitly says global distances are non-progressive: a half-note-distance group at mm.33–45 is followed by a dotted-whole-distance group at mm.47–62; a continuously decreasing process begins only around m.72 ([Teoria Contrapunctus 5](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/kdf/V/01.php); [Alevizos 2019, pp.145–152](https://revuemusicaleoicrm.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/7_rmo_vol.6.1_alevizos.pdf)). **Confidence: high.** Therefore “progressively closer” is a useful preset arc, not a universal Bach invariant.

### 2. Subject/answer pitch and motif identity

6. **Real versus tonal answer is structural, not a transpose toggle.** A real answer retains intervals under dominant transposition. A tonal answer alters one or more intervals to reconcile melodic identity with tonic/dominant harmony: commonly scale degree 1 answers 5 and 5 answers 1; opening-range contraction/expansion can turn a fifth into a fourth or vice versa before exact transposition resumes ([Puget Sound](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/FugueAnalysis.html); [UCSB](https://rothfarb.faculty.music.ucsb.edu/courses/103/subject_answer.html)). **Confidence: high.**
7. **“Tonic, fifth, octave answers” must mean entry levels/registral placements, not three fixed chromatic offsets.** Octave preserves pitch-class function; dominant answers may be real or tonally adjusted; diatonic `+4 degrees` (a fifth in seven-note indexing) alone cannot infer the correct answer. The preset must either (a) restrict its contract to real/diatonic answer approximation, or (b) detect a subject’s tonic/dominant skeleton and rewrite tonal-answer intervals. **Confidence: high.**
8. **Identity is rhythmic-contour plus salient head, not literal full-note equality.** BWV 846’s incomplete entries are still heard as entries; Contrapunctus 5 develops dotted subject, codetta, anapaest/countersubject fragments, rectus/inversus forms, and syncopation. Thus a two-to-four-note user motif can work only if the engine captures ordered scale-degree intervals and inter-onset rhythm, assigns a stable motif ID, and distinguishes complete entry, head-only entry, and unrelated continuation ([BWV 846](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/BWV846/); [Alevizos](https://revuemusicaleoicrm.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/7_rmo_vol.6.1_alevizos.pdf)). **Confidence: high.**

### 3. Countersubject, independence, harmony, and cadence

9. **A countersubject is recurring counterpoint against subject/answer; it is not every accompaniment.** Free counterpoint may change on each entry. Contrapunctus 5 has no fixed countersubject in Teoria’s reading, but motives from the subject supply free counterpoint and episodes; Alevizos discusses a segmented countersubject/anapaest. This is a real interpretive difference in labeling, not evidence for filling every gap with a generic third stack ([Puget Sound](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/FugueAnalysis.html); [Teoria Contrapunctus 5](https://www.teoria.com/en/articles/kdf/V/01.php); [Alevizos](https://revuemusicaleoicrm.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/7_rmo_vol.6.1_alevizos.pdf)). **Confidence: medium.**
10. **Invertible counterpoint requires role/register exchange without forbidden vertical results.** Contrapunctus 5 combines inversion/reversion and, in some passages, triple invertible counterpoint. A fixed per-entry chord fan-out is not equivalent: it couples voices homorhythmically and obscures line independence. **Confidence: high** from Alevizos’s score-based account.
11. **Dissonance is licensed by placement and resolution.** Alevizos identifies impossible combinations where leading tone/tonic or scale-degree 6/5 collisions create unacceptable accented dissonance; Bach’s dotted passing note can soften a clash into an appoggiatura/passing-resolution effect. The engine should not ban every 2nd/7th, but should require prepared/passing status and stepwise resolution, especially on strong beats ([Alevizos, pp.148–151](https://revuemusicaleoicrm.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/7_rmo_vol.6.1_alevizos.pdf)). **Confidence: high.**
12. **Cadence requires planned thinning/closure.** Subject saturation normally yields to cadential material or a coda: BWV 846 has no subject in its last two measures; Contrapunctus 5 ends with simultaneous forms, but as a composed culminating close rather than an indefinitely running queue. The preset must stop admitting new entries, resolve active dissonance, release/hold a tonic-supported sonority, then clear the phrase state. **Confidence: medium-high.**

### 4. Density, register, and temporal evolution

- **Within a phrase:** `Capture/solo statement → answer at stable exposition distance → overlap after subject head is established → optional closer entry/head-fragment → admission closes → cadence/release`.
- **Across a section:** `Sparse exposition (2 voices) → one or more stretto groups with varied voice order/pitch level → density peak (3–4 independent entries, possibly smallest safe distance) → episode/thinning → return or cadential culmination`.
- **Triggers:**
  - subject learned only after 2 matching performances or one explicit phrase boundary;
  - next entry may start after a configurable minimum recognizable head (normally 2 notes), and strictly before prior full subject ends for `stretto=true`;
  - increase density after another confirmed motif recurrence or after a section/bar threshold, not on every NoteOn;
  - decrease distance only if prospective pairwise simulation passes strong-beat consonance/resolution checks;
  - cadence on phrase silence, explicit stop/release policy, maximum group length, or tonic-directed terminal motif; no new entry after cadence admission closes.
- **Register:** assign each entry a persistent lane/register; prefer alternating outer/inner entries and at least a third of separation at attacks; allow crossings only deliberately. Octave displacement may clarify duplicate pitch class but must not substitute for voice independence.
- **Density bound:** 2 voices initially, 3 typical peak, 4 maximum for this live preset. BWV 849’s five voices are evidence of Bach’s range, not a sensible default for an unrecognized live motif.

### 5. Testable invariants

1. Every generated stretto entry carries the same motif ID as the captured subject and preserves its ordered rhythmic proportions and scale-degree contour, except an explicitly labeled tonal-answer alteration, inversion, augmentation/diminution, or head-fragment.
2. `stretto=true` only when `0 < entry_distance < learned_subject_duration`; an entry at or after full duration is ordinary imitation/canon, not stretto.
3. The first exposition establishes a larger reference distance before any reduced-distance group. Reduction may be staged, but need not be globally monotonic.
4. Entry transposition choices are bounded to tonic, dominant-answer, and octave-equivalent levels by default; a dominant entry declares `real` or `tonal`, never silently conflates them.
5. Tonal answer maps prominent opening 1↔5 functionally and permits only the minimum interval changes needed to retain the local tonic/dominant plan.
6. Each entry is a single independent line by default. Harmony fan-out is off; any countersubject is separately identified and rhythmically independent.
7. No new voice enters if maximum active lanes is reached, its register cannot be placed, or pairwise strong-beat validation predicts an unprepared/unresolved dissonance or parallel perfect interval.
8. Weak-beat 2nds/7ths/4ths are allowed only with documented preparation/passing/suspension role and stepwise resolution by the next eligible strong point.
9. Entry order is explicit and persistent (e.g. low→high→middle or outer→inner), not whichever MIDI note arrived most recently.
10. A cadence state blocks new entries, resolves/releases every emitted NoteOn exactly once, clears pending queues on stop/reset/panic, and requires a fresh motif confirmation before restarting.
11. Silence matters: at least one thinning/episode window or cadence gap occurs before a new density cycle.
12. Fixed-delay playback may be marketed as “overlapping answer approximation,” never as automatic Bach fugue generation.

### 6. Variable parameters

- motif length 2–8 notes (catalog guidance stays 2–4), similarity tolerance, head length, phrase-silence threshold;
- answer policy `real | tonal-if-needed | octave`, entry degree/register, voice order;
- exposition distance and stretto distances in beats or motif-duration fractions; number/order of groups; monotone reduction optional;
- active voices 2–4, register floor/ceiling, minimum attack spacing, crossing allowance;
- rectus/inversus, head-only probability, time ratio 0.5/1/2 only when explicitly enabled;
- counterline policy `none | learned countersubject | free constrained line`;
- dissonance strictness, strong-beat grid, cadence trigger/horizon, Hold mode;
- tempo/meter and articulation. Tuning remains 12-TET/MIDI for the product; this report finds no basis for a special Bach tuning emulation in this preset.

### 7. Rejected shortcuts / misleading behavior

- “Stretto = delay/echo” with no subject identity or overlap test.
- Three arbitrary copies at `+0/+7/+12` semitones; this ignores diatonic spelling and tonal answers.
- Every canon voice emitting its own 2–4-note harmony stack; this makes chord clouds, not fugal entries.
- Automatically decreasing delay after every incoming NoteOn; it responds to performance density, not thematic form.
- Calling any truncated accidental match a subject; require the learned head and timing tolerance.
- Parallel thirds/sixths throughout as a fake countersubject; independence and invertibility disappear.
- A universal “Bach career” claim based on BWV 846 or late *Art of Fugue*.
- Five-voice default because BWV 849 is five-voice; live input and current lifecycle ownership make this unsafe and stylistically indiscriminate.

## Abstract acceptance examples (novel; not copied melodies)

All beat numbers are relative quarter-note beats; scale degrees are local and octave marks use `'`.

1. **Real dominant answer, then true overlap.** Learned subject `M1 = [1@0.0(0.5), 2@0.5(0.5), 4@1.0(0.5), 3@1.5(0.5)]`, duration 2 beats. Exposition answer enters at beat 2 as `[5,6,1',7]` (real diatonic transposition). Stretto repeat enters at beat 3.0 while the beat-2 answer still has 1 beat left. Assert motif ID `M1`, distance 1.0 < 2.0, two active lanes, no chord fan-out.
2. **Tonal answer differs minimally from literal transpose.** Subject `M2 = [1@0(1), 5@1(0.5), 4@1.5(0.5), 3@2(1)]`. Literal dominant transposition would begin `[5,2',1',7]`; tonal answer uses functional opening `[5,1',7,6]` (illustrative policy outcome), declares `answer_kind=tonal`, and retains the rhythm. Assert the engine does not implement this merely as a fixed `+4 degrees` operation. This is an abstract functional test, not a claimed Bach quotation.
3. **Safe staged density and cadence.** `M3=[3@0(.5),2@.5(.5),1@1(1)]`, duration 2. Entries: lane A degree 1 beat 0; lane B degree 5 beat 2 (ordinary answer); lane C degree 1' beat 3.25 (1.25-beat stretto); lane B head fragment `[3,2]` beat 4.0 only if vertical simulation resolves any weak-beat 2nd by beat 4.5. At silence beginning beat 5, transition to Cadence, reject further entry, release all by beat 6 on tonic support.
4. **Non-monotone group modeled honestly.** `M4=[5@0(.5),6@.5(.5),4@1(1),5@2(1)]`, duration 3. Group distances `[1.0, 2.0, 0.5]` across separated groups with an episode gap. Assert the 2.0 group is still stretto (`2<3`) but is farther than the previous group; no invariant demands global monotonic decrease, matching Contrapunctus 5’s evidence.

## Current Strict Canon mapping and gaps

### Exact mapping found in code

The catalog labels preset 07 “Current Strict Canon baseline,” but the code’s concrete facility is `CanonLane` plus per-voice `HarmonyEngine`; there is no distinct `StrictCanon` type in the minimum relevant code read.

- `crates/contrapunk-companion/src/canon_lane.rs`: each `CanonVoice` has `delay_beats` clamped 0–16, `transpose_degrees` −7…7, `time_ratio` 0.125…8, optional mode/reference/voice-count/position/voice-leading/octave/species/strictness/Hold overrides. Up to 8 voices.
- Scheduling is transport-total-beat based. For an input at relative phrase time `r`, voice-on is `anchor + delay + r*time_ratio`; NoteOff duration is also multiplied by `time_ratio`. Phrase anchor resets after more than 2 beats of input silence.
- Pitch transposition uses the live scale’s `harmonize_smart`, then the subject pitch is passed through a per-voice mini `HarmonyEngine`. A valid `reference_voice < current_voice` creates a cascade. Per-voice mini-engines inherit global key/scale/settings unless overridden and copy counterpoint history along a cascade.
- **Important exact behavior:** each canon entry can emit a whole `HarmonyEngine` stack (`voice_count` defaults to 2), not merely one imitative line. This is unsuitable as the preset default because it multiplies density per entry.
- `HarmonyEngine` provides diatonic/chromatic pitch handling, voice position/register revoicing, optional voice-leading, and partial beat-aware Species counterpoint. It does not recognize motifs, create fugal answers, plan entries, or validate the full delayed subject against other delayed subjects.
- `CanonLane` tracks pending ons/offs and source note, sorts emissions by fire beat, dispatches matured events during router ticks, and serializes configuration. The companion is disabled by default and CanonLane itself is disabled by default.

| Register/density | voice_count/position/octave, caps | Mechanically available; preset must prevent stack multiplication |

### Required minimal product boundary

For the current release, configure **one subject note per canon voice** (`PassThrough`/`voice_count=1`), 2–3 delayed lanes, diatonic tonic/dominant/octave-equivalent levels, and explicit delays shorter than the instructed 2–4-note motif. UI copy should say “overlapping delayed answers” and require the player to repeat the same motif. Do not enable per-entry harmony stacks. A genuinely adaptive Stretto Engine needs motif capture and a small section FSM; StrictCounterpoint alone does not supply those.

## Transport, Hold, and lifecycle requirements

1. **Transport is required for honest timing.** Canon scheduling reads `Transport.total_beats()`. When stopped, sample position freezes, so queued emissions do not mature. The Tauri router ticks lanes about every receive-timeout loop, while the transport itself is sample-driven. Tempo changes currently recompute total beats from the same absolute samples at the new BPM; this can move scheduled-beat relationships discontinuously, so tests must cover tempo changes with pending canon events (`crates/contrapunk-transport/src/clock.rs`).
2. **Play/pause semantics:** `play()` resumes frozen time. `transport_stop` is not pause-for-canon: it stops and calls all-notes-off/reset-runtime. Therefore pending stretto entries must not reappear after stop. Reset rewinds and also drains.
3. **Hold choice:** default global Hold is `NearFuture{1 beat}`, which may cancel later stretto voices on source NoteOff. For a 2–4-note captured subject, use lane-level `PhraseEnd` only if bar horizon matches the taught phrase, or `Forever` to preserve scheduled imitation; the latter must still be defeated by Stop/Reset/Panic. `Cancel` contradicts delayed-answer playback whenever the source is released before an entry.
4. **Exact Hold resolution:** voice override → lane override → global. On source NoteOff, Cancel drops pending ons and schedules immediate offs for sounded notes; NearFuture retains only ons within its tail and caps release; PhraseEnd retains to `sequence_anchor + beats_per_bar`; Forever keeps natural scaled timing. Orphan offs are removed.
5. **Lifecycle:** disabling CanonLane clears pending ons/offs, held map, and anchors. Changing voice configuration clears in-flight state. CC123, transport stop/reset, router exit, or explicit panic must release tracked input/harmony/canon/counterpoint notes, clear `HarmonyEngine.active_notes`, and call `Companion.reset_runtime`. `stop_routing` currently sets the stop signal and the router’s teardown performs the downstream drain; acceptance must wait for teardown, not merely observe `is_running=false`.
6. **Every NoteOn exactly one NoteOff:** overlapping equal pitches are reference-counted in the Tauri router’s per-lane note counts/routed ownership. Preserve voice/source ownership through any Stretto FSM; pitch-set-only cleanup is insufficient.
7. **Surface gap:** this code review establishes Tauri/shared-core behavior. WASM and plugin host transport adapters were outside the requested minimum read; cross-surface preset availability remains unverified.

## Disagreements and confidence ledger

- **BWV 846 count:** Teoria reports 22 complete entries and several incomplete ones; other traditions count 24 by admitting fragments. Preset invariant should use motif-ID confidence, not a hard historical entry count. **Confidence high that the count is definition-sensitive.**
- **Contrapunctus 5 countersubject:** Teoria says no fixed countersubject; Alevizos discusses segmented countersubject material. Treat recurring anapaest as analysable material but do not claim a single invariant complete countersubject. **Confidence medium.**
- **Progressive compression:** catalog promises progressively closer answers; Contrapunctus 5 explicitly is not globally monotone. Keep progressive compression as the product’s bounded dramatic arc, not “what Bach fugues always do.” **Confidence high.**
- **BWV 849 later entries:** sources disagree in analytical labeling; only opening real-answer and five-voice/multi-subject contrast are used here. **Confidence medium beyond exposition.**
