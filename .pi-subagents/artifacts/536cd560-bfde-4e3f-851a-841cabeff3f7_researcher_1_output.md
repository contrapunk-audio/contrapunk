# Research: Preset 01 — Cloister Organum  
## Music theory and temporal behavior

## Summary

A defensible Notre-Dame preset must not reduce Léonin and Pérotin to parallel fourths. The repertory alternates three materially different behaviors: **organum purum** (a freely flowing duplum over a sustained chant tone), **discant/clausula** (both voices measured in rhythmic modes), and **triplum/quadruplum** (two or three rhythmically coordinated upper voices over a much slower chant tenor). The smallest honest Contrapunk implementation is therefore a bounded **two-voice, chant-tenor organum-purum approximation** using modal pitch material, long input holds, predominantly perfect-consonance arrivals, and phrase rests; explicit interval-stack selection and sectional rhythmic-state switching remain named gaps.

---

## 1. Scope and corpus

### Selected repertory

This report draws its rules from:

- two-voice *Magnus liber organi* repertory associated by the later testimony of Anonymous IV with **Léonin**, especially organa dupla such as *Viderunt omnes* and the broader W1/F/W2 transmitted corpus;
- the Notre-Dame repertory of **two-part discant clausulae**, including substitute clausula practice;
- the three- and four-voice repertory associated with **Pérotin**, especially *Viderunt omnes* and *Sederunt principes*, plus the larger tripla/quadrupla practice.

The repertory survives chiefly in the manuscript complexes commonly called W1, F, and W2. The modern *Magnus Liber Organi* edition itself reflects materially different editorial decisions about the underdetermined rhythm of sustained-tenor passages. [Turner, “Notre Dame Organum Duplum”](https://www.musicandpractice.org/volume-1/notre-dame-organum-duplum-modern-edition/)

### Attribution caution

Anonymous IV’s account linking Léonin with the *Magnus liber* and Pérotin with revisions and large-scale tripla/quadrupla was written decades later. “Léonin style” and “Pérotin style” are useful repertorial labels, not secure modern authorship metadata. Scholarship also emphasizes compilation, oral practice, substitution, and reuse rather than a fixed authored work in the modern sense. [Bradley, “Who ‘Made’ the Magnus Liber?”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0261127901001061)

**Preset scope:** late-twelfth-/early-thirteenth-century Parisian liturgical organum, with the operational baseline centered on **organum duplum/purum**, not a claim to reproduce every Notre-Dame texture.

**Confidence:** High for the three-way stylistic distinction; medium for individual attributions.

## 2. Three practices that must remain separate

| Practice | Tenor behavior | Added-voice behavior | Rhythmic organization | Operational identity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Organum purum / organum per se** | One chant pitch sustained for a long span | One florid duplum, usually above it | Rhythm underdetermined/free; ligatures shape gestures without proving one continuous rhythmic mode | Drone-like span, but with responsive intervallic articulation |
| **Discant / clausula** | Chant melisma segmented into repeated/measured notes | Duplum rhythmically active against tenor | Modal, measurable rhythm; phrases/ordines bounded by rests | Propulsive two-part rhythmic counterpoint |
| **Organum triplum/quadruplum / cum alio** | Very slow chant foundation | Two or three upper voices moving at broadly comparable rates | Upper voices coordinated through modal rhythmic organization | Layered upper-voice polyphony, not parallel interval duplication |

Organum purum’s free rhythm remains disputed in detail, but modern performers and editors broadly distinguish it from the more securely measurable discant passages. Turner warns that forcing sustained-tenor passages into a fixed “lilting” modal rhythm destroys their flexibility. [Turner](https://www.musicandpractice.org/volume-1/notre-dame-organum-duplum-modern-edition/); [Yudkin, “The Rhythm of Organum Purum”](https://doi.org/10.2307/763685)

Pérotin-generation “organum cum alio” adds voices that move at the duplum’s rate rather than at the tenor’s rate; consequently, the added voices are in discant with one another even while all unfold above the slow chant. [Taruskin, “Organum cum alio”](https://www.oxfordwesternmusic.com/view/Volume1/actrade-9780195384819-div1-006005.xml)

**Confidence:** High.

## 3. Tonal center, modality, chromaticism, and tuning

### 3.1 Tonal organization

1. The lowest voice is a pre-existing chant, not a generated functional bass.
2. Pitch organization derives from medieval chant modality, recurrent melodic outlines, finals, reciting/focal pitches, and the locally sustained tenor pitch.
3. The current tenor pitch creates a temporary **dronal center**, while the work’s chant mode supplies the larger pitch hierarchy.
4. A modern “key + scale” is only an approximation: mode cannot be inferred reliably from the final alone, and individual chants may change local orientation.

Yampolsky’s corpus analysis argues that organum-purum melodic material is primarily mode-dependent, while also finding a strong local dronal hearing and one analytical dimension better explained by consonance. [Yampolsky, *Melodic Mode in Notre Dame Organum Duplum*](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/482281/)

### 3.2 Operational rule

- Require a user-selected chant final/mode approximation.
- Keep the pitch collection diatonic by default.
- Treat the held input as the **local tenor reference**, not automatically as a new tonic on every note.
- Do not introduce functional dominant–tonic progressions, major/minor chord syntax, blues chromaticism, or automatic modal interchange.
- Musica ficta and B-flat/B-natural choices are source- and context-dependent; they should not be randomized by a preset.

### 3.3 Tuning

The notation does not prescribe a single modern tuning standard. Pythagorean-leaning intonation is historically plausible for perfect intervals, but exact pitch, temperament, ensemble composition, and ficta remain performance decisions. The preset should prioritize pure-sounding unisons, fifths, fourths, and octaves if microtuning ever becomes available, but must not advertise an exact historical temperament.

**Confidence:**  
- Modal/dronal organization: High.  
- Exact ficta per generic preset: Low.  
- Pythagorean tuning as one defensible realization: Medium.

**Competing interpretation:** Mode-centered and consonance-centered analyses are not mutually exclusive; modal melodic design governs the line while consonance governs important vertical meetings.

## 4. Interval and sonority vocabulary

### 4.1 Organum purum

- Structural arrivals favor **unison, octave, fifth, and contextually fourth-related perfect consonances**.
- The duplum may pass through seconds, thirds, sixths, and sevenths during its florid motion; these are events in a line, not a modern chord vocabulary.
- Dissonance should be brief or directed, not held as an unexplained stable sonority.
- Long durations and phrase boundaries should disproportionately coincide with consonant vertical meetings.

Sanders relates rhythmic weight and note length in organum to consonant coordination: consonance can identify structurally important “meetings,” rather than every instantaneous vertical interval being equally stable. [Sanders, “Consonance and Rhythm in the Organum of the 12th and 13th Centuries”](https://doi.org/10.2307/831113)

### 4.2 Discant/clausula

- Consonance is coordinated with measured rhythmic positions.
- Dissonance may arise on intervening events but should lead to a consonant boundary or phrase-ending sonority.
- Repeated tenor patterns permit recurring sonority cycles.
- Avoid interpreting these cycles as chord progressions; clausula composition explores “harmonic sounds in measured time,” not functional harmony. [Bradley, *Creating Clausulae at Notre-Dame-de-Paris*](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/09fe45cf-e487-467e-aeea-575d40f0fb28)

### 4.3 Triplum/quadruplum

- Sonority must be evaluated among **all sounding voices**, not only each voice against the tenor.
- A safe perfect interval from each generated voice to its immediate predecessor can still create a tritone or harsh compound against another voice.
- Upper voices need independent but mutually coordinated contours and rhythms.
- Doubling at unison or octave is permissible, but continuously cloning one contour at fixed intervals erases upper-voice independence.

### Testable tension/resolution rule

At phrase attacks and cadences, require a perfect consonance against the tenor. Within the phrase, permit weaker intervals only on shorter events and require stepwise or small-leap departure toward a subsequent perfect-consonance arrival.

**Confidence:** High for perfect-consonance structural priority; medium for any fixed machine-readable list applying identically across all manuscripts and textures.

## 5. Spacing, inversion, doubling, and register

### Organum purum

- Tenor is the lower foundational voice.
- Duplum is predominantly above it, but voice crossing should not be declared impossible.
- A practical initial spacing is unison, fourth, fifth, or octave, expanding and contracting through the phrase.
- Avoid a permanently fixed octave-plus-fifth “power chord.”
- Avoid placing the florid voice several octaves away throughout; registral contrast should remain singable and relational.

### Discant/clausula

- Voices remain sufficiently close for audible contrapuntal exchange.
- Repeated tenor cells may occupy a compact range while the duplum traces a larger arch.
- Cadential convergence to unison or octave is more characteristic than ending on a modern complete triad.

### Triplum/quadruplum

- The chant remains lowest in the normative texture.
- Upper voices occupy overlapping ranges and may cross.
- Upper voices should not be mechanically assigned “root, fifth, octave” roles.
- Doubling should be episodic; independent melodic parts are the defining behavior.

### Operational register target

For MIDI realization, place the tenor roughly in a low-to-middle male vocal register and keep the first added voice approximately within an octave or twelfth above it. Three-/four-part simulation needs bounded individual ranges and voice-crossing support, not only global octave spreading.

**Confidence:** Medium; exact ranges vary by item and performance transposition.

## 6. Melodic contour, motives, repetition, transformation, ornament

### Organum purum

- Florid upper gestures elaborate one held tenor pitch.
- Prefer conjunct motion, short turns around modal focal tones, neighboring motion, and occasional controlled leaps followed by compensating motion.
- Ligature groupings imply perceptible gesture units even where exact duration is disputed.
- Repetition should be formulaic and varied, not a literal DAW loop.
- Ornament is structural melodic elaboration; it should not become chromatic jazz embellishment.

### Discant/clausula

- Motivic cells may recur because tenor rhythmic units recur.
- Upper-voice material can be designed in sustained, interconnected spans and may contribute substantially to overall clausula form rather than merely reacting note-by-note to the tenor. [Bradley, *Creating Clausulae*](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/09fe45cf-e487-467e-aeea-575d40f0fb28)
- Repetition may preserve rhythm while changing contour or preserve contour while changing its position against the tenor.

### Triplum/quadruplum

- Upper voices should exchange short figures, overlap, and complement one another.
- Identical simultaneous contours are a special event, not a default.
- Density comes from coordinated independent parts, not from chordal attacks on every input note.

**Confidence:** Medium-high.

## 7. Voice motion and layer interaction

### Organum purum

- The tenor’s apparent stillness creates **oblique motion** as the default relation.
- Duplum motion may repeatedly depart from and return to consonance.
- The tenor is not an inert electronic drone: its attacks, duration, releases, and transitions articulate the phrase and should respond to the upper line. Turner specifically criticizes performance that treats the tenor as an unrelated drone beneath ornament. [Turner](https://www.musicandpractice.org/volume-1/notre-dame-organum-duplum-modern-edition/)
- Parallel perfect motion may occur and must not be prohibited by later Renaissance counterpoint rules.

### Discant/clausula

- Both parts are active.
- Contrary, oblique, similar, and parallel motion are all available.
- Rhythmic coordination matters more than enforcing later species-counterpoint prohibitions.
- The tenor’s repeated modal pattern and duplum phrase must share boundaries.

### Triplum/quadruplum

- Upper voices are mutually independent.
- One voice may sustain while another moves; voices may use complementary modal patterns.
- Motion must be checked pairwise and against the total sonority.
- A cascade in which voice 3 is generated only from voice 2 is insufficient: voice 3 must also account for tenor and voice 1.

**Confidence:** High that later “no parallel fifths/octaves” rules are misleading here.

## 8. Rhythm, meter, subdivision, accent, and phase

### 8.1 Organum purum

- Do **not** quantize the duplum into an unbroken rhythmic mode.
- Use breath-shaped, unequal gesture lengths.
- Let consonant arrivals, ligature-like groups, and the imminent tenor change influence duration.
- No swing.
- No modern recurring backbeat.
- Silence separates phrase units.

The precise rhythm is a major scholarly and editorial dispute. Yudkin rejects a blanket modal reading; modern editors range from neutral note values to rhythmically suggestive transcriptions. Turner favors flexible performance informed by the source ligatures. [Yudkin](https://doi.org/10.2307/763685); [Turner](https://www.musicandpractice.org/volume-1/notre-dame-organum-duplum-modern-edition/)

### 8.2 Discant/clausula

- Transport is required for an honest simulation.
- Use a repeating long/short modal pattern within an **ordo**, not an indefinitely running groove.
- Modes I and II—long–short and short–long relationships in modern abstraction—are useful minimal defaults, but the historical system contains six modes and manuscript notation permits qualifications and irregularities.
- Phrase-ending rests reset the pattern.
- Accents follow the long positions and consonant meetings, not a modern 4/4 measure.

The University of Basel’s notation resource describes the six modal patterns as phrase-level organizations recognized through ligature sequences. [University of Basel, “Rhythmic patterns”](https://tales.nmc.unibas.ch/en/from-ink-to-sound-32/coordination-in-rhythm-modal-notation-189/rhythmic-patterns-how-to-recognise-a-mode-957)

### 8.3 Triplum/quadruplum

- Multiple upper voices may share or complement modal patterns.
- Their phrase lengths must align often enough to produce clear structural arrivals.
- The tenor advances on a much slower timescale.
- This is coordinated polyrhythmic layering within modal mensuration, not free asynchronous note spawning.

**Confidence:**  
- Discant’s measured/modal character: High.  
- Exact realization of organum-purum rhythm: Low–medium.  
- One fixed rhythmic mode for all Pérotin works: Low.

## 9. Phrase, cadence, density, silence, and formal pacing

### Phrase scale

Absolute bar counts are a modern overlay. Define phrases by:

- one held tenor pitch or tenor segment;
- an upper-voice gesture/ordo;
- a consonant arrival;
- a rest or chant-tone change.

### Cadence behavior

- Reduce melodic activity near the cadence.
- Approach a perfect consonance by step or controlled contrary/oblique motion.
- Prefer unison or octave as the strongest terminal simplification; fifths can serve as intermediate or less final arrivals.
- Release together or leave a brief clean silence before the next tenor attack.
- Do not append a leading-tone dominant chord.

### Density

- Organum purum: one sustained tenor plus one florid voice.
- Clausula: two active measured voices.
- Triplum/quadruplum: slow tenor plus two/three active upper voices.
- Liturgical plainchant alternates with polyphonic solo sections; continuous maximum-density polyphony is misleading.

**Confidence:** High at texture level; medium for a universal cadence-ranking rule.

## 10. Dynamics and articulation

The manuscripts do not encode modern dynamics, but arrangement behavior should reflect vocal phrasing:

- soft or clear tenor onset, then sustained support;
- connected upper-voice gestures with subtle local emphasis at long notes and consonant arrivals;
- slight taper before a rest or tenor change;
- no piano-like repeated hard attacks on a sustained organal voice;
- no automatic velocity crescendo on every phrase;
- discant may be more incisive than organum purum, but remains vocal rather than percussive.

In a reverberant realization, articulation must preserve separable entries and cadence silence. Cathedral acoustics should not be used as an excuse for indistinct note lifecycle or permanent sustain.

**Confidence:** Medium; these are performance-derived operational recommendations rather than explicit notational instructions.

## 11. Temporal behavior

### 11.1 Within-phrase evolution

#### Organum purum

`Perfect-consonance attack → florid expansion → local consonant plateaus → registral/ornamental peak → contraction → perfect-consonance cadence → breath`

Triggers:

- **Attack:** new long tenor note after silence.
- **Expansion:** tenor held beyond one pulse or threshold duration.
- **Peak:** elapsed hold time, rising input intensity, or accumulated upper gestures.
- **Cadence preparation:** imminent tenor release, reduced player velocity, or phrase-rest detection.
- **Reset:** tenor release plus minimum silence.

#### Discant/clausula

`Measured pickup → stable rhythmic-mode cycle → one or more repeated/varied ordines → cadence lengthening → rest`

Triggers:

- transport running;
- chant melisma or explicitly selected discant section;
- phrase count or player release requests cadence;
- rest resets the modal pattern.

#### Triplum/quadruplum

`Tenor attack → staged upper entries → interlocking density → coordinated upper-voice arrival → tenor advance`

Triggers:

- long tenor hold permits additional upper entry;
- phrase boundary aligns voices;
- density ceiling prevents further entry;
- tenor change clears or re-targets upper parts.

### 11.2 Across-section evolution

A defensible full arrangement would use:

`Plainchant/solo → organum purum → copula-like intensification → discant/clausula → cadence/rest → return or expanded triplum/quadruplum`

The transition is linked historically to chant texture: sustained-tenor organum and measured discant are not random “variation scenes.” Clausulae can replace measured segments, and later repertorial transmission preserves multiple alternatives.

### 11.3 Repertory evolution

- Earlier/Léonin-associated repertory foregrounds two-voice organum duplum alternating sustained-tenor organum with discant.
- Pérotin-associated practice expands and revises the repertory, develops substitute clausulae, and creates large-scale three-/four-part pieces.
- Added voices change the compositional problem: rhythmic coordination and all-voice sonority replace a simple tenor–duplum relation.
- Later clausula reuse leads toward the motet, where added texts and greater layer differentiation produce another distinct genre.

This is an evolution of available practices, not a clean replacement of “primitive parallel organum” by “advanced chords.”

**Confidence:** High for broad development; medium for a strict composer-by-composer chronology.

## 12. Testable stylistic invariants

1. **Chant foundation:** The lowest/foundational voice is a slow modal line, not a functional bass progression.
2. **Section distinction:** Organum purum, discant/clausula, and triplum/quadruplum must not share one undifferentiated algorithm.
3. **Organum-purum obliquity:** During a long tenor hold, the added line produces multiple notes or a shaped gesture rather than one static chord only.
4. **Structural perfect consonance:** Phrase attacks and endings resolve to unison, fourth/fifth-related, or octave sonorities appropriate to the texture.
5. **Directed dissonance:** Non-perfect vertical intervals occur as shorter connective events, not arbitrary sustained endpoints.
6. **Modal containment:** Output remains within the configured chant-mode approximation unless an explicitly defined ficta rule applies.
7. **No functional cadence:** Phrase endings do not generate V–I chord syntax or raised-leading-tone behavior automatically.
8. **Rhythmic separation:** Organum purum remains flexibly timed; discant is transport-locked and modal-patterned.
9. **Breath/reset:** Phrase rests terminate or reset the generated pattern and release all generated notes.
10. **Independent added voices:** In triplum/quadruplum, upper voices are not constant transpositions or simultaneous duplicates.
11. **Tenor responsiveness:** The tenor attack, change, and release govern generated phrase state.
12. **Bounded density:** Maximum texture is one tenor plus the historically selected number of added voices; no Mirror-generated note cloud.
13. **Parallel-perfect permission:** Later prohibitions on parallel fifths/octaves must not be imposed as universal rules.
14. **All-pairs validation:** Three-/four-voice sonorities must be checked globally, not only along a chained generation path.

## 13. Parameters that may vary without losing identity

- chant final and modal family;
- tenor transposition and practical vocal register;
- duplum range, provided it remains relational to the tenor;
- relative frequency of fourths, fifths, unisons, and octaves;
- amount and location of connective dissonance;
- organum-purum gesture length and rubato;
- selected rhythmic mode and tempo for clausulae;
- number of repeated ordines before cadence;
- two, three, or four voices when the corresponding texture algorithm exists;
- degree of voice crossing;
- upper-voice entry staggering;
- phrase length and silence duration;
- performance temperament and modest ficta choices;
- reverberation, timbre, and ensemble size, if not presented as historically exact.

## 14. Misleading behaviors and rejected shortcuts

1. **Continuous parallel fourths:** evokes generic early organum caricature, not Notre-Dame organum purum.
2. **Power-chord generator:** a fixed root–fifth–octave block suppresses florid motion and sectional change.
3. **Chained fourth stacks:** can yield unintended tritones between nonadjacent voices and does not model Pérotin’s independent upper parts.
4. **Renaissance species counterpoint:** automatic rejection of parallel fifths/octaves imports later norms.
5. **Modern triadic harmonization:** thirds-stacked seventh chords are foreign to the selected behavior.
6. **Functional tonal cadence:** dominant–tonic inference misrepresents modal chant organization.
7. **One rhythmic mode everywhere:** especially misleading in organum purum.
8. **Random consonant-below mode:** random thirds through sevenths lacks phrase direction and historical tenor/duplum roles.
9. **Electronic drone plus unrelated noodling:** the tenor must articulate and interact with consonance points.
10. **Mirror octave multiplication:** creates unhistorical automatic doublings and excessive density.
11. **All voices attacking every input:** Pérotin’s added voices require temporal independence.
12. **Permanent four-voice density:** erases alternation with chant, duplum, and rests.
13. **Automatic chromatic borrowing:** modal interchange is not a substitute for source-sensitive ficta.
14. **Claimed exact imitation:** the current reactive engine cannot reconstruct chant-derived phrase form, ligature rhythm, substitute clausulae, or manuscript variation.

## 15. Abstract acceptance examples

Scale degrees refer to a seven-note chant-mode approximation; `T` is tenor, `D` duplum, and beats are modern test coordinates rather than claims about original barring.

### Example A — organum purum: one tenor tone, free upper arc

**Input**

```text
Mode approximation: Dorian
T: degree 1, beat 0.0–8.0
Then silence: beat 8.0–9.5
```

**Acceptable abstract output**

```text
D:
beat 0.0   degree 5   (perfect-consonance attack)
beat 1.2   degree 6
beat 1.8   degree 5
beat 2.7   degree 4
beat 3.1   degree 2
beat 4.3   degree 4
beat 5.0   degree 5   (local plateau)
beat 6.1   degree 7
beat 6.7   degree 6
beat 7.5   degree 1'  (octave cadence)
release by beat 8.0
```

**Assertions**

- tenor remains unchanged for the span;
- duplum rhythm is unequal rather than a repeated long–short loop;
- connective intervals are shorter than structural fifth/octave arrivals;
- all generated notes stop at input release;
- no note occurs during the phrase rest.

### Example B — discant/clausula: measured repeated ordo

**Input**

```text
Transport: running, ternary pulse
T chant segment: 1, 2, 1, 7, 1
Tenor attacks: beats 0, 1.5, 3, 4.5, 6
Phrase rest after beat 7.5
```

**Acceptable abstract output**

```text
D rhythmic mode: long–short, reset at phrase rest

beat 0.0   degree 5   long
beat 1.0   degree 4   short
beat 1.5   degree 6   long
beat 2.5   degree 5   short
beat 3.0   degree 5   long
beat 4.0   degree 3   short
beat 4.5   degree 2   long
beat 5.5   degree 7   short
beat 6.0   degree 3   long
beat 7.0   degree 1'  cadence
```

**Assertions**

- output aligns with transport;
- rhythmic pattern repeats within the ordo;
- terminal event is consonant and lengthened;
- rest resets phase before the next phrase;
- stopping transport releases the lane.

### Example C — abstract triplum, showing why fixed stacks fail

**Input**

```text
T: degree 1 held beats 0–6
```

**Acceptable relational output**

```text
D:  beat 0 degree 5 → beat 2 degree 4 → beat 4 degree 5 → beat 5.5 degree 1'
Tr: beat 1 degree 1' → beat 2.5 degree 7 → beat 3.5 degree 6 → beat 5.5 degree 5
```

**Assertions**

- upper voices enter at different times;
- contours differ;
- both coordinate at beat 5.5;
- output is not `[T, T+4th, previous+4th]`;
- every simultaneous sonority is validated across T–D, T–Tr, and D–Tr.

### 16.2 Explicit interval-stack feasibility

`HarmonyEngine::harmonize()` chains each new harmony from the preceding generated note. In `DiatonicFourths`, three voices can therefore become a **stack of diatonic fourths**, not a declarative set of intervals against the tenor. This cannot guarantee requested tenor-relative structures such as:

```text
[T, T+P5, T+P8]
[T, T+P4, T+P8]
```

Nor can it select a different interval stack at attack, continuation, and cadence. Chaining also does not validate relationships between nonadjacent voices. The preset catalog’s stated requirement—**explicit interval stacks**—is therefore confirmed by code inspection.

Minimum required representation:

```text
intervals relative to anchor/tenor, per temporal state
```

not:

```text
apply the same interval recursively to the previous voice
```

No new general-purpose “medieval engine” is justified until that smaller primitive exists.

### 16.3 Existing modes that should not be substituted

- `StrictCounterpoint`: explicitly prefers later species-style behavior and avoids parallel fifths/octaves; wrong baseline.
- `DiatonicThirds`: stacks thirds into seventh-chord-like structures; wrong.
- `RandomBelowNoSeconds`: may output thirds through sevenths without phrase direction; wrong.
- `FunctionalHarmony` / `BachChorale`: wrong tonal and voice-leading model.
- `OctaveMode::Mirror`: duplicates every harmony at ±octaves and exceeds bounded historical texture.

### 16.4 Companion / PatternLane mapping

The supplied source audit establishes beat-phase plumbing in `HarmonyEngine` but did not include Companion or PatternLane implementations. Therefore only the following capability claims are safe:

- A beat-synchronized PatternLane could plausibly host a repeated long–short or short–long **discant approximation** if it supports:
  - explicit per-step duration;
  - phrase-bounded reset;
  - rests;
  - note-off cleanup;
  - intervals relative to the held tenor;
  - transport stop behavior.
- A single pattern lane cannot by itself produce independent triplum/quadruplum unless lanes have separate rhythmic patterns, bounded registers, synchronized cadence points, and all-voice sonority validation.
- Organum purum should not be forced into PatternLane quantization unless the lane supports unequal/free gesture timing and release-triggered cadences.

**Named code gap:** `NotreDameIntervalStack` or equivalent data-level tenor-relative interval selection.  
**Named temporal gap:** `Purum → Discant → Cadence` phrase/section state.  
**Named lane gap:** phrase-bounded rhythmic-mode ordines with reset.  
**Named polyphonic gap:** independent multi-lane upper voices with global sonority checks.  
**Named modal gap:** medieval chant-mode/ficta behavior beyond a seven-note modern mode.  
**Named articulation gap:** generated melismatic events during one sustained input note.

## 17. Smallest honest preset recommendation

### Operational scope now

Implement only a **Cloister Organum — Duplum approximation**:

- clean monophonic input interpreted as slow chant tenor;
- two voices total;
- input in lowest voice position;
- user-selected diatonic mode;
- no modal interchange;
- long notes and explicit rests;
- added voice constrained to an explicitly selected fourth, fifth, or octave relationship;
- phrase attack/cadence biased to fifth or octave;
- no `StrictCounterpoint`, functional harmony, or Mirror mode.

However, current `HarmonyEngine` cannot generate a florid multi-note duplum from one sustained tenor note. Without a Companion lane or hold-time gesture generator, the actual result remains **punctum-against-punctum open-interval harmonization**, not organum purum.

### Availability decision

**`needs_more_capability`**

The title may be previewed only with explicit approximation copy such as:

> “Open-interval chant harmonization inspired by Notre-Dame organum; does not reproduce its florid duplum or measured clausulae.”

Do not advertise triplum/quadruplum until independent lanes and global sonority validation exist.

## 18. Confidence and competing interpretations

| Claim | Confidence | Qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Organum purum has sustained tenor and florid duplum | High | Core repertorial definition |
| Discant/clausula has both voices measured | High | Rhythm can still vary by source and transcription |
| Pérotin-style added voices move at comparable rates above slow tenor | High | Exact local modal realization varies |
| Purum should not receive one rigid rhythmic mode | Medium-high | Some editors hear modal implications in ligatures/c copula-like passages |
| Structural consonance governs important rhythmic meetings | High | Exact consonance taxonomy and transcription remain debated |
| Medieval modality can be represented by modern Dorian/etc. | Low as equivalence; medium as approximation | Must be disclosed as approximation |
| Pythagorean tuning is the exact required tuning | Low | Plausible option, not uniquely prescribed |
| Every cadence must end at octave/unison | Medium | Strong operational simplification, not a universal corpus law |
| Léonin personally composed all attributed duplum | Low–medium | Anonymous IV is retrospective |
| Pérotin alone authored all associated tripla/quadrupla | Low–medium | Repertorial attribution remains mediated by later testimony |

### Principal scholarly disagreement

The greatest implementation-sensitive disagreement concerns **organum-purum rhythm**. A rigidly modal realization is not defensible as the sole answer; neither is shapeless note-by-note freedom. The safest operational interpretation is flexible, grouped melody whose timing responds to ligature-like gesture, consonance, breath, and tenor articulation.

## Sources

### Kept

- Ernest H. Sanders, [“Consonance and Rhythm in the Organum of the 12th and 13th Centuries”](https://doi.org/10.2307/831113) — foundational account of consonant coordination and rhythmic weight.
- Jeremy Yudkin, [“The Rhythm of Organum Purum”](https://doi.org/10.2307/763685) — central corrective against blanket modal transcription.
- Asher V. Yampolsky, [*Melodic Mode in Notre Dame Organum Duplum*](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/482281/) — corpus-based evidence for modal and dronal organization.
- Catherine A. Bradley, [*Creating Clausulae at Notre-Dame-de-Paris*](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/09fe45cf-e487-467e-aeea-575d40f0fb28) — detailed account of tenor design, recurring upper-voice techniques, and measured clausula composition.
- Penelope Turner, [“Notre Dame Organum Duplum: What Does a Performer Need?”](https://www.musicandpractice.org/volume-1/notre-dame-organum-duplum-modern-edition/) — compares manuscript/editorial rhythm and explains responsive tenor performance.
- Richard Taruskin, [“Organum cum alio”](https://www.oxfordwesternmusic.com/view/Volume1/actrade-9780195384819-div1-006005.xml) — clear distinction between duplum and Pérotin-generation multi-upper-voice practice.
- Catherine A. Bradley, [“Who ‘Made’ the Magnus Liber?”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0261127901001061) — attribution and compilation caution.
- University of Basel, [“Rhythmic patterns—how to recognise a mode”](https://tales.nmc.unibas.ch/en/from-ink-to-sound-32/coordination-in-rhythm-modal-notation-189/rhythmic-patterns-how-to-recognise-a-mode-957) — institutional explanation of six rhythmic modes and phrase-level ligature patterns.
- Rebecca A. Baltzer, [“Notre Dame,” *The Cambridge History of Medieval Music*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-history-of-medieval-music/notre-dame/94C93F0FE9B8B2690D8329693A9FCA83) — authoritative repertorial overview and bibliography.
- Mary E. Wolinski, [“Organum at Notre-Dame in the twelfth and thirteenth centuries: rhetoric in words and music”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0961137106000349) — broader account of tripla/quadrupla elaboration and liturgical-rhetorical context.

### Dropped

- Wikipedia summaries — useful discovery aids but unnecessary where scholarship or primary-treatise translations were available.
- Scribd uploads and personal blogs — unclear provenance or secondary teaching material.
- Modern “cathedral sound” explainers — often conflate acoustics, instrumentation, and later performance traditions.
- Generic early-organum rules predating the Notre-Dame repertory — risk importing ninth- through eleventh-century parallel-organum practice into the selected corpus.

## Gaps

1. Exact interval-frequency statistics by texture and manuscript were not available in the accessible source abstracts; a score-based W1/F/W2 sample should validate numerical weights before implementation.
2. Exact cadence profiles for selected *Viderunt*/*Sederunt* passages require consultation of the modern critical edition or manuscript facsimiles.
3. PatternLane and Companion source files were not among the supplied audit files, so their phrase reset, relative-pitch, autonomous-note, and multi-lane synchronization behavior must be verified before claiming support.
4. Historical tuning, ensemble size, tempo, ficta, and organum-purum rhythm remain legitimate performance variables rather than preset-certifiable facts.
